Conclusion

Working From Grace

We have come to the end of this book, but the real question is what happens when you close it.

Tomorrow will still come with responsibilities.

There will still be work to do, decisions to make, people to love, resources to manage, problems to solve and outcomes to pursue. Some prayers may be answered quickly. Others may require patience. Some plans will work. Others will need to be changed. Some seasons will bring abundance; others may require careful stewardship. There will be moments of confidence and moments when you have to remind yourself again of what you believe.

So the question with which we should finish is the same question that has been underneath this entire book:

❦

Am I working for grace, or am I working from grace?

The difference is profound.

Working for grace says, "If I do enough, believe strongly enough, pray correctly enough, give enough, serve enough and avoid enough mistakes, perhaps God will respond favourably to me."

Working from grace says, "God has revealed His grace to me in Christ. I have received life. I have His Word. I have an assignment. Therefore, I can believe, expect, work, learn and continue."

Both people may appear hardworking.

Both may pray.

Both may study Scripture.

Both may pursue results.

But one is working under the pressure of qualification, while the other is working from the confidence of reception.

That difference changes the psychology of the Christian life.

We Work Because We Have Received Life

One of the central arguments of this book has been that eternal life is not merely a reward waiting at the end of a successful Christian life.

Eternal life is received in Christ.

Jesus says in John 5:24 that the person who hears His word and believes the One who sent Him has eternal life. 1 John 5:11–12 says that God has given us eternal life and that this life is in His Son. In John 17:3, Jesus speaks of eternal life in terms of knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent.

The life has already begun.

We are not trying to perform our way into it.

We are living from it.

That changes everything.

The Christian life is not a long examination in which we spend our earthly years trying to accumulate enough evidence of faithfulness to convince God to bless us and finally reward us with eternal life.

We have received life in Christ.

And because we have received life, we now have a life to live.

❦

We are not trying to earn life through faithful living. We are learning to live faithfully because we have received life.

This is where grace and work finally meet.

Grace Has Never Been the Opposite of Work

Paul gives us perhaps the clearest picture of this in 1 Corinthians 15:10.

He says that by the grace of God he is what he is. Yet in the same verse, he speaks about labouring abundantly.

Paul does not see grace and labour as enemies.

Grace did not make him passive.

Grace made his labour possible.

He could acknowledge his work without making himself the source of everything that work produced.

This is the balance we have been pursuing throughout this book.

We can work hard without believing our work purchases God's favour. We can pursue excellence without using excellence to create identity.

We can seek results without making results the measure of God's love.

We can acknowledge achievement without forgetting grace.

We can recognise mistakes without allowing mistakes to become condemnation.

We can labour greatly while still saying with Paul:

It is by the grace of God that I am what I am.

Therefore:

❦

Grace does not rescue us from work. Grace rescues us from using work as the basis of our righteousness.

And once work no longer has to carry the impossible burden of making us worthy before God, we become free to work for the reasons work was actually given to us.

The Movement of Grace

The argument of this book can be expressed in one movement:

❦
Grace→Faith→Expectation→Work→Fruit→Glory to God

Grace begins with God.

God reveals.

God gives.

God promises.

God supplies.

Faith receives what God has revealed.

Because faith believes, faith expects.

Because faith expects, faith acts.

Because faith acts, there is work.

And faithful work should produce fruit.

But even the fruit is not meant to terminate upon us. Jesus says in Matthew 5:16 that people should see our good works and glorify our Father in heaven.

The movement begins with God and ultimately returns glory to God.

This is why Ephesians 2:8–10 is so important.

We are saved by grace through faith, not by works. Yet Paul immediately says that we are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works.

Grace does not eliminate good works.

Grace establishes their proper place.

We do not perform good works to become God's workmanship.

We perform good works because in Christ we already are.

There Is Something to Do

Our purpose is to glorify God.

Our assignments are the particular good works and responsibilities through which that purpose is expressed in the seasons of our lives.

And our earthly lives provide the time in which those assignments are carried out. That means something very practical:

❦

If there is still life to live, there is still something to do.

Your assignment may presently be to raise your children well.

It may be to search diligently for employment.

It may be to complete your education.

It may be to rebuild a business.

It may be to lead an organisation.

It may be to learn a skill.

It may be to care for someone.

It may be to recover strength.

It may be to serve quietly in a place where few people notice you.

It may simply be to become faithful with responsibilities that presently look ordinary.

Do not despise ordinary assignments.

A good parent is doing good work. A good child is doing good work.

A diligent student is doing good work.

An honest employee is doing good work.

A responsible employer is doing good work.

A faithful neighbour is doing good work.

A person who serves a stranger when help is needed is doing good work.

Our assignments change because our seasons change.

Some seasons carry more responsibility.

Others carry less.

Some are visible.

Others are hidden.

But the central question remains:

❦

What good work belongs to me now?

Grace frees us to answer that question without needing the assignment to prove our importance.

God Remains the Source

Then comes the question of supply.

What happens when the assignment appears larger than the resources presently available?

We return to what we have established:

God is the Source.

Resources are channels and tools.

Money matters, but money is not the Source.

A salary matters, but the salary is not the Source.

A business matters, but the business is not the Source.

Customers matter, but customers are not the Source.

Relationships matter, but people are not the Source.

Doctors and treatment can matter greatly, but they are not the ultimate source of life.

Opportunities matter.

Connections matter.

Savings matter.

Skills matter. But the believer must never psychologically transfer the role of Source from God to the channel.

Because when we do, every threatened channel begins to look like a threatened future.

The loss of a job becomes:

"My provision is finished."

The loss of a customer becomes:

"My business has no future."

The inability of one person to help becomes:

"There is no help."

Grace teaches us to say something different:

❦

"The present channel may have changed. My Source has not."

That does not make us careless.

It makes us better stewards. Because God is the Source, I can manage what is in my hands without fear.

I can budget.

Save.

Learn.

Work.

Ask.

Seek.

Knock.

Develop capacity.

Explore new channels.

Accept help.

Use wisdom.

And continue.

The future is not empty merely because I cannot presently see everything it will require.

People Are Precious, but They Are Not God

Grace also changes the way we relate to people.

We can love deeply without trying to control absolutely.

We can serve without turning service into a transaction.

We can apologise without demanding immediate forgiveness.

We can forgive without pretending trust requires no rebuilding.

We can correct without becoming self-righteous.

We can establish boundaries without abandoning love.

We can raise children faithfully without pretending we can control every decision they will eventually make.

We can lead people without needing their constant approval to maintain our identity.

This is freedom.

People can be precious without becoming ultimate.

And when people are no longer required to carry the weight of being our source, we may actually become freer to love them well.

Grace teaches us:

❦

I am responsible for my obedience, but I am not sovereign over another person's heart.

I can do my part.

I can love.

Speak.

Listen.

Apologise.

Forgive.

Correct.

Serve.

Pray.

And then trust God with what belongs beyond my control.

When the Outcome Has Not Appeared Yet

Perhaps this is the part you need most as you finish this book.

You believed. You expected.

You worked.

But it has not happened yet.

Do not rush to interpret the delay.

Remember the distinctions we have made.

Promise is not process.

Process is not timing.

Timing is not your preferred timetable.

Method is not promise.

A failed strategy does not automatically mean a failed expectation.

A closed channel does not automatically mean there is no supply.

A difficult process does not automatically mean you are travelling in the wrong direction.

And a setback does not automatically mean God has rejected you.

This gives us the freedom to evaluate honestly.

Perhaps the strategy needs to change.

Change it. Perhaps you need more knowledge.

Learn.

Perhaps you need help.

Ask.

Perhaps you need to wait.

Wait responsibly.

Perhaps something needs rebuilding.

Rebuild it.

Perhaps you need to admit that an assumption was wrong.

Admit it.

Grace gives us enough security to learn without losing our identity.

❦

We can remain flexible about process while remaining confident in God's faithfulness.

That is not weak faith.

That is mature faith.

What Is in Your Hands?

Now bring all of this down to today.

Not next year.

Not the next decade.

Today.

What assignment is in front of you?

What have you been postponing because you are afraid you do not have enough?

What did you abandon because one attempt failed?

What responsibility have you avoided because you cannot guarantee the outcome?

What have you refused to begin because you cannot see the entire route?

Ask three questions.

First:

❦

What has God already supplied?

Look again.

What time do you have?

What strength?

What knowledge?

What experience?

What relationships?

What opportunities?

What money?

What tools?

What access?

What ideas?

Do not despise what is presently available simply because it is smaller than what the entire assignment may eventually require.

Second:

❦

What does God's Word give me grounds to believe?

Not what fear says.

Not merely what you wish.

What has God revealed?

Let His Word shape expectation.

And third:

❦

What is the next responsible step?

Perhaps you need to make the call.

Send the application.

Start studying.

Create the budget.

Speak to someone.

Ask for help.

Rest and recover.

Apply again.

Develop the skill. Change the strategy.

Begin the project.

Return to something you abandoned too quickly.

Sometimes faithfulness looks remarkably ordinary.

But ordinary actions, repeated faithfully, can become the work through which extraordinary outcomes eventually emerge.

Grace Gives Urgency Without Panic

There is work to do.

But panic is not required.

You do not need anxiety to become responsible.

You do not need condemnation to become disciplined.

You do not need fear to become productive.

You do not need insecurity to pursue excellence.

Grace provides another foundation.

We can work because the assignment matters.

We can pursue excellence because the quality of our good works matters. We can steward carefully because resources have purpose.

We can persevere because temporary circumstances do not have final authority.

We can rest when necessary because we are not the Source.

We can begin again because a failed attempt has not destroyed our identity.

This is what might be called peaceful diligence.

Peace without passivity.

Diligence without anxiety.

Expectation without control.

Responsibility without condemnation.

Hard work without self-salvation.

A Rhythm for the Rest of Your Life

Throughout this book we have returned to a simple cycle:

❦
Receive→Believe→Expect→Act→Evaluate→Learn→Return to Grace→Act Again

Perhaps this can become a rhythm for the rest of your life.

Receive.

Begin with God.

Recognise what grace has already provided.

Believe.

Trust what God has revealed.

Expect.

Allow His Word to shape your expectation.

Act.

Take responsibility.

Do the work.

Evaluate.

Look honestly at what your actions are producing.

Learn. Do not waste the information contained in experience.

Return to Grace.

If you succeed, grace protects you from pride.

If you experience a setback, grace protects you from condemnation.

And then:

Act again.

Perhaps more wisely.

Perhaps through another method.

Perhaps after rest.

Perhaps through another channel.

But continue.

This is not merely a method for productivity.

It is a way of living from grace.

And Still, Christ

Yet even this cycle is not our foundation.

Christ is. That must remain clear.

Christianity is not ultimately confidence in our ability to practise good principles.

Our hope is not in productivity.

Our hope is not in positive thinking.

Our hope is not in financial planning.

Our hope is not in personal discipline.

Our hope is not even in our ability to remain resilient.

All these things have their proper place.

But none of them is Christ.

The promises of God find their Yes in Him, as 2 Corinthians 1:20 declares.

The cross reveals grace in its deepest expression.

The resurrection declares that apparent defeat did not have the final word.

And the eternal life we have received is in the Son.

Even the message of this book must never become another performance system.

You do not now have to become perfect at living from grace. You may still become afraid.

You may misunderstand.

You may choose a strategy that fails.

You may need correction.

You may mismanage something.

You may discover that what you thought was wisdom was actually assumption.

You may need to repent.

You may need to learn.

You may need to begin again.

And when that happens, where do you return?

Grace.

Not because mistakes do not matter.

But because grace is the foundation from which honest repentance, learning, correction and renewed obedience become possible.

So, Labour

Work.

Not to earn grace.

Work from grace.

Plan.

Not because your plan controls tomorrow, but because planning is part of stewardship.

Expect.

Not because God must submit to your imagination, but because His Word gives faith something upon which to stand.

Use resources.

But do not make them your source.

Love people.

But do not make them responsible for your identity.

Pursue results.

Results matter.

Fruit matters. But do not allow every temporary result to become a verdict on your worth or on God's faithfulness.

Learn from setbacks.

Change methods when wisdom requires it.

Wait when waiting is necessary.

Rest when rest is responsible.

Rebuild when something has been damaged.

And begin again when beginning again is the faithful thing to do.

Perhaps you can now say:

I do not work to earn grace.

I work from grace.

I do not work to prove my worth.

I work because I have received life.

I do not need to become my own source.

God is my Source.

I do not need to control every person or every process.

I am responsible for my obedience. A setback can teach me without defining me.

I can learn.

I can adjust.

I can begin again.

I can expect what God has given me grounds to expect.

I can faithfully use what is in my hands today while trusting Him concerning what will be required tomorrow.

Working From Grace, Not for Grace

Grace did not remove your reason to work.

Grace gave you the right reason to work.

You have received life in Christ.

There is purpose in that life.

There are assignments through which that purpose can be expressed.

There is supply for life and responsibility.

There are people to love.

There are good works to perform. There will be things to learn.

There may be setbacks from which you must rebuild.

There may be seasons when the process takes longer than you expected.

There will be moments when you cannot see the entire route.

But you do not need to see the entire route before taking the next faithful step.

You do not need tomorrow's entire supply before faithfully stewarding today's.

And you do not need perfect circumstances before your life can produce good works that glorify God.

So live the life you have received.

Use what God has placed in your hands.

Do the good work before you.

Expect what His Word gives you reason to expect.

Learn when the result teaches you something.

Adjust when wisdom requires it.

Continue when difficulty tells you to stop but God has not.

Love people without trying to own their responses. Steward resources without making them your source.

And let the quality of your life and work cause your light to shine so that people see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven.

This is the freedom grace gives the Christian worker.

Not freedom from labour.

Freedom in labour.

Freedom to work without making work your saviour.

Freedom to pursue results without making results your identity.

Freedom to expect without trying to control God.

Freedom to face tomorrow without possessing all of tomorrow's resources today.

Freedom to learn without condemnation.

Freedom to begin again.

Freedom to persevere.

And freedom to give God the glory when the fruit finally appears.

We do not spend our lives trying to persuade God to become gracious. Grace has already come to us in Christ.

So receive.

Believe.

Expect.

Work.

Evaluate.

Learn.

Return to grace.

And act again.

We labour, not to reach grace, but because grace has reached us.
