Final Reflection

What Am I Working From?

At the end of this book, one question is worth carrying into every area of life:

❦

What am I working from?

Not simply, What am I doing?

Two people can perform the same action while working from very different foundations. Both may pray. Both may work hard. Both may give. Both may serve. Both may pursue an opportunity. Both may persevere through difficulty.

Yet one may be working from fear while the other is working from faith.

One may be trying to prove worth while the other is responding to grace. One may be desperately attempting to control the future while the other is faithfully stewarding the present and trusting God concerning what remains unseen.

The question, therefore, is not only whether you are working.

It is what your work is flowing from.

When something goes wrong, what is your first interpretation?

Do you immediately assume that God is displeased with you?

When an expected outcome is delayed, do you begin searching for something you must do to make God respond?

When resources become constrained, do you assume that your future has become constrained too?

When someone disappoints you, do you feel that everything you did for that person has become worthless?

When a strategy fails, do you interpret the failed strategy as evidence that you are a failure?

When you cannot see how something will happen, do you assume that it cannot happen?

These questions reveal something about the psychology from which we live.

Performance continually turns life into an examination. Grace turns life into stewardship.

Performance asks:

Have I done enough to qualify?

Grace asks:

What has God provided, and what is my responsibility now?

Performance interprets mistakes as threats to identity.

Grace allows us to acknowledge mistakes, learn and act again.

Performance needs to control outcomes.

Grace allows us to expect while remaining flexible about process.

Performance looks at resources and says:

This is all there is.

Faith looks to God as Source and says:

This is what I have been given to steward today.

The purpose of these questions is not to discover another reason to condemn yourself.

That would reproduce the very psychology this book has challenged.

Instead, recognition gives us an opportunity to return to grace. Perhaps you discover that you have been working from fear.

Return to grace.

Perhaps you have made another person's approval too important.

Return to grace.

Perhaps you have confused a job, salary, customer, opportunity or relationship with your Source.

Return to grace.

Perhaps you have become discouraged because a strategy failed.

Evaluate it. Learn from it. Then return to grace.

Perhaps you have stopped because the outcome has taken longer than expected.

Return to what God has revealed. Examine your assumptions about process and timing. Determine what responsibility remains. Then continue.

Grace gives us somewhere to return.

Not so that we can escape responsibility, but so that we can carry responsibility without condemnation.

The question remains:

❦

What am I working from?

May the answer increasingly become:

Grace.

❦

Grace-Centred Life Assessment

A reflective self-assessment

This assessment is not a test of whether you have "enough faith." It is a tool for recognising where performance thinking, fear or misplaced dependence may still be influencing the way you live.

Rate each statement:

1 — Rarely true of me 2 — Sometimes true of me 3 — Often true of me 4 — Consistently true of me

Grace and Identity

1. I understand that God's grace is the foundation of my Christian life rather than a reward for my performance. ___ 2. When I make a mistake, I can acknowledge it without allowing it to define my entire identity. ___ 3. I can receive correction without immediately interpreting it as rejection. ___

Faith and Expectation

1. I allow God's Word, rather than fear alone, to shape what I expect. ___ 2. I can distinguish what God has revealed from assumptions I have made about timing, process and method. ___ 3. I can continue believing God even when I do not yet understand how an expected outcome will happen. ___

Purpose and Assignment

1. I understand that my ultimate purpose is to glorify God through the life I have received. ___ 2. I can identify important responsibilities belonging to my present season. ___ 3. I recognise that ordinary responsibilities can become good works through which God is glorified. ___

Resources and Supply

1. I recognise God as my Source while responsibly using the channels through which provision comes. ___ 2. When one channel becomes constrained, I do not automatically conclude that all future supply has ended. ___ 3. I faithfully manage what is presently available instead of despising it because it appears small. ___

Relationships

1. I can take responsibility for my behaviour without trying to control another person's response. ___ 2. I can love and serve people without making their approval the source of my identity. ___ 3. I understand that forgiveness, wisdom, accountability and appropriate boundaries can coexist. ___

Setbacks and Resilience

1. I can evaluate a failed strategy without automatically interpreting it as spiritual failure. ___ 2. I am willing to change methods when necessary while remaining faithful to what God has revealed. ___ 3. I can learn from setbacks and begin again. ___

Work and Rest

1. I can work diligently without believing that my work purchases God's favour. ___ 2. I can rest when necessary without believing that everything depends upon my continuous effort. ___

Reflect

Do not use your total score to create another spiritual ranking system.

Instead, look at the individual statements.

Which two or three received your lowest ratings?

Write them here:

1. ______________________________________________ 2. ______________________________________________ 3. ______________________________________________

These may be areas where the psychology of grace needs to become more deeply established in your thinking and daily practice.

Working Through a Setback

A framework for responding without condemnation

A setback does not have to become condemnation.

Use the following framework whenever an important plan, attempt or expectation experiences difficulty:

Remember

What has God actually revealed?

What remains true despite what has happened?

Do not allow disappointment to erase your foundation.

Re-examine

What exactly happened?

Was the problem connected to:

my interpretation? my timetable? my strategy? an assumption? inadequate preparation? insufficient information? something outside my control?

What does the evidence actually show?

Respond

What responsible action is available now?

Do I need to:

learn? ask for help? change strategy? wait? rest? prepare? try again?

Remain

What should remain unchanged? What should I continue believing, doing or expecting?

Rebuild

What has the setback affected?

Do I need to rebuild:

confidence? finances? health? relationships? knowledge? systems? skills? strategy?

What is the first rebuilding step?

❦

A setback can become information without becoming condemnation.

The Grace Cycle

A personal worksheet

Receive→Believe→Expect→Act→Evaluate→Learn→Return to Grace→Act Again

Receive

What has God already provided for the responsibility before me?

Believe

What has God revealed that I should believe?

Expect

What outcome does this give me legitimate grounds to expect?

Act

What responsibility belongs to me now?

Evaluate

What result is my present action producing?

Learn

What is this result teaching me?

Return to Grace

How am I responding to the result?

With pride?

Fear?

Condemnation?

Dependence upon God? What truth about grace do I need to remember?

Act Again

What should I now:

continue? change? improve? stop? begin?

❦

The Grace Cycle is not a formula for controlling outcomes. It is a practical rhythm for living responsibly from grace.

Discussion and Study Questions

Questions for personal or group study

Chapter One

1. What is the difference between working for God's grace and working from God's grace? 2. Where have you seen performance thinking influence your relationship with God? 3. Why can attempting to earn God's favour create fear and anxiety? 4. How does grace change the way a believer interprets mistakes and setbacks? 5. In what area of your life do you most need to move from performance towards grace?

Chapter Two

1. What is the relationship between grace and faith? 2. Why should faith not become another work through which we attempt to qualify for God's blessing? 3. How does Scripture provide evidence upon which faith can rest? 4. What does God's impartiality teach us about His character? 5. How should the truth that God's promises find their Yes in Christ affect Christian expectation?

Chapter Three

1. What is the difference between eternal life as a reward and eternal life as a present possession in Christ? 2. How does receiving eternal life change the way we understand Christian living? 3. Why should grace produce action rather than passivity? 4. How can expectation influence the quality of our work? 5. What changes when you begin working because you have received rather than working in order to receive acceptance?

Chapter Four — Purpose and Assignment

1. What is the believer's fundamental purpose? 2. How are assignments different from purpose? 3. What assignments belong to your present season? 4. How can ordinary responsibilities become good works through which your light shines? 5. What would excellence look like in one assignment you currently carry?

Chapter Five — God as Supplier

1. What resource or channel are you most tempted to treat as your source? 2. Why can confusing a channel with the Source produce anxiety? 3. What has God already placed in your hands that you may have overlooked? 4. What is the difference between confidence in God's supply and careless stewardship? 5. How would your approach to the future change if you truly believed, "The present channel may change; my Source has not"?

Chapter Six — Relationships, Family and Service

1. Where are you tempted to make another person's response the payment for your love or service? 2. What is the difference between influence and control? 3. How can forgiveness and appropriate boundaries coexist? 4. Is there a relationship in which you are carrying responsibility that actually belongs to another person? 5. What would working from grace change about the way you presently love, lead or serve?

Chapter Seven — Delay, Setbacks and Uncertainty

1. What is the difference between a promise, process, timing and method? 2. Is there a setback you have interpreted as condemnation rather than information? 3. Is there a method you need to change without abandoning the outcome you believe God has given you grounds to expect? 4. What can you responsibly do while waiting? 5. What would it mean for you to refuse to give temporary circumstances permanent authority?

Labour Without Fear

1. What does "peaceful diligence" mean to you? 2. How can a believer work intensely without attempting to earn God's favour? 3. What responsibility belongs to you, and what responsibility belongs to God? 4. How has your understanding of success changed through this book? 5. What will working from grace look like practically in your life this week?

Key Declarations From the Book

Reminders of the book's central truths

Read these slowly. They are not formulas for controlling God. They are reminders of the central truths developed throughout this book.

❦

I do not work to earn grace. I work from grace.

I have received life in Christ; therefore I can live and work from that life.

Grace does not remove responsibility. It changes the foundation from which responsibility is carried.

Faith receives what grace provides.

My work does not create my worth before God. My purpose is to glorify God, and my assignments give me opportunities to express that purpose.

God is my Source. Resources are channels and tools.

The present channel may change. My Source has not.

Present supply is not necessarily the full measure of future supply.

I can use what is available while trusting God concerning what is still required.

I am responsible for my obedience, not for controlling another person's heart.

People can be precious without becoming ultimate.

Faithfulness to what God has revealed does not require loyalty to an ineffective method.

A setback can give me information without becoming condemnation. I can remain flexible about process while remaining confident in God's faithfulness.

I do not need tomorrow's entire supply before taking today's faithful step.

Grace gives urgency without panic.

I can learn without losing my identity.

I can adjust. I can rebuild. I can begin again.

I can work diligently without making work my saviour.

And finally:

We labour, not to reach grace, but because grace has reached us.
