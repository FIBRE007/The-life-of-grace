🎧 2. The Fourth Dimension: God’s Kingdom

The Fourth Dimension: God’s Kingdom

Introduction

If Chapter One established that there is an unseen spiritual realm, then an even greater question now demands an answer:

Who governs that realm?

Perhaps, like many people, you have unconsciously imagined the spiritual world as a vast battlefield where God and Satan struggle for control, each trying to gain the upper hand. Popular culture, films, novels and even some Christian teaching have painted the universe as though it were divided into two nearly equal kingdoms locked in an endless contest.

But is that the picture Scripture gives?

Does the Bible really present God and the devil as opposing powers of similar authority?

Does heaven exist under constant threat from the kingdom of darkness? Can Satan challenge God's throne? Can God's purposes be frustrated? Can His government be shaken?

Or have many believers accepted a view of spiritual reality that the Bible itself never teaches?

These are not merely theological questions. They shape how you pray, how you respond to difficulties, how you understand spiritual warfare and, ultimately, how you see God Himself.

If you believe God is continually struggling against an almost equal adversary, your confidence in Him will always be limited by fear. But if Scripture demonstrates that God reigns with absolute authority, that His throne has never been threatened and that His Kingdom has never been shaken, then everything changes.

This chapter is an invitation to see God as He reveals Himself. Not merely as powerful. Not merely as holy.

But as the unrivalled King whose government fills the Fourth Dimension with perfect order, perfect peace and perfect obedience.

It is also an invitation to rediscover Jesus Christ—not merely as the One who was born in Bethlehem, but as the eternal Word who existed before time began, through whom every created thing came into existence and by whom all things continue to exist.

As we journey through this chapter, you will notice a pattern repeated throughout Scripture.

Whenever God's Kingdom confronts the kingdom of darkness, there is never uncertainty about the outcome.

Darkness may resist. It may deceive. It may imitate. It may intimidate. But it never ultimately prevails.

Why? Because God's Kingdom is not one kingdom among many. It is the Kingdom above every kingdom.

Let us begin where every true understanding of reality begins—not with the devil, not with angels, not with humanity, but with God.

The Fourth Dimension Is God's Kingdom

When people hear the phrase the spiritual realm, they often imagine a mysterious universe populated by angels, demons and other spiritual beings.

While these beings certainly exist, Scripture never introduces the unseen realm by drawing our attention to them.

It first draws our attention to God.

The Bible consistently presents the unseen realm, not as a place, but as a Kingdom.

A kingdom is more than a location. It has a king. It has purpose. Without a king there is no kingdom. Without government there is only chaos.

The Fourth Dimension is therefore first and foremost God's Kingdom. David declares,

“The Lord has established His throne in heaven, and His kingdom rules over all.”

Psalm 103:19

Notice how comprehensive this statement is. It does not say God's Kingdom rules over some things. It does not say His authority is limited to heaven. It declares that His Kingdom rules over all. Everything that exists ultimately falls under His sovereign government.

Daniel received a similar revelation.

As he looked into the unseen realm, he did not first see angels.

He did not first see spiritual conflict.

He first saw a throne.

Then he saw "One like the Son of Man" brought before the Ancient of Days.

And concerning Him it was declared,

“Then to Him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve Him. His dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and His kingdom the one which shall not be destroyed.”

Daniel 7:14

Daniel's vision answers one of humanity's deepest questions. Who truly rules history? The answer is not found in earthly governments. Empires rise. Empires fall.

Kings come. Kings die. Presidents are elected. Governments change.

But above every earthly throne stands a throne that has never been vacant.

God's throne.

John was given the same privilege centuries later.

When the Lord invited him into the heavenly realm, what did he first see?

Not warfare. Not confusion. Not competing powers. John writes,

“Immediately I was in the Spirit; and behold, a throne set in heaven, and One sat on the throne.”

Revelation 4:2

The throne is mentioned before anything else because the throne explains everything else.

Everything in heaven revolves around the One seated upon it. Every act of worship. Every angelic assignment. Every judgment. Every purpose.

Every decree. Every created being. Everything proceeds from God's government. The same picture continues into Revelation 5. The entire heavenly host gathers around the throne.

Not one voice questions God's authority. Not one creature competes for His position. Instead they proclaim with one united voice,

“Worthy is the Lamb who was slain to receive power and riches and wisdom, and strength and honour and glory and blessing!”

Revelation 5:12

Heaven is not centred on conflict. It is centred on worship. It is not organised around fear. It is organised around the glory of God.

Perhaps you have unconsciously imagined the Fourth Dimension as a mysterious realm where God and the devil continually struggle for supremacy.

Scripture presents a completely different picture. The Fourth Dimension is not defined by Satan's activity. It is defined by God's reign. Everything begins with Him. Everything exists because of Him.

Everything ultimately answers to Him. That leads us to a deeply personal question. Who governs your life? Every human being lives under a kingdom. Some live according to the values of this present world.

Others willingly submit themselves to the government of God.

Living from the Fourth Dimension begins when you recognise that God's Kingdom is not merely a future destination.

It is the highest reality in existence.

And every day you are invited to live under its government rather than under the changing values of this world.

The question is not whether God is King. He already is. The question is whether you have joyfully submitted yourself to His reign.

Heaven Is Perfectly Governed

If the Fourth Dimension is God's Kingdom, then we must ask another question.

What is life like in God's Kingdom?

Many people think of heaven primarily as a beautiful place.

Scripture certainly describes its splendour.

It speaks of glory beyond human imagination, unending worship and the presence of God.

But one feature of heaven often goes unnoticed.

Heaven is perfectly governed.

Everything functions exactly as God intends. Nothing is out of place. Nothing operates independently. Nothing rebels against God's rule.

The first glimpse Isaiah received of heaven was not its beauty but its order.

He writes,

“I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up, and the train of His robe filled the temple. Above it stood seraphim... And one cried to another and said: 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; The whole earth is full of His glory!'”

Isaiah 6:1–3

Notice what captured Isaiah's attention. The Lord was seated upon the throne. He was not pacing. He was seated. Throughout Scripture, the throne represents settled authority.

A king who is seated is a king who is secure. God is never portrayed as struggling to maintain His position. His reign is settled. His authority is absolute. Around Him stood the seraphim.

Each knew exactly what to do. Each fulfilled the assignment entrusted to him. There was no confusion. No rivalry. No disorder.

Their worship was not forced. It flowed from joyful recognition of God's infinite worth. The same order appears in John's vision. The twenty-four elders cast their crowns before the throne. The living creatures continually declare God's holiness.

Countless angels worship in perfect unity. No one competes for recognition. No one seeks personal glory. Everything points to God. David describes the same reality.

“Bless the Lord, you His angels, who excel in strength, who do His word, heeding the voice of His word. Bless the Lord, all you His hosts, you ministers of His, who do His pleasure.”

Psalm 103:20–21

Notice the sequence. The angels hear God's Word. Then they obey it. Immediately. Joyfully.

Completely. Delayed obedience does not exist in heaven. God speaks. Heaven responds. What a remarkable contrast to life on earth.

Here, God's commands are questioned. His truth is debated. His authority is resisted. His Word is often ignored. But heaven reveals God's original intention for creation.

A Kingdom where His will is gladly embraced. This explains why Jesus taught His disciples to pray,

“Your kingdom come. Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.”

Matthew 6:10

Have you ever considered that Jesus assumed something extraordinary in this prayer? He assumed that God's will is already perfectly accomplished in heaven. He did not teach us to pray, "Lord, establish Your authority in heaven." Nor did He teach us to pray, "Lord, help heaven overcome rebellion." No.

He taught us to pray that earth would increasingly reflect the reality that already exists in heaven.

The problem has never been heaven. The problem is earth. God's Kingdom is already perfect. His government is already complete. His authority is already unquestioned.

The invitation is for His heavenly government to find expression in your life, your family, your work, your church and your community.

This changes the meaning of the Lord's Prayer. When you pray, "Your Kingdom come," you are not merely asking God to take you to heaven one day.

You are asking Him to make your life increasingly reflect heaven today.

You are praying,

"Father, let my thoughts agree with Yours.

Let my decisions reflect Your wisdom.

Let my words honour Your truth.

Let my home display Your peace.

Let my work reflect Your excellence.

Let my life become a place where Your will is obeyed as joyfully as it is obeyed in heaven."

That is the prayer of a citizen of God's Kingdom. Perhaps you have viewed Christianity primarily as preparing to leave earth. Jesus presents something far greater. He calls you to live on earth under the government of heaven. Every act of forgiveness reflects heaven.

Every choice to believe God's Word above your circumstances reflects heaven.

Every decision to love instead of hate, to serve instead of dominate, to speak truth instead of deception, becomes evidence that God's Kingdom is finding expression in your life.

This is what it means to live from the Fourth Dimension.

It is not escaping reality.

It is allowing the perfect government of God's Kingdom to shape the way you live within reality.

Before we can understand how to live under that government, however, we must understand the One through whom that Kingdom has always been established.

Who is Jesus Christ? Did His existence begin in Bethlehem? Or has He always occupied the centre of God's Kingdom? The answer will transform the way you worship Him.

Jesus Before Bethlehem

For many people, the story of Jesus begins in Bethlehem. They picture a manger. Shepherds. Wise men. A virgin mother.

The incarnation.

While these events are true and precious, Scripture teaches that they are not the beginning of Christ's story.

Bethlehem was not the beginning of Jesus.

It was the moment when the eternal Son entered human history as a man.

To understand the Fourth Dimension, you must understand who Jesus truly is.

If Jesus were merely another created spiritual being, then He would simply be one among many heavenly creatures.

But the Bible presents Him altogether differently.

It reveals Him as the eternal Word through whom all things came into existence.

John opens his Gospel, not with Bethlehem, but with eternity.

He writes,

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”

John 1:1

Notice the careful progression. John does not say, "In the beginning the Word came into existence." He says, "In the beginning was the Word." Before time began... the Word already existed. Before creation... the Word already existed.

Before angels... before humanity... before the universe... the Word already existed. Then John makes an even stronger statement.

“The Word was with God, and the Word was God.”

John 1:1

The Word is distinct from the Father. Yet the Word fully shares the divine nature. John is introducing us to One who has always existed. One who possesses everything that belongs to God. He continues,

“All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made.”

John 1:3

Pause and think carefully about that statement. John divides reality into only two categories. There are things that were made. And there is the One through whom they were made.

Everything that belongs to the first category owes its existence to the second.

Nothing that has ever been created came into existence apart from Him. That includes galaxies. Stars. Time. Matter.

Angels. Cherubim. Seraphim. Humanity. Every created thing exists because the Word brought it into being.

John then reveals the astonishing truth,

“And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us...”

John 1:14

Bethlehem was not the beginning of the Word.

It was the beginning of His earthly life as a man.

The eternal Word clothed Himself with humanity without ceasing to be who He had always been.

Micah had prophesied this centuries earlier.

Speaking of the coming Messiah, he wrote,

“Yet out of you shall come forth to Me the One to be Ruler in Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting.”

Micah 5:2

The Messiah's origins are not traced to Bethlehem. They are traced to eternity. His birth revealed Him. It did not create Him. Paul confirms this truth.

Speaking of Christ he writes,

“He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or powers. All things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.”

Colossians 1:15–17

Notice how comprehensive Paul's language is. Visible things. Invisible things. Earthly things. Heavenly things.

Thrones. Dominions. Principalities. Powers. Every order of spiritual being owes its existence to Christ.

Nothing exists independently of Him. Nothing predates Him. Nothing sustains itself apart from Him. The Fourth Dimension itself exists under His lordship.

Created Spirits and the Uncreated Son

At this point, an important distinction must be made. Scripture speaks of many spiritual beings. There are angels. Cherubim. Seraphim.

Archangels. Living creatures. Demons. Even Satan himself. All of them are real.

But they all belong to one category.

Created spirits.

Every angel was created. Even Satan, despite his rebellion, remains a created spirit. None possesses self-existence. None existed before creation. None can exist independently of God.

Every one of them owes its existence to the One who created all things.

Jesus does not belong in that category.

John has already told us why.

“Without Him nothing was made that was made.”

John 1:3

Read those words again.

If everything that was made was made through Christ, then Christ Himself cannot belong to the class of things that were made.

Otherwise He would have had to create Himself, which is impossible.

Paul reinforces the same truth in Colossians.

He says that all things, whether visible or invisible—including principalities and powers—were created through Him and for Him.

That means every angelic being and every spiritual power derives its existence from Christ.

He is not one spirit among many. He is the Creator of every created spirit. The writer of Hebrews goes even further. After describing the greatness of the Son, he declares,

“But when He again brings the firstborn into the world, He says: 'Let all the angels of God worship Him.'”

Hebrews 1:6

Why are the angels commanded to worship the Son? Because the Son is not another angel. He is God. Throughout Scripture, worship belongs to God alone. No faithful angel ever accepts worship for himself.

Yet the Father commands all the angels to worship Christ. The conclusion is unavoidable. Jesus Christ is not the highest created spirit. He is the eternal, uncreated Son of God. This is why Jesus declared,

“Before Abraham was, I AM.”

John 8:58

He did not merely claim to exist before Abraham.

He identified Himself with the divine name revealed to Moses at the burning bush.

The Jews immediately understood His claim. That is why they picked up stones to kill Him. They recognised that He was identifying Himself with the eternal God. John ends Revelation with Christ declaring,

“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last.”

Revelation 22:13

Only God can truthfully speak like this. Only God is self-existent. This truth should move you beyond admiration into worship.

The One who walked the dusty roads of Galilee is the same One before whom angels bow.

The One who hung upon the cross is the One through whom every galaxy came into existence.

The One who rose from the dead is the eternal Word who has always shared the Father's glory.

This is the King of the Fourth Dimension. He is not merely your Saviour. He is your God.

Understanding who Jesus truly is prepares us to confront one of the greatest misconceptions in Christian thinking—the belief that Satan stands as God's opposite or equal. Scripture leaves no room for such an idea.

Chapter Summary

We have entered God's Kingdom and found it unlike anything the visible world prepares us for.

Heaven is not a place of confusion or contest. It is perfectly governed. God's will is not debated there, negotiated there, or resisted there. It is done — instantly, joyfully and completely — and the prayer our Lord taught us asks that the same should become true on earth as it already is in heaven.

We also discovered that the King of this realm did not begin at Bethlehem. The Word was with God and was God before anything created existed. Everything that was made was made through Him. The child in the manger was the eternal Son, stepping into a world He Himself had spoken into being.

This is the government under which every believer now lives.

Yet Scripture is honest about something else, and we must face it. There is opposition. There are powers that resist God's purposes, and their activity is visible in the world we live in. If God's Kingdom is perfectly governed, what are we to make of them?

That is the question the next chapter answers.
