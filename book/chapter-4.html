🎧 4. The Finished Work of Christ

The Finished Work of Christ

Introduction

Few words in human history carry more weight than the final words Jesus spoke from the Cross.

“It is finished.”

John 19:30

These were not the words of defeat.

Neither were they merely the final breath of a dying man.

They were the triumphant declaration of the eternal Son of God, announcing that the greatest work ever undertaken had been brought to completion.

But before we can appreciate what was finished, we must first ask a far more important question.

What exactly was the problem?

If mankind's greatest problem was simply sinful behaviour, why was the incarnation necessary?

Why couldn't God simply forgive? Why did the eternal Word become flesh? Why the Cross? Why the Resurrection? Why did the Son of God endure rejection, suffering and death?

The magnitude of God's solution reveals the magnitude of humanity's problem. Many people believe that sin is humanity's greatest problem. Scripture takes us deeper. Before there was sin, there was unbelief. Before there was disobedience, there was deception.

Before Adam reached for the forbidden fruit, something far more significant had already taken place.

He had accepted another word above God's Word. Notice carefully how the Fall begins. The serpent does not begin by encouraging Eve to eat. He begins with a question.

“Has God indeed said...?”

Genesis 3:1

His first attack was not against behaviour.

It was against God's Word.

When Eve entered into conversation with the serpent, the battle was already underway.

The serpent then contradicted God's Word.

“You will not surely die.”

Genesis 3:4

He questioned God's character.

“For God knows...”

Genesis 3:5

He suggested that God was withholding something good.

He presented an alternative version of reality.

Only after God's Word had been doubted did Eve reach for the fruit.

The outward act merely revealed what had already happened within. The Fall therefore began, not in Adam's hand, but in Adam's heart. It began when another voice became more trustworthy than God's. This pattern is repeated throughout Scripture. The writer to the Hebrews warns believers,

“Beware, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief in departing from the living God.”

Hebrews 3:12

Notice the connection. Unbelief leads to departure from God. It always has. Paul makes an equally searching statement.

“Whatever is not from faith is sin.”

Romans 14:23

That verse is often overlooked. Paul is not merely defining isolated acts. He is revealing the root from which they grow.

If something does not originate from trusting God, it belongs to another source.

James explains the process.

“Each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death.”

James 1:14–15

Sin does not suddenly appear. Something gives birth to it. Genesis reveals what that "something" was. Adam and Eve first accepted another interpretation of reality. Only then did they act.

Even Jesus identified unbelief as the central issue in the Spirit's work. Speaking of the Holy Spirit, He said,

“...He will convict the world of sin... because they do not believe in Me.”

John 16:8–9

That statement is remarkable. Jesus could have listed countless sins. Instead, He points to unbelief. Why? Because unbelief is the fountain from which every other sin flows.

The Fall therefore exposed something far deeper than moral failure. It exposed humanity's desire to live independently of God. That independence is what the Bible later calls the flesh.

This chapter will argue that the flesh is not merely your physical body.

It is humanity attempting to live apart from complete dependence upon God. The flesh does not necessarily appear evil. Sometimes it appears respectable. But its defining characteristic is always the same. It trusts itself more than it trusts God.

It seeks to accomplish, preserve or protect through human ability what can only be received through faith.

That is why the greatest temptation is not always pleasure. Often it is pressure. What happens when God's promise seems delayed? What happens when obedience becomes painful? What happens when circumstances contradict God's Word?

At what point do you begin to think, "Perhaps I need to help God."

Those moments reveal far more about us than seasons of comfort ever could.

They expose whether we are walking by faith or by the flesh.

Understanding this is essential because it explains why Jesus did far more than forgive sins.

He did not simply remove guilt. He addressed the very condition the Fall revealed. He did not improve the flesh. He judged it. He did not restore Adam.

He became the Last Adam. He did not merely reopen Eden. He opened the way to the Father. He did not merely give mankind another opportunity. He inaugurated a completely new creation.

And when He cried,

“It is finished!”

John 19:30

He announced that everything necessary for mankind to be restored to God had been accomplished forever.

The question is no longer whether Christ finished the work.

The question is whether you are living from that finished work or still trying to accomplish through the flesh what God has already accomplished through His Son.

What the Fall Revealed

If unbelief was the root of the Fall, then an important question remains.

What did the Fall actually reveal?

Many people answer, "It revealed sin." That is true. But it revealed something even deeper. It revealed the flesh. The flesh is one of the most misunderstood subjects in Scripture. Many immediately think of the physical body.

Others think only of immoral desires. The Bible presents a much broader picture. The flesh is not merely the body. Nor is it merely the desire to commit obvious sins.

The flesh is humanity attempting to live independently of God.

It is life governed by self rather than by complete dependence upon God.

The flesh says,

"I know what God has said, but I think there is another way."

"I cannot wait any longer." "I must take control." "I must protect myself." "I must solve this myself." The flesh may appear religious. But its defining characteristic is always the same.

It trusts self above God.

This explains why the greatest temptations in your life may not come through pleasure.

More often, they come through pressure. Ask yourself honestly. When do you find it hardest to trust God? Is it when everything is going well? Or is it when the promise seems delayed?

When the pain refuses to leave? When the doctor's report is discouraging? When the finances do not add up? When your prayers appear unanswered? When obedience becomes expensive?

When the future looks uncertain? At what point do you begin to think, "Perhaps I need to help God." That question exposes the flesh. The flesh rarely announces itself through open rebellion. It usually disguises itself as wisdom. As urgency.

As responsibility. As common sense. As "doing what needs to be done." Yet beneath all these appearances lies one question:

Will you continue depending upon God, or will you now depend upon yourself?

Scripture gives us painful examples.

God promised Abraham that he would have a son.

“One who will come from your own body shall be your heir.”

Genesis 15:4

Years passed. Nothing changed. The promise remained. The child did not come. Eventually Sarah proposed another solution.

“Please, go in to my maid; perhaps I shall obtain children by her.”

Genesis 16:2

Notice what happened. Abraham did not reject God. He simply tried to accomplish God's promise by human effort. The flesh always believes God's promises need human assistance. Faith waits.

The flesh produces Hagar. Saul faced a similar test. The Philistines gathered for battle. His soldiers began to scatter. Samuel delayed.

Fear increased.

Instead of waiting as God had commanded, Saul took the sacrifice into his own hands.

“Therefore I felt compelled, and offered a burnt offering.”

1 Samuel 13:12

"I felt compelled." Those words describe the flesh perfectly. Pressure convinced Saul that obedience could wait. Fear replaced dependence. Samuel's response was immediate.

“You have done foolishly. You have not kept the commandment of the Lord your God.”

1 Samuel 13:13

The issue was never merely the sacrifice. The issue was independence. Rebekah made the same mistake. God had already declared,

“The older shall serve the younger.”

Genesis 25:23

God had spoken.

His purpose was certain.

Yet instead of trusting God to fulfil His own word, she devised a plan of deception.

Jacob disguised himself. Isaac was deceived. The blessing was obtained. But at what cost? The flesh always believes deception can accomplish what only faith should receive.

Even Moses, the servant of the Lord, faced this battle. When the people complained again, God instructed him,

“Speak to the rock before their eyes.”

Numbers 20:8

Instead,

“Moses lifted his hand and struck the rock twice with his rod.”

Numbers 20:11

Why?

Because years of frustration had finally exposed something hidden within his own heart.

He no longer acted from simple dependence. He acted from irritation and self-reliance. The outward action revealed the inward source. Peter also loved Jesus deeply. Yet when Jesus began speaking about His coming death, Peter rebuked Him.

Jesus answered with startling words,

“You are not mindful of the things of God, but the things of men.”

Matthew 16:23

Peter's intentions were sincere. His source was wrong. Human reasoning had replaced divine revelation. Good intentions are never a substitute for dependence upon God. Do you see the pattern?

Abraham. Saul. Rebekah. Moses. Peter.

Different circumstances. The same root. Each moment revealed the flesh. Not because they desired evil. But because they struggled to trust God when trusting Him became difficult.

This is why Paul writes,

“For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh... Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.”

Romans 8:5, 7

Notice that Paul speaks first about the mind.

The flesh is first a way of thinking before it becomes a way of living.

It begins when the mind quietly concludes, "I cannot depend upon God here." Paul then reaches a devastating conclusion.

“So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.”

Romans 8:8

Why not? Is God impossible to satisfy? No.

The flesh cannot please God because it refuses the very relationship God created man to enjoy.

God never desired mere outward obedience. He desired trusting sons. The flesh cannot produce that. Neither can it produce righteousness. Isaiah declared,

“All our righteousnesses are like filthy rags.”

Isaiah 64:6

Notice that Isaiah does not speak merely about sin.

He speaks about our righteousness.

Even the best righteousness the flesh can produce falls infinitely short of God's righteousness.

This is why Paul abandoned confidence in himself.

“...not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is through faith in Christ...”

Philippians 3:9

There are therefore two kinds of righteousness. One comes from faith. The other comes from self. One glorifies Christ. The other glorifies man.

One depends upon grace. The other depends upon performance. The flesh cannot produce God's righteousness. It can only manufacture self-righteousness.

And that is precisely why the flesh can never restore mankind to God.

If the Fall revealed the complete inability of the flesh, then one question presses upon us.

How did Jesus Christ accomplish what no law, no sacrifice and no human effort could ever accomplish?

The answer is found in His finished work upon the Cross.

The Finished Work of Christ

Everything we have seen so far leads to one unavoidable conclusion. If the flesh cannot please God... If unbelief is the root of sin... If separation cannot be repaired by human effort... Then humanity does not merely need better instruction.

It needs a Saviour. This is why the Gospel is not God's advice for improving yourself.

It is God's announcement that He has done for humanity what humanity could never do for itself.

Long before Christ came into the world, God had already declared His intention to save.

Immediately after pronouncing judgment upon the serpent, He made an astonishing promise.

“And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel.”

Genesis 3:15

Before Adam and Eve left the Garden, God announced redemption. Grace appeared before they were expelled. Hope was proclaimed before judgment was completed. This tells you something remarkable about God. The Fall did not surprise Him.

Redemption was not an emergency plan. Peter later confirms this truth. Speaking of Christ, he writes,

“He indeed was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you.”

1 Peter 1:20

Paul says the same.

“...He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world...”

Ephesians 1:4

And John describes Jesus as,

“...the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.”

Revelation 13:8

These Scriptures do not suggest that God caused the Fall.

Rather, they reveal that before sin entered history, God had already purposed redemption in Christ.

The Cross was not God's reaction. It was the unveiling of His eternal plan. But why was the Cross necessary? Could God not simply forgive?

He could have. God is not bound by procedure, and forgiveness costs Him nothing He is unwilling to give.

But forgiveness alone would have left you exactly as you were.

Consider what the Fall actually did. It did not merely produce a record of wrong actions that needed erasing. It changed what man was. Adam did not sin and remain the creature he had been; he died spiritually, and every person born of him inherited that death. The problem was not only a debt on the account. The problem was the nature of the one holding the account.

So imagine God had simply pardoned Adam. The guilt is gone. The record is clean.

And Adam is still dead inside.

He would sin again by tomorrow, not because he was insufficiently forgiven, but because nothing in him had changed. A pardoned corpse is still a corpse. Forgiveness addresses the past. It cannot produce a new nature.

This is why the Cross had to accomplish something far greater than acquittal.

Yet God's character had first to be satisfied, and it is here that Scripture is careful.

God is perfectly loving.

“God is love.”

1 John 4:8

God is perfectly holy.

“Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts.”

Isaiah 6:3

God is perfectly just.

“All His ways are justice.”

Deuteronomy 32:4

These truths never compete with one another. If God ignored sin, His justice would be compromised. If He judged humanity without providing salvation, His love would remain unrevealed.

The Cross is where God's justice and God's love meet perfectly.

“...being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God set forth as a propitiation by His blood... to demonstrate His righteousness... that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus.”

Romans 3:24–26

God remains just. Yet He also becomes the justifier. Justice was not ignored; it was satisfied. Love was not diminished; it was demonstrated.

That settles the legal question. But watch what God does next, because this is where most believers stop reading and where the gospel actually begins.

The Cross Made a New Creation Possible

Paul summarises the exchange in one unforgettable sentence.

“For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Notice carefully what Paul does not say.

He does not say Christ merely forgave our sins.

He says Christ became sin for us — and that we might become something. Not be excused. Not be overlooked. Become.

That word is the hinge of the whole gospel.

And Paul does not leave us guessing what we become.

“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

A new creation.

Not a repaired one. Not a forgiven version of the old one. Something that did not exist before.

This is why the Cross could not stop at pardon. God's purpose was never to produce forgiven sinners who remained inwardly unchanged. His purpose was to bring into existence a new kind of humanity, in Christ, sharing His life.

Consider how thoroughly Scripture presses this.

Paul does not say the old man was pardoned. He says the old man was executed.

“knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin.”

Romans 6:6

Crucified. Not reformed, not improved, not placed on probation. God's answer to the old nature was not rehabilitation but death.

“I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.”

Galatians 2:20

And the burial was not the end of it.

“Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

Notice the pattern: death, burial, resurrection. It is the pattern of Christ, and it becomes the pattern of everyone joined to Him. The Cross dealt with what we were. The resurrection produced what we are.

God had promised exactly this centuries before the Cross.

“I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

A new heart. A new spirit. Not a cleaned version of the old.

“I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be My people.”

Jeremiah 31:33

Under the old covenant the law was written on stone and stood outside a man, condemning him. Under the new it is written within him, because the one it is written on has been made new.

This is why Jesus, speaking to a deeply religious man who had kept the law from childhood, did not offer him improvement.

“Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”

John 3:3

Not born better. Born again. A second origin. A new beginning of life itself.

And Paul uses the language of creation deliberately.

“For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Created. The same word God's people used of the heavens and the earth. Something brought into being that was not there before.

“and that you put on the new man which was created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness.”

Ephesians 4:24

Titus states the mechanism plainly:

“not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

Titus 3:5

Regeneration. Not reformation.

Here, then, is what the Cross actually achieved, and it is far larger than most believers have been taught.

Forgiveness cancels a debt. New creation produces a person capable of never incurring it in the same way again.

Forgiveness deals with what you have done. New creation deals with what you are.

Forgiveness would have sent you back to Eden with a clean record and the same nature that failed there. New creation gives you a nature Adam never possessed — the very life of Christ within you.

This is why God did not simply forgive.

Forgiveness alone would have been mercy. This is mercy and re-creation, and it is why Paul could not speak of the Christian life as an improved version of the old one. There is no continuity to improve. Something died and something new was raised.

This is the Great Exchange. Christ took what was ours. He gave us what was His. But His work went even further. Remember the problem established earlier in this chapter.

The issue was never merely sinful actions. The issue was separation from God. Therefore the solution could never stop at forgiveness. Relationship had to be restored. Paul declares,

“Now all things are of God, who has reconciled us to Himself through Jesus Christ.”

2 Corinthians 5:18

Notice the direction. God did not merely reconcile Himself to us. He reconciled us to Himself. The barrier stood on humanity's side. Christ removed it completely.

Paul tells the Colossians,

“And you, who once were alienated and enemies in your mind by wicked works, yet now He has reconciled.”

Colossians 1:21

Alienated. Enemies. Separated. Now reconciled. The entire movement of redemption is towards restored fellowship.

The writer of Hebrews explains why repeated animal sacrifices could never accomplish this.

“For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins.”

Hebrews 10:4

Those sacrifices pointed forward. Christ fulfilled them. Then comes one of the greatest declarations in Scripture.

“But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down at the right hand of God.”

Hebrews 10:12

Why did He sit down? Because His work was complete. The priests of the Old Covenant never sat down. Their work was never finished. Sacrifice followed sacrifice.

Year after year. Generation after generation. But Christ offered one sacrifice. Forever. Nothing remained to be added.

This is why, hanging upon the Cross, Jesus cried,

“It is finished.”

John 19:30

He was not saying His strength was finished.

He was not saying His life was finished.

He was declaring that the work the Father had given Him to accomplish was complete.

Everything necessary for reconciliation had been accomplished. Everything necessary for fellowship had been restored. The debt had been paid. Justice had been satisfied. The old order had reached its appointed end.

The way back to the Father had been opened. No wonder that at the very moment of His death,

“...the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.”

Matthew 27:51

The veil had always declared, "Keep out." "Access denied." "Sin separates." But God Himself tore it from top to bottom. God acted. God opened the way. God invited humanity back into His presence. The Cross therefore did far more than forgive sins.

It restored access to the Father. It accomplished what Eden never could after the Fall. Not a return to a garden. But reconciliation with God Himself. And that raises the next question.

If the barrier has been removed and fellowship has been restored, how does the believer now live in that restored relationship?

The answer is found in the greatest gift Christ gave after His finished work—the Holy Spirit.

Why Every Believer Receives the Holy Spirit

If the Cross removed the barrier between God and man, another question naturally follows.

How is that restored relationship now experienced?

Did Christ merely forgive us and leave us to live for God in our own strength?

Did He reconcile us to the Father and then expect the flesh to accomplish what it had already proven incapable of doing?

The answer is found in one of the greatest gifts of the New Covenant.

The Holy Spirit.

The gift of the Holy Spirit is not an addition to salvation.

It is the necessary consequence of Christ's finished work.

From the beginning of this chapter, we have seen that the real problem was never simply sinful actions.

The problem was that mankind became separated from God and the flesh was revealed as incapable of pleasing Him.

If that diagnosis is correct, then only one solution remains. God Himself must once again dwell within man. That is precisely what Jesus came to accomplish. Long before the Cross, God promised through the prophet Ezekiel,

“Then I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean... I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you... I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes.”

Ezekiel 36:25–27

Notice the emphasis. God did not promise better discipline. He promised His Spirit. He did not merely promise new behaviour. He promised a new heart.

Why?

Because the old heart had already demonstrated its inability to trust Him completely.

The solution was never external improvement. It was inward transformation. Jeremiah declared the same promise.

“I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts...”

Jeremiah 31:33

God's answer was always internal. Not merely a new environment. A new life. Jesus confirmed this when speaking to Nicodemus.

“Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.”

John 3:5

Then He added,

“That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.”

John 3:6

These words explain why the flesh can never produce the life God desires.

The flesh can reproduce only itself. No amount of discipline can transform flesh into spirit. No amount of religion can produce new birth. Only the Holy Spirit gives life. Jesus later made an extraordinary promise.

“He who believes in Me... out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.”

John 7:38

John immediately explains what Jesus meant.

“But this He spoke concerning the Spirit, whom those believing in Him would receive...”

John 7:39

Notice that the promise is made to those who believe. The Holy Spirit is not given because believers become spiritually mature. He is given because Christ has completed His work.

On the night before His crucifixion, Jesus comforted His disciples with another promise.

“And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever.”

John 14:16

Notice the word forever. The Holy Spirit is not a temporary visitor. He is God's abiding presence within His people. Jesus continues,

“...for He dwells with you and will be in you.”

John 14:17

This marks one of the greatest differences between the Old and New Covenants.

Under the Old Covenant, the Spirit came upon individuals for specific assignments. Under the New Covenant, the Spirit dwells within every believer. The relationship Adam lost has now been restored in a greater way. God no longer merely walks with His people. He lives within them.

After His resurrection, Jesus instructed His disciples,

“Behold, I send the Promise of My Father upon you...”

Luke 24:49

That promise was fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost.

“And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit...”

Acts 2:4

Pentecost was not an isolated spiritual event.

It was the public announcement that Christ's finished work had inaugurated the New Covenant.

The Spirit had come because the Son had finished His work.

Paul explains the significance of this beautifully.

“For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.”

Romans 8:14

Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say, "As many as perform impressive miracles..." Nor, "As many as possess remarkable gifts..." He says,

"As many as are led by the Spirit of God."

The distinguishing mark of God's children is not extraordinary ability. It is continual dependence upon the Holy Spirit. Paul continues,

“For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, 'Abba, Father.'”

Romans 8:15

The Holy Spirit does more than empower us.

He restores relationship.

He teaches us to know God, not merely as Creator or Judge, but as Father.

This is exactly what the Fall destroyed. This is exactly what Christ restored. Paul says the same to the Galatians.

“And because you are sons, God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out, 'Abba, Father!'”

Galatians 4:6

Notice the order.

God does not send His Spirit so that you might become His son.

He sends His Spirit because you are His son through Christ. The Spirit is the evidence of restored relationship. He is also the seal of God's completed work. Paul writes,

“...having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise.”

Ephesians 1:13

The seal speaks of ownership. Security. Authenticity.

God places His own Spirit within His people as the guarantee that they belong to Him.

This changes the entire Christian life. You are no longer trying to reach God. God has come to dwell within you. You are no longer attempting to manufacture righteousness through the flesh. The Spirit now produces the life that the flesh never could.

You are no longer striving to earn acceptance. You live from acceptance. The Christian life therefore is not the life of improved flesh. It is the life of Christ expressed through the Holy Spirit.

This is why every believer must learn to walk in step with the Spirit.

For although the flesh has been judged at the Cross, it still seeks to draw the believer back into self-dependence.

How then do the sons of God live each day? Not by striving harder. Not by trusting themselves more.

But by continually walking under the direction and fullness of the Holy Spirit.

The Spirit: God's Answer to the Flesh

If the Cross accomplished everything necessary to reconcile mankind to God, one question still remains.

How can a holy God now dwell with sinful humanity?

Has God simply decided to overlook the flesh? Has He learned to tolerate what once separated man from Himself? The Scriptures answer with an emphatic No. God has not lowered His standard. He has created a new man.

From the beginning of this chapter, one truth has become increasingly clear. The problem was never merely sinful actions. The problem was that the Fall revealed the flesh. The flesh had shown itself incapable of trusting God. Incapable of producing God's righteousness.

Paul therefore reaches a conclusion that every believer must understand.

“So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.”

Romans 8:8

Notice he does not say, "They struggle to please God." He says, "They cannot." Why? Because the flesh is not merely weak. It is fundamentally independent of God. Everything it produces originates from self. Even its righteousness is self-righteousness.

Isaiah declared,

“But we are all like an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are like filthy rags...”

Isaiah 64:6

Notice that Isaiah is not speaking primarily about sin.

He is speaking about our righteousness.

Even the best the flesh can offer falls infinitely short of God's righteousness.

Paul himself discovered this. Before meeting Christ, he possessed an impressive religious record. Circumcised on the eighth day. A Hebrew of Hebrews. A Pharisee.

Blameless according to the righteousness of the Law. Yet after encountering Christ, he wrote,

“...that I may gain Christ and be found in Him, not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is through faith in Christ...”

Philippians 3:8–9

Paul recognised that there are two completely different kinds of righteousness. One comes from self. The other comes from God. One depends upon performance. The other depends upon faith.

One glorifies man. The other glorifies Christ. This explains why the New Covenant required far more than forgiveness. Humanity did not merely need a clean record. Humanity needed a new life.

Centuries before Christ came, God announced exactly what He intended to do. Through Ezekiel He declared,

“I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you... I will put My Spirit within you...”

Ezekiel 36:26–27

Notice carefully what God promised. Not improved flesh. Not stronger determination. Not better discipline. A new heart.

A new spirit. His own Spirit. Jeremiah records the same promise.

“I will put My law in their minds, and write it on their hearts...”

Jeremiah 31:33

God's answer has always been internal. The Law written on stone exposed man's inability. The Spirit dwelling within man makes obedience possible through a transformed life. This is why Jesus told Nicodemus,

“Most assuredly, I say to you, unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”

John 3:3

Nicodemus immediately thought about the flesh.

Jesus spoke about the Spirit.

“That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.”

John 3:6

The flesh can only reproduce itself. It cannot become spiritual through education. Through religion. Through discipline. Or through effort.

Only the Holy Spirit can produce spiritual life.

This explains why the gift of the Holy Spirit is not an optional experience reserved for a few believers.

It is God's answer to the greatest problem revealed by the Fall. The flesh could never restore fellowship with God. Therefore God Himself comes to dwell within those who believe. Paul writes,

“Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you...?”

1 Corinthians 6:19

What Adam lost in the Garden was fellowship with God. What Christ restored is even greater. God no longer merely walks with His people. He dwells within them. This is the fulfilment of God's eternal purpose.

Not merely forgiven people. But a people in whom God Himself lives by His Spirit. The Cross therefore accomplished far more than cancelling sin. It prepared a people in whom the Holy Spirit could permanently dwell. The flesh has been judged.

The old man has been crucified with Christ. The barrier has been removed.

The Father has been reconciled to His children through the finished work of His Son.

Everything is now in place for the life God intended from the beginning.

The question is no longer whether God has done His part.

The question is whether you will live from what Christ has already accomplished.

For everything necessary for fellowship with the Father has now been provided. Nothing remains to be added. The work is finished.

Chapter Summary

Everything we have considered in this chapter leads to one unavoidable conclusion.

The greatest tragedy in human history was not merely that Adam ate the forbidden fruit.

The tragedy began much earlier.

It began when humanity chose to believe another voice above the Word of God.

Unbelief became the root. Sin became the fruit. Separation became the consequence. And the flesh was exposed.

From that moment onward, mankind attempted to accomplish independently what could only be accomplished through continual dependence upon God.

The flesh became the defining characteristic of fallen humanity. Sometimes it expressed itself through obvious rebellion. Sometimes through religious performance. Sometimes through self-righteousness. Sometimes through sincere attempts to "help God." But in every case, the issue remained the same.

The flesh trusted self more than God.

Scripture proved that no amount of moral effort, religious observance or human determination could reverse what the Fall had accomplished.

The problem was never merely behaviour. The problem was nature. That is why God did not simply restore Eden. A perfect environment could never change a fallen heart. The issue was not where man lived.

God's answer was therefore never the improvement of Adam. It was the coming of Christ. Long before Adam left the Garden, God announced redemption. What mankind could never accomplish, God purposed to accomplish Himself. In the fullness of time, the eternal Word became flesh.

Jesus Christ came as the Last Adam, perfectly obeying where the first Adam had failed.

Upon the Cross, He bore the judgment that belonged to us. He satisfied divine justice. He revealed divine love. He reconciled humanity to the Father. When He declared,

“It is finished.”

John 19:30

He announced that everything necessary for mankind's reconciliation had been completed forever. Nothing remained to be added. The old order had reached its end. The new creation had begun. The veil was torn.

Access to the Father was restored.

The Holy Spirit was given, not as an optional blessing, but as God's answer to the inability of the flesh.

God no longer merely calls people back to Himself. Through Christ, He places His own Spirit within them. The Gospel is therefore far greater than the forgiveness of sins.

Forgiveness would have cancelled the debt and left us exactly as we were — pardoned, yet still carrying the nature that failed in Eden. God did not stop there. At the Cross the old man was not merely excused; he was crucified. In the resurrection something was raised that had never existed before.

The Gospel is God's complete answer to everything the Fall destroyed. It is the restoration of relationship. The beginning of a new creation.

The fulfilment of God's eternal purpose to have a people who know Him, live under His government and express His Kingdom upon the earth.

The work has been finished.

The invitation now is simply to believe.

Reflection Questions

  1. Have you understood the Fall primarily as an act of disobedience, or have you recognised that unbelief was the deeper root from which disobedience grew?
  1. In what areas of your life are you still tempted to "help God" rather than trust His Word and His timing?
  1. Have you been relying on your own righteousness, abilities or religious performance instead of resting in the finished work of Christ?
  1. How has this chapter changed your understanding of why Jesus declared, "It is finished"?
  1. Do you truly believe that Christ has accomplished everything necessary for your reconciliation with the Father, or are you still striving to earn what He has already secured by grace?

Prayer

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for revealing through Your Word that my greatest problem was never beyond Your grace. Thank You that when humanity chose unbelief, You did not abandon Your eternal purpose. Before the foundations of the world, You had already prepared redemption through Your Son.

Lord Jesus, thank You for Your finished work. Thank You that You bore my sin, removed my condemnation, reconciled me to the Father and opened the way into His presence. Thank You that when You declared, "It is finished," You completed everything necessary for my salvation and fellowship with God.

Forgive me for every moment I have trusted my own understanding more than Your Word. Forgive me for every attempt to accomplish through the flesh what can only be received by faith. Teach me to rest in Your finished work and to depend completely upon You.

Holy Spirit, thank You for dwelling within me. Let my life continually reflect the reality of the new creation that Christ has accomplished. May I live each day from the grace You have provided, trusting Your Word above every circumstance and bringing glory to the Father.

In the name of Jesus Christ,

Amen.
