Preface

Every generation of believers faces the same question: how can we remain faithful to God in a world that constantly shapes our thinking and urges us to define reality solely by what we can see, hear and feel?

The answer has never changed. God has always called His people to live from a higher reality — one governed not by circumstances, but by His eternal truth.

From the opening pages of Scripture we discover that the visible world was born out of the invisible. Before creation could be seen, it existed in the heart, mind and spoken word of God. Throughout history, the men and women God used most powerfully were those who learned to trust His promises above the evidence of their senses. Above all, Jesus Christ lived every moment in perfect agreement with His Father's will, demonstrating what it truly means to walk by faith and not by sight.

This book invites you into that same way of living.

A word about the title.

I first came across the phrase the fourth dimension many years ago while reading a book of that name by Dr David Yonggi Cho. The expression stayed with me, and I have borrowed it here as a way of naming something Scripture speaks of constantly. The use I make of it in this book is my own, and what follows should be read as my understanding rather than his.

Let me also say plainly what the phrase is not meant to suggest. I am not proposing that there is a first, second or third dimension in any spiritual sense, nor a fifth beyond it. This is not a system of levels or realms to be climbed. Scripture presents two realities — the seen and the unseen — and the Fourth Dimension is simply a memorable name for the unseen one: the spiritual realm that Scripture reveals, and which governs the visible world rather than existing quietly alongside it.

Neither is the phrase offered as a new doctrine, a mystical experience or a spiritual formula. It describes the believer's privilege of seeing, thinking, speaking and living from God's eternal perspective through faith in the finished work of Jesus Christ. It is about allowing the realities of God's Kingdom to become more certain to you than the ever-changing circumstances of this world.

Our generation has unprecedented access to information, yet many believers struggle to experience the peace, confidence and victory that Christ purchased for them. We are overwhelmed by bad news, discouraged by personal difficulty and distracted by the pressures of ordinary life. In such a world, the call to walk by faith and not by sight is more relevant than ever.

The journey of this book unfolds as a single continuous argument.

We begin by establishing that the unseen realm is real — not as a comforting idea, but as a matter of evidence — and that it governs the visible world rather than merely existing alongside it. From there we come to the One who reigns over that realm, discovering that His Kingdom is perfectly ordered, that His will is never contested in heaven, and that Jesus Christ was the eternal Word long before He was the child of Bethlehem. We then face the rebellion honestly — for the powers that oppose God are real and were made powerful by Him, yet the dividing line between the holy and the fallen was never power but worship.

But a holy God and a fallen humanity could not simply be brought together. So we turn to the finished work of Christ, where sin was answered once for all and the way into God's presence was opened. And we discover that this finished work did more than forgive us; it made us new. We received an identity we did not earn and cannot lose.

Yet our identity changed the moment we believed, while our thinking did not. So we come to the renewing of the mind — the patient work by which God brings our thinking into agreement with what is already true of us. From a renewed mind we learn to live by faith, the means by which believers participate in the realities of God's Kingdom, and which always begins with God speaking.

The final chapters bring these truths into ordinary life. We learn to live confidently in the long season between the promise and its fulfilment. We discover that thanksgiving, peace and rest are not moods we manufacture but the atmosphere a trusting heart carries into difficult days. We ask what actually pleases the Father, and find that He is not only pleased but is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him. And we close where the Christian life always leads — outward, as sons who have been sent, shining as lights in a dark world.

My prayer is that this book will become more than something you read. May it become a companion on your journey, a resource you return to often, and a reminder that God's perspective is always higher, His promises are always trustworthy and His grace is always sufficient.

As you begin, approach each chapter with an open Bible, a teachable heart and a willingness to let the Holy Spirit renew your thinking. Do not rush. Pause often to reflect, pray and apply what you learn. Transformation does not come from acquiring knowledge but from responding in faith to the truth God reveals.

May the words of the Apostle Paul become your daily confession:

“Therefore we do not lose heart… while we do not look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporary, but the things which are not seen are eternal.”

2 Corinthians 4:16–18

May you learn to see through Christ's eyes, think with the mind of Christ, speak in agreement with God's Word, and live each day from His eternal perspective.
