The Renewed Mind: Learning to Think Like Christ

Introduction

If Christ has truly made you a new creation, why do you still sometimes think like the old person?

If your sins have been forgiven, why does guilt still speak so loudly?

If you have been accepted by the Father, why do you still struggle with rejection?

If you have been delivered from the kingdom of darkness, why do fear, anxiety and unbelief still influence your decisions?

Has Christ's work failed? Has the Cross accomplished only part of what it promised? Or is there another explanation? These questions deserve honest answers. By now, we have already established two great truths.

Chapter Three proved that Christ completely dealt with sin, the flesh and separation through His finished work.

Chapter Four established that everyone who is in Christ has become a new creation and now possesses an entirely new identity.

If those truths are settled—and Scripture leaves no room to doubt them—then another question naturally follows.

Why is there often such a difference between what God says about believers and what believers actually experience?

Why does a believer who is righteous in Christ sometimes still think like a condemned criminal?

Why does someone who has peace with God still live in constant anxiety?

Why does a child of God sometimes think more like an orphan than a son?

The answer is not found in God's unwillingness. Neither is it found in any deficiency in Christ's finished work. The answer is found in something God intends to transform after salvation.

The mind.

God never intended salvation to stop at forgiving your sins. Neither did He intend it to stop at changing your eternal destination. His purpose is far greater. His purpose is to transform the entire way you think. Not merely what you know.

But how you see. How you interpret life. How you understand yourself. This is why Paul writes,

“And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind...”

Romans 12:2

Notice something remarkable. Paul is writing to believers. Not unbelievers. These are people who have already trusted Christ. Already received the Holy Spirit.

Already become new creations. Yet Paul still commands them to be transformed. Why?

Because although salvation recreates the spirit immediately, the mind must learn to agree with what God has already accomplished.

This is one of the greatest miracles of the Christian life. God does not merely change where you are going. He changes how you think. The renewed mind is therefore not an optional part of Christian growth.

It is the very means by which the realities of Heaven become increasingly visible in the life of the believer.

But before we learn how God renews the mind, we must first answer another question.

Why didn't God simply renew it the moment He saved you?

The Mind: The Last Frontier of Redemption

Why didn't God simply replace your mind the moment He saved you?

If He could make you a new creation instantly, why not also give you an entirely new way of thinking?

Why would an all-powerful God leave the very place where so many battles are fought seemingly unchanged?

If redemption is complete, why does Paul command believers to be transformed?

These are not small questions.

In fact, unless they are answered, many believers quietly conclude that something must still be wrong with them.

Some even begin to question whether they are truly saved.

Others wonder why the Christian life seems so much harder than they expected.

Still others become discouraged because, although they genuinely love Christ, they find themselves thinking thoughts they know are inconsistent with who they have become in Him.

But what if the problem is not that Christ failed to complete His work?

What if the problem is that many believers expect God to do something He never promised to do?

Scripture never says that God instantly replaces the believer's mind. Instead, it consistently speaks of renewing it. There is profound wisdom in that choice of words. Paul writes,

“And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind...”

Romans 12:2

Notice what he does not say. He does not say, "Receive a new mind." Nor does he say, "Wait until God changes the way you think." He says,

"Be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Transformation and renewal are connected. One cannot consistently happen without the other. Why? Because God is not merely interested in changing your destination. He desires to change your perception.

He wants you to see life as He sees it. To value what He values. To reject what He rejects. To judge circumstances according to His Word rather than according to appearances. Paul says something similar to the believers in Ephesus.

“...that you put off, concerning your former conduct, the old man which grows corrupt according to the deceitful lusts, and be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and that you put on the new man which was created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness.”

Ephesians 4:23–24

Notice the sequence. The new man has already been created. That happened the moment you believed.

Yet Paul still commands believers to be renewed in the spirit of their minds.

Why?

Because although God has changed who you are, He now intends to change how you think.

This explains why Paul can also say,

“Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 2:5

Think carefully about that statement.

If believers automatically thought like Christ from the moment they were born again, why would Paul need to give this instruction?

The command itself proves that possessing the life of Christ is not the same as consistently thinking like Christ.

Again, Paul tells the Corinthians,

“...we have the mind of Christ.”

1 Corinthians 2:16

At first glance, these two verses seem almost contradictory. One says, "Let this mind be in you..." The other says, "We have the mind of Christ." How can both be true? Because Scripture is speaking about two different realities. Positionally, every believer possesses the life of Christ. Practically, every believer must continually learn to think in agreement with Christ.

Think of a newborn child. At birth, he possesses everything necessary to become a mature adult. Nothing essential is missing. Yet he must still learn. Relationships.

Judgment. Wisdom. Discernment. The capacity is present. The maturity develops.

So it is with every believer. God has not left you spiritually incomplete. Neither has He left you intellectually abandoned. He has given you His Spirit. He has given you His Word.

He has given you the mind of Christ. Now He calls you to progressively live from those realities.

This is why spiritual maturity is never measured merely by how long someone has been a Christian.

It is measured by how much their thinking has come into agreement with God's thinking.

Have you ever wondered why two believers can hear the same sermon yet respond completely differently?

Why can two Christians read the same promise, pray the same prayer and face the same crisis, yet one walks in peace while the other lives in fear?

Is God showing favouritism?

Certainly not.

More often than not, the difference lies in the condition of the mind.

One has increasingly learned to interpret life through God's Word. The other still interprets life primarily through natural reasoning. This was true throughout Scripture. Consider the twelve spies. God had already promised Israel the land.

That promise was settled. Twelve men entered Canaan. All twelve saw the same cities. The same giants. The same obstacles.

Yet they returned with two completely different conclusions. Ten said,

“We are not able to go up against the people, for they are stronger than we.”

Numbers 13:31

Joshua and Caleb said,

“Let us go up at once and take possession, for we are well able to overcome it.”

Numbers 13:30

What was the difference? Not the evidence. Not the circumstances. Not the promise. The difference was the lens through which they interpreted reality.

Ten saw the promise through the problem. The renewed mind does not deny reality. It simply refuses to let visible reality have the final word. It asks,

"What has God said?"

Then it interprets everything else through that answer. This was also true of Elisha's servant. When the Syrian army surrounded the city, the servant panicked. His conclusion was entirely reasonable. The enemy was real.

The danger was real. The army was visible. Elisha, however, saw something his servant did not. He prayed,

“LORD, I pray, open his eyes that he may see.”

2 Kings 6:17

God did not create another army. The heavenly host was already there. What changed? The servant's perception. His circumstances remained exactly the same.

His understanding changed completely.

How many of our fears persist, not because God is absent, but because our minds are still interpreting reality through natural sight rather than through God's revealed truth?

That question deserves honest reflection. For God is not merely trying to change your circumstances. Very often, He is first changing the way you see them. And that raises another important question.

If God intends to transform our thinking, what does it actually mean to renew the mind?

What Does It Mean to Renew the Mind?

If renewing the mind is so important, why do many believers misunderstand what it actually means?

Is renewing the mind simply reading more chapters of the Bible? Is it memorising hundreds of verses? Is it attending more conferences? Can someone possess extensive biblical knowledge and still have an unrenewed mind? Can a theologian?

Can someone quote Scripture fluently and yet interpret life exactly as an unbeliever would?

These questions may seem uncomfortable, but the New Testament forces us to ask them.

Because nowhere does Scripture equate information with transformation. The Pharisees knew the Scriptures. They memorised large portions of them. They studied them daily. Yet Jesus said to them,

“You search the Scriptures, for in them you think you have eternal life; and these are they which testify of Me. But you are not willing to come to Me that you may have life.”

John 5:39–40

Knowledge alone did not renew their minds.

In fact, their knowledge sometimes reinforced wrong conclusions because it was not received with faith and humility.

This should cause every believer to pause.

Is it possible to know much about God and still fail to think like Him?

The answer, according to Scripture, is yes. That is why Paul does not say, "Be informed." He says,

“Be transformed by the renewing of your mind.”

Romans 12:2

Transformation is God's objective. Renewal is His method. Notice the first word.

"Be transformed."

The Greek word describes a complete change from the inside outward.

It is the same word from which we derive the English word metamorphosis.

A caterpillar does not merely behave differently. Its very nature of expression changes. Likewise, God is not asking believers merely to modify behaviour. He is transforming the source from which behaviour flows. Why?

Because behaviour is always the fruit of belief. You cannot consistently live beyond what you truly believe. That is why God always works first upon the mind. The verse continues,

“…be transformed by the renewing of your mind…”

Romans 12:2

Notice Paul does not say, "By acquiring more information." Nor, "By trying harder." Renewing means replacing. Replacing lies with truth. Replacing fear with faith. Replacing self-reliance with dependence upon God. Replacing natural reasoning with spiritual understanding.

Replacing the conclusions shaped by the Fall with the conclusions revealed by Christ.

Think about how many assumptions most people carry throughout life. Some believe they will never amount to anything. Some believe seeing is believing. Scripture challenges every one of these assumptions.

Renewing the mind therefore means allowing God to examine every belief you hold and asking one simple question:

"Did this belief come from God, or did it come from somewhere else?"

That can be deeply uncomfortable.

Because many of the things we assume to be true have never actually been tested against God's Word.

This is why Paul speaks of being renewed,

“...in the spirit of your mind.”

Ephesians 4:23

He is not referring merely to intellectual ability. He is speaking about the governing disposition of the mind. The inward attitude from which thoughts arise. The lens through which reality is interpreted. Your mind is more than memory.

It includes your imagination. Your expectations. Your reasoning. Your judgments. Your values.

Your worldview. Your assumptions. Your conclusions.

It is the invisible framework through which you interpret everything that happens around you.

That is why two people can experience the same event and arrive at completely different conclusions.

One interprets through fear. Another through faith. One through human wisdom. Another through God's promises. The difference is not intelligence.

It is the condition of the mind. This also explains why Proverbs says,

“As he thinks in his heart, so is he.”

Proverbs 23:7

Notice that Scripture does not merely say, "As he behaves..." It reaches deeper. Behaviour follows thought. Belief shapes identity. Identity influences destiny. No wonder Satan labours to influence what people believe.

And no wonder God places such emphasis upon renewing the mind. Perhaps the greatest misconception about renewing the mind is this: Many believers imagine it is simply learning to think more positively. It is not. Biblical renewal is not positive thinking. It is truthful thinking.

Positive thinking says, "Everything will work out." Biblical thinking says,

"God works all things together for good to those who love Him." (Romans 8:28)

Positive thinking depends upon optimism. Renewed thinking depends upon God's revealed Word. Positive thinking ignores reality.

Renewed thinking acknowledges reality but refuses to let reality speak louder than God.

When David stood before Goliath, he did not deny the giant existed. He simply believed God's covenant was greater than the giant. When Abraham considered his aged body and Sarah's barrenness, Scripture says,

“He did not waver at the promise of God through unbelief, but was strengthened in faith...”

Romans 4:20

Abraham did not ignore the facts. He refused to allow the facts to overrule God's promise. That is the renewed mind. It does not live in denial. It lives in agreement with God.

And this leads to another important question.

If renewing the mind means replacing lies with truth, how does God actually accomplish that transformation in the life of the believer?

That is where we now turn.

How God Renews the Mind

If God commands believers to renew their minds, does He simply leave them to discover how on their own?

Does He expect us to struggle through trial and error? Does transformation happen automatically with the passing of time? Or has God already revealed how He accomplishes this work? The answer is found throughout Scripture.

God has never commanded His people to do something without also providing the means by which it can be accomplished.

The renewing of the mind is no exception.

God Himself is the One who renews the mind, but He does so through means He has ordained.

The believer's responsibility is not to invent those means.

It is to cooperate with them.

Through His Word

Why has God placed such extraordinary emphasis on His Word?

Why did Jesus repeatedly answer Satan by saying,

“It is written...”

Matthew 4:4, 7, 10

Could He not simply have demonstrated His divine authority? Certainly He could have. Yet He deliberately established a pattern for every believer. The battle for the mind is won by truth. Jesus prayed to the Father,

“Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth.”

John 17:17

Notice what sanctifies. Truth. Not emotions. Not experiences. Not opinions.

Truth. The Word of God does far more than provide information. It reshapes perception. It exposes deception. It reveals reality as God sees it.

The writer of Hebrews says,

“For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword... and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.”

Hebrews 4:12

Notice that Scripture reaches beyond behaviour. It examines thoughts. Intentions. Motives. The hidden places of the mind.

Have you ever noticed how often God's Word contradicts what feels natural? The world says, "Protect yourself." Christ says,

“Whoever loses his life for My sake will find it.”

Matthew 16:25

The world says, "Take revenge." Christ says,

“Love your enemies.”

Matthew 5:44

The world says, "Worry because everything depends upon you." God says,

“Cast all your care upon Him, for He cares for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

Every time Scripture contradicts our natural instincts, it is exposing an area where the mind still needs renewal.

That is why God instructed Joshua,

“This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night...”

Joshua 1:8

Notice that God did not merely tell Joshua to read it occasionally. He was to meditate upon it continually. The biblical idea of meditation is not emptying the mind.

It is filling the mind with God's truth until His perspective gradually becomes your own.

David describes the blessed man in exactly the same way.

“His delight is in the law of the LORD, and in His law he meditates day and night.”

Psalm 1:2

Ask yourself honestly.

What occupies your thoughts most consistently?

Your fears? Your finances? Your disappointments? Social media? News headlines?

Other people's opinions? Or God's Word? Whatever consistently occupies the mind eventually shapes it. No one thinks accidentally. Every mind is continually being discipled by something.

The only question is,

Who is discipling yours?

Through the Holy Spirit

If God's Word is sufficient, why do believers also need the Holy Spirit?

Could we not simply read the Bible and understand everything ourselves? Jesus answered that question long before it was asked. Speaking to His disciples, He said,

“But the Helper, the Holy Spirit... He will teach you all things, and bring to your remembrance all things that I said to you.”

John 14:26

Again,

“When He, the Spirit of truth, has come, He will guide you into all truth...”

John 16:13

Notice that Jesus does not merely say the Spirit gives information. He guides. He teaches. He reminds. He illuminates.

Paul explains why this is necessary.

“The natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God... because they are spiritually discerned.”

1 Corinthians 2:14

Left to ourselves, we naturally interpret life through human wisdom.

The Holy Spirit teaches us to interpret life through God's wisdom.

Have you ever read a passage of Scripture that you had read many times before, only for it suddenly to become alive?

The words had not changed. The Holy Spirit had illuminated them. He had renewed your perception. That is one of His great ministries. He does not merely help you remember verses.

He helps you see reality through them. This is why renewing the mind is not merely an intellectual exercise. It is a deeply spiritual work.

The Spirit takes the written Word and writes it increasingly upon the believer's heart.

Without Him, the Bible can become information. With Him, it becomes transformation. But there is another means God repeatedly uses. One that many believers underestimate.

Prayer.

Why would God use prayer to renew the mind?

And if He already knows His will, what exactly changes when we pray?

Those questions deserve careful attention.

Through Prayer

If God already knows everything, why should you pray?

If He already knows your needs before you ask, what purpose does prayer serve?

Does prayer change God? Or does prayer first change the one who prays? Many believers think of prayer almost exclusively as presenting requests to God.

While Scripture certainly encourages us to bring our needs before Him, prayer is much more than making requests.

Prayer is one of God's primary means of aligning our hearts and minds with His own.

The greatest example of this is not found in someone asking for provision or protection.

It is found in Jesus Himself. On the night before the Cross, Jesus entered the Garden of Gethsemane. He knew the suffering that awaited Him. He knew the rejection. The scourging.

The Cross. The wrath that He would bear. In His humanity He prayed,

“Father, if it is Your will, take this cup away from Me; nevertheless not My will, but Yours, be done.”

Luke 22:42

Ask yourself carefully. Did Jesus pray because the Father did not know His will? Certainly not. Did Jesus pray to persuade the Father? No.

He prayed until His human will rested completely in the Father's will. Prayer became the place where surrender was completed. Notice something remarkable. When Jesus entered the garden, His soul was deeply troubled. When He left the garden, the circumstances had not changed.

Judas was still coming. The soldiers were still coming. The Cross still awaited Him. What changed? Jesus did.

Prayer had aligned His heart completely with the Father's purpose.

How often do we enter prayer hoping God will change our circumstances, while God desires first to change our perspective?

Could it be that many of our greatest battles are not won in public but in the secret place of prayer?

Paul writes,

“Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.”

Philippians 4:6

Notice what follows.

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:7

What receives God's peace? Your circumstances? Not immediately. Your heart and your mind. Prayer places the mind where God's peace can govern it. The problem may still exist.

The diagnosis may remain unchanged. The financial pressure may still be present. But something within begins to change.

The renewed mind increasingly interprets the problem through God's presence rather than interpreting God's presence through the problem.

Paul immediately continues,

“Finally, brethren, whatever things are true... noble... just... pure... lovely... of good report... meditate on these things.”

Philippians 4:8

Notice the connection. Prayer. Peace. Thinking. Paul moves naturally from prayer to the discipline of directing the mind.

Prayer and renewed thinking belong together. One without the other leaves the believer unbalanced. Prayer without truth can become emotional. God intends both to work together. Prayer softens the heart.

The Word renews the mind. The Holy Spirit illuminates both. Together they gradually produce a believer who increasingly thinks as Christ thinks.

Through Obedience

Can truth transform someone who refuses to obey it?

Can hearing sermons alone renew the mind?

Can reading books produce spiritual maturity if God's Word never moves beyond the intellect?

These are uncomfortable questions because they expose a common misconception. Many believers assume that knowledge automatically produces transformation. Scripture never teaches that. James writes,

“But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.”

James 1:22

Notice the warning. Who is being deceived? Not the person who has never heard. The person who hears but never obeys. Knowledge without obedience often produces deception rather than maturity.

James continues,

“For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man observing his natural face in a mirror... and immediately forgets what kind of man he was.”

James 1:23–24

The Word functions like a mirror.

It reveals reality.

But what good is a mirror if the one looking into it walks away unchanged?

Obedience is where revealed truth becomes lived truth. Consider Abraham. When God called him to leave his country, did Abraham understand every detail? No. Yet he obeyed.

“By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called...”

Hebrews 11:8

His understanding grew as he walked with God.

Think about Naaman.

When Elisha instructed him to wash seven times in the Jordan River, the command seemed unreasonable.

His first response was anger.

“Indeed, I said to myself...”

2 Kings 5:11

Notice those words. "I said to myself." Naaman already had a way of thinking. God's instruction confronted it. Healing came, not when Naaman argued, but when he obeyed. How often does God ask us to obey before we fully understand?

How many breakthroughs are delayed because we insist that God explain everything before we trust Him?

Peter experienced the same principle.

When Jesus told him to launch into the deep after an unsuccessful night of fishing, the instruction contradicted Peter's professional experience.

Yet Peter replied,

“Nevertheless at Your word I will let down the net.”

Luke 5:5

That single decision revealed a renewed way of thinking. God's Word became more authoritative than personal experience. Every act of obedience reinforces renewed thinking. Every act of disobedience strengthens old patterns.

Renewing the mind therefore involves much more than agreeing intellectually with God's truth.

It involves choosing to live as though God's Word is true, even before circumstances appear to confirm it.

And this raises another searching question.

What has been shaping your thinking every single day without you even realising it?

For no mind remains unchanged.

Every mind is continually being formed by whatever it is exposed to most consistently.

Through Continual Exposure

What has been discipling your mind every single day? Have you ever stopped to ask yourself that question? If someone observed everything you watched... everything you listened to... everything you read... every conversation you entertained... every thought you repeatedly rehearsed... what conclusion would they reach? Who is really shaping the way you think? Is it Christ?

Or is it the culture around you?

Many believers assume that the greatest influence upon their minds is the hour or two they spend in church each week.

But is that really true?

Can one hour of biblical teaching overcome forty or fifty hours of exposure to the world's way of thinking?

Can occasional Bible reading outweigh constant immersion in fear, entertainment, social media, political division, materialism and human philosophy?

Scripture repeatedly teaches that whatever continually occupies the mind eventually shapes it.

David begins the book of Psalms with a remarkable contrast.

“Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly... but his delight is in the law of the LORD, and in His law he meditates day and night.”

Psalm 1:1–2

Notice the contrast. Every person is being influenced. No one thinks independently. The question is never,

"Am I being influenced?"

The real question is,

"By whom?"

David describes two kinds of people.

One allows his thinking to be shaped by the counsel of the world.

The other delights in God's Word until it becomes the governing influence of his life.

The difference is seen in the outcome. One is unstable. The other is described as,

“Like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that brings forth its fruit in its season... and whatever he does shall prosper.”

Psalm 1:3

Why does one life become stable while another becomes unstable?

Because stability begins in the mind long before it becomes visible in behaviour.

Paul gives similar instruction.

“Finally, brethren, whatever things are true... whatever things are noble... whatever things are just... whatever things are pure... whatever things are lovely... whatever things are of good report... meditate on these things.”

Philippians 4:8

Notice that Paul is not merely telling believers what not to think. He is intentionally directing what they should continually think about. Why? Because the mind cannot remain empty. Whatever continually fills it eventually directs it.

This explains why Paul also writes,

“For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit.”

Romans 8:5

Notice the phrase,

"set their minds."

The renewed mind is intentional. It does not drift. It chooses its focus. It chooses its influences. It chooses what it allows to shape its conclusions.

This should cause every believer to pause and ask some uncomfortable questions. What voices occupy my attention most consistently? Whose opinions carry the greatest weight in my decisions? Do news headlines shape my expectations more than God's promises? Do social media algorithms disciple me more consistently than Scripture?

Have I unknowingly allowed fear to become my teacher?

Have I become more familiar with the world's narratives than with God's truth?

These are not insignificant questions. Because every influence is forming you into someone's image. Paul warns,

“Do not be conformed to this world...”

Romans 12:2

The word conformed describes being pressed into a mould. The world has a mould. Its values. Its fears. Its ambitions.

Its definitions of success. Its understanding of identity. Its interpretation of suffering. Its view of truth.

If believers are not intentionally being transformed by God's Word, they will gradually be conformed to the world's patterns without even noticing.

Transformation is intentional. Conformity is automatic. You never drift toward Christlikeness. You drift toward whatever repeatedly captures your attention. This is why Jesus prayed,

“Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth.”

John 17:17

God's truth does not merely inform. It separates. It reshapes. It sanctifies. It teaches believers to see the world differently.

Perhaps this explains why Jesus could sleep during a storm while experienced fishermen panicked.

The same storm confronted everyone.

But not everyone had allowed the same truth to shape their thinking.

Perhaps this explains why Paul and Silas could worship in prison while others would have despaired.

Perhaps this explains why Stephen could pray for those who were stoning him.

Their circumstances were extraordinary. But even more extraordinary was the way they interpreted those circumstances. They had learned to think differently. And that brings us to another crucial question.

What happens when wrong ways of thinking become so deeply rooted that they begin to feel completely normal?

Scripture has a name for that.

It calls them strongholds.

Strongholds: When Lies Become Normal

What if your greatest spiritual battle is not a sinful habit, but a way of thinking?

What if the greatest stronghold in your life is not something you do, but something you believe?

Could it be possible to sincerely believe something that God has never said?

Could a believer pray passionately, worship sincerely, attend church faithfully and still have areas of thinking that remain completely opposed to God's truth?

If that sounds unlikely, consider the disciples.

How many times did Jesus correct not their commitment, but their thinking?

When they argued over who was greatest, Jesus corrected their understanding of greatness (Mark 9:33–37).

When they wanted to call fire down from heaven, Jesus replied,

“You do not know what manner of spirit you are of.”

Luke 9:55

When Peter rebuked Jesus for speaking about the Cross, Jesus answered,

“Get behind Me, Satan!... for you are not mindful of the things of God, but the things of men.”

Matthew 16:23

Notice carefully what Jesus addressed. Peter loved Jesus. Peter had even confessed that Jesus was the Christ.

Yet his thinking had become aligned with human reasoning rather than God's purpose.

Jesus did not merely correct Peter's words. He corrected the mindset behind them. That is exactly what Scripture calls a stronghold. Paul writes,

“For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh. For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds.”

2 Corinthians 10:3–4

Many believers immediately think of demonic activity whenever they hear the word stronghold.

Certainly Satan seeks to deceive. But notice how Paul defines the strongholds he is talking about. He continues,

“Casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ.”

2 Corinthians 10:5

Paul explains the stronghold himself. A stronghold is an argument. A thought. A way of reasoning. A conclusion that has established itself against the knowledge of God.

Strongholds are built one thought at a time.

Eventually those lies become so familiar that they begin to feel like truth.

That is why they are so dangerous. The greatest deception is not believing something that obviously appears false. The greatest deception is believing a lie that feels completely reasonable. Was that not exactly what happened to Eve? The serpent never denied the existence of God.

He simply persuaded her to reinterpret God's Word. The lie sounded believable. The conclusion seemed logical. The deception felt reasonable. Every stronghold begins the same way.

Perhaps you have believed: "God could never really use someone like me." "Nothing will ever change." "My past is too great." "If I cannot understand everything, I cannot trust God." "I have always been this way." "Fear is simply part of my personality." "I can never forgive that person." Where did those conclusions come from? Did they come from God's Word?

Or have they been accepted simply because they have been repeated often enough?

Remember what Jesus said to the religious leaders.

“...you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.”

John 8:32

Notice something profound. Jesus did not say, "Power will make you free." Nor, "Emotion will make you free." Nor, "More religious activity will make you free." He said,

Truth.

Why? Because bondage begins with believing lies. Freedom begins with believing truth. This explains why Jesus immediately added,

“If the Son makes you free, you shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Christ does not merely remove the penalty of sin. He dismantles the lies upon which sin has built its influence. This is why the Word of God is indispensable.

Every time you expose your thinking to Scripture, God shines His light upon another hidden assumption.

Sometimes it is an assumption about Him. Sometimes about yourself. Sometimes about faith. His Word lovingly asks,

"Who told you that?"

That question echoes all the way back to Eden.

After Adam and Eve sinned, God asked Adam,

“Who told you that you were naked?”

Genesis 3:11

God was exposing the source of Adam's new way of thinking. In the same way, the Holy Spirit continually asks believers, "Who told you that I have abandoned you?" "Who told you that your past defines you?" "Who told you that fear is your master?" "Who told you that your circumstances are greater than My promises?"

Every renewed mind learns to question every thought by asking one simple question:

Does this thought agree with God's Word?

If it does not, no matter how familiar it feels... no matter how logical it appears... no matter how widely accepted it is... it must be brought captive to Christ. Notice that Paul does not tell believers to negotiate with wrong thinking.

He says,

“...bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ.”

2 Corinthians 10:5

Not some thoughts. Every thought. Every conclusion. Every fear. Every assumption.

Every interpretation.

The renewed mind does not give authority to thoughts simply because they enter the mind.

It examines them under the light of Scripture. For not every thought deserves agreement. Not every feeling deserves belief. Not every conclusion deserves acceptance. Only God's truth deserves ultimate authority.

And yet, if renewing the mind is so necessary... if God has provided His Word, His Spirit, prayer and obedience...

why does this process often feel so difficult?

Why do old ways of thinking seem to return so easily?

That question deserves an honest answer.

Why Renewing the Mind Is Often Difficult

If renewing the mind is God's will... why does it often feel like such a struggle? Why is it easier to worry than to trust? Why does fear often seem more natural than faith? Why does bitterness feel easier than forgiveness?

Why do we sometimes know what God says, yet still react as though He never said it?

Could it be that the greatest evidence of an unrenewed mind is not ignorance of Scripture, but the instinctive way we interpret life?

These are questions every honest believer must answer.

Because if renewing the mind were automatic, Paul would never have commanded believers to pursue it.

If it were effortless, the New Testament would not repeatedly exhort believers to "put off," "put on," "walk," "stand," "set your mind," and "be transformed."

The difficulty is not that God's Word lacks power.

The difficulty is that the old way of thinking has often been practised for years.

Think about it. Long before you came to Christ, your mind had already been educated. It learned how the world measures success. It learned to trust what it could see. To believe what it could prove.

To depend upon its own understanding. Solomon therefore writes,

“Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding.”

Proverbs 3:5

Why would God command us not to lean on our own understanding?

Because our own understanding has been shaped by a fallen world.

It is entirely possible to be sincerely born again while still instinctively leaning upon ways of thinking that were formed before Christ.

That is why the renewed mind often feels unnatural at first.

When Jesus says,

“Love your enemies…”

Matthew 5:44

everything within the natural mind protests.

When He says,

“Bless those who curse you…”

Luke 6:28

the old way of thinking calls that weakness.

When He teaches,

“It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

Acts 20:35

the world's system calls that foolishness.

When Paul says,

“Rejoice in the Lord always…”

Philippians 4:4

the natural mind asks, "How can I rejoice when nothing around me has changed?" Notice what Scripture repeatedly does. It confronts not merely behaviour, but instinct. The renewed mind begins to call normal what Heaven calls normal.

The unrenewed mind continues to call normal what the fallen world calls normal.

That is why Paul says,

“For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.”

Romans 8:6

Notice that Paul is not describing two different kinds of people. One mind is governed by the flesh. The other is governed by the Spirit. One produces death. The other produces life and peace.

Ask yourself honestly. When unexpected pressure comes, what is your first instinct? Do you immediately remember God's promises? Or do you immediately imagine the worst?

When someone offends you, does your first thought move toward forgiveness or retaliation?

When you receive bad news, does your heart instinctively run toward God's faithfulness or toward fear?

Those first reactions often reveal which patterns of thinking have become most deeply established.

This is why renewing the mind requires perseverance.

The writer of Hebrews exhorts believers,

“Let us run with endurance the race that is set before us, looking unto Jesus...”

Hebrews 12:1–2

Notice the two commands. Run. Keep looking. Renewing the mind is not accomplished by one sermon. Or one Bible study.

Or one conference.

It is the daily, lifelong practice of continually bringing your thinking back under the authority of Christ.

Do not become discouraged if the process takes time. A tree does not become strong in one day. Neither does a disciple. Peter did not think like Christ overnight. The disciples repeatedly misunderstood Jesus.

They argued over greatness. They feared storms. They struggled with unbelief.

Yet Jesus patiently taught them, corrected them and continued shaping the way they thought.

After Pentecost, those same men viewed the world differently. What had changed? Not merely their circumstances.

Their minds had increasingly come into agreement with God's truth through the ministry of the Holy Spirit.

The same transformation is God's desire for every believer. Perhaps this is one of the greatest encouragements in the Christian life. God is not impatient with your growth. He is committed to it. Paul expresses this confidence beautifully:

“Being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.”

Philippians 1:6

The God who recreated your spirit has also committed Himself to renewing your mind.

Your responsibility is not to transform yourself. Your responsibility is to continually yield your thinking to His truth. And over time, something remarkable begins to happen. The truths that once seemed difficult become natural. The commands that once felt impossible become joyful.

The fears that once dominated your thoughts begin to lose their power. The lies that once governed your decisions are replaced by God's promises. People begin to notice something different. Not merely in what you know... but in how you think.

And that raises one final question.

How do you know that your mind is actually being renewed?

Scripture gives unmistakable evidence.

Evidence of a Renewed Mind

How would you know if your mind is actually being renewed? Would it simply be because you know more Bible verses? Would it be because you have attended more conferences? Would it be because you can explain more doctrines? Or does Scripture point to something much deeper?

These questions are important because it is possible to mistake information for transformation.

Knowledge alone does not prove renewal. The Pharisees possessed remarkable biblical knowledge. Yet Jesus repeatedly exposed the condition of their hearts and minds. The renewed mind is not measured first by what it knows.

It is measured by how it thinks, how it responds, and increasingly, how it reflects Christ.

The greatest evidence that your mind is being renewed is not that you can quote more Scripture.

It is that Scripture increasingly governs your interpretation of life.

A Renewed Mind Produces Peace

When pressure comes, where does your mind immediately go? To fear? To panic? To endless "what if" scenarios? Or does it instinctively turn toward God?

Isaiah writes,

“You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You.”

Isaiah 26:3

Notice the connection. Peace follows where the mind rests. The verse does not say the person has no problems. It says his mind is stayed upon God. Paul echoes the same truth.

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:7

A renewed mind does not become peaceful because life becomes easier.

It becomes peaceful because it increasingly trusts the character of God.

A Renewed Mind Produces Stability

Why are some believers easily shaken while others remain steady during enormous trials?

Is one more loved by God? Has one received a stronger Holy Spirit? No. Often the difference lies in where their minds have learned to rest. James writes,

“But let him ask in faith, with no doubting, for he who doubts is like a wave of the sea driven and tossed by the wind.”

James 1:6

The renewed mind is becoming increasingly stable. Not because circumstances never change. But because God's truth has become more constant than changing circumstances. Paul describes mature believers this way.

“...that we should no longer be children, tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine...”

Ephesians 4:14

Notice that maturity is revealed by stability. The renewed mind is not easily manipulated. Not easily deceived. Not easily frightened. It has become anchored in truth.

A Renewed Mind Produces Discernment

How do believers learn to distinguish truth from error? By suspicion? By fear? By constantly studying deception? Scripture points in another direction.

The writer of Hebrews says,

“But solid food belongs to those who are of full age, that is, those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.”

Hebrews 5:14

Discernment grows as the mind is continually trained by God's truth.

The believer increasingly recognises error because he has become familiar with truth.

Just as those who handle genuine currency recognise counterfeit notes by knowing the authentic one, believers recognise deception by continually immersing themselves in God's Word.

A Renewed Mind Produces Humility

One surprising mark of a renewed mind is humility. Why? Because the renewed mind increasingly sees reality from God's perspective. Pride is built upon self-sufficiency. Humility grows from dependence upon God.

Paul writes,

“not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think, but to think soberly, as God has dealt to each one a measure of faith.”

Romans 12:3

Notice that even humility is connected to thinking. A renewed mind no longer measures life by comparison with others. It measures everything by Christ.

The more clearly we see Him, the less interested we become in promoting ourselves.

A Renewed Mind Produces Gratitude

Why do some people continually complain while others continually give thanks? Often because they are interpreting life through different lenses. Paul commands believers,

“In everything give thanks; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:18

Gratitude is not pretending difficulties do not exist. It is recognising that God's goodness remains constant even when circumstances change. A renewed mind increasingly notices God's faithfulness.

A Renewed Mind Produces Love

Jesus said,

“By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.”

John 13:35

Love is not merely an emotion.

It is evidence that the believer increasingly values people as Christ values them.

An unrenewed mind asks, "How does this affect me?" A renewed mind increasingly asks, "How would Christ respond?" This is exactly why Paul says,

“Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 2:5

The renewed mind does not merely admire Christ.

It increasingly adopts His way of thinking.

A Renewed Mind Produces Hope

Finally, the renewed mind becomes increasingly hopeful. Not because life becomes easier. But because God's promises become more convincing than present difficulties. Paul writes,

“For whatever things were written before were written for our learning, that we through the patience and comfort of the Scriptures might have hope.”

Romans 15:4

Hope is born where God's Word continually renews the mind. The renewed believer does not deny suffering. He simply refuses to believe suffering has the final word. As you reflect on your own life, ask yourself honestly. When pressure comes, what naturally rises to the surface?

Fear? Bitterness? Self-reliance? Or peace... gratitude... humility... hope... and confidence in God?

Your first reaction is often one of the clearest indicators of which patterns of thinking have become most deeply established.

This chapter has not been calling you merely to know more. It has been calling you to think differently. To see differently. To interpret reality differently.

To allow God's Word to become the governing authority over every thought, every conclusion and every response.

For the renewed mind is not simply one that has accumulated biblical knowledge.

It is one that increasingly agrees with God.

And when a believer consistently agrees with God, another question naturally emerges.

If my mind has been renewed by God's Word, how do I now step into and experience the realities that God has promised?

That question takes us to one of the greatest themes in all of Scripture.

Faith.

Chapter Summary

Throughout this chapter, one truth has been established repeatedly:

The Christian life is not merely about receiving a new spirit. It is about learning to think from your new identity in Christ.

When you believed the Gospel, God did not begin a process of making you His child.

He made you His child. He did not gradually forgive your sins. He forgave them. He did not slowly justify you. He justified you.

He did not partially reconcile you. He reconciled you completely through Christ.

Yet, although your position before God changed instantly, your patterns of thinking did not.

For years, your mind had been educated by a fallen world. It learned to judge by appearances. To trust the senses above the Word. To measure possibility by human ability. To define identity by performance.

To respond to fear rather than to faith. To seek acceptance through achievement rather than resting in God's grace. God therefore begins another wonderful work. Not recreating your spirit again. But renewing your mind.

He does this through His Word. Through the ministry of the Holy Spirit. Through prayer. Through obedience. Through continual exposure to His truth.

He patiently replaces every lie with truth. Every fear with confidence in Him. Every worldly conclusion with His eternal perspective. This process is neither instant nor accidental. It is intentional.

It requires humility. It requires perseverance. It requires continual surrender.

But it is one of the greatest evidences that God is actively transforming His children into the likeness of His Son.

The renewed mind gradually becomes evident. Peace begins replacing anxiety. Truth overcomes deception. Hope overcomes despair. Love overcomes selfishness.

Humility replaces pride. Stability replaces fear. The believer no longer reacts primarily according to natural instinct. He increasingly responds according to the mind of Christ. This is why Paul commands,

“Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 2:5

The goal of the Christian life is not merely to know more about Christ.

It is to increasingly think as Christ thinks. To value what He values. To love what He loves. To reject what He rejects. To interpret every circumstance through the authority of His Word.

The renewed mind is where Heaven begins to reshape earthly living. Yet one great question still remains. If God's Word has renewed my thinking... How do I actually lay hold of the realities God has promised? How does God's truth move from conviction into experience?

How does what God has already accomplished become evident in my daily life?

The answer Scripture gives is both simple and profound.

Faith.

Not faith as human optimism. Not faith as positive confession. Not faith as wishful thinking. But faith as Heaven defines it.

For the renewed mind prepares the believer to understand one of the greatest spiritual principles in all of Scripture:

The righteous shall live by faith.

That is where we now turn.

Reflection Questions

  1. When unexpected challenges arise, what is your first instinct—fear, self-reliance, anxiety, or confidence in what God has said? What does that reveal about the present condition of your mind?
  1. Which beliefs about God, yourself or your circumstances have you accepted without ever testing them against Scripture?
  1. What voices have been discipling your mind most consistently over the past year? God's Word, the Holy Spirit, or the values and fears of the culture around you?
  1. In which areas has God already begun renewing your mind, and where do you sense He is inviting you to surrender long-held assumptions to the authority of His Word?
  1. If someone observed your thoughts, conversations and responses under pressure for one week, would they conclude that your mind is increasingly governed by the perspective of Christ?

Give me a hunger for Your Word.

Teach me to meditate upon it day and night.

When circumstances seem overwhelming, teach me to interpret them through Your truth rather than interpreting Your truth through my circumstances.

Amen.

Looking Ahead

Chapter 7 – Living by Faith

Your mind has been renewed by the Word. But how do you now live from that renewed understanding? How do God's promises move from truths you believe to realities you experience? Scripture answers with one of its most repeated and most misunderstood themes: faith.
