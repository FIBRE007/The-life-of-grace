The New Creation: Living from Your Identity in Christ

Introduction

In the previous chapter we saw that the finished work of Christ answered the fall completely. The old creation was judged. The new creation began. The Holy Spirit was given. Nothing was left undone.

Yet a question troubles many sincere believers, and it deserves an honest answer. If all of that is already true of me, why does so little of it feel true?

One of the greatest tragedies in the Church today is not that many believers have never been saved.

It is that many who have truly been saved still think like people who have not.

Christ has finished the work. The Father has accepted the sacrifice. The Holy Spirit has been given. The old man has been crucified. The new creation has come into being.

Yet many believers continue to live as though none of these things are true.

Why?

Why do sons still think like slaves?

Why do those who have been declared righteous continue to see themselves as condemned?

Why do those who have been brought near continue to approach God as though they are still far away?

Why do those who have been accepted continue striving for acceptance? The answer is not that Christ's work is incomplete. The answer is that the enemy understands something many believers do not. He cannot undo what Christ has accomplished. He cannot rewrite God's verdict concerning His children.

But if he can persuade believers to live as though these realities are not true, he can rob them of the joy, confidence and effectiveness that should characterise their lives.

The greatest battlefield after the Cross is therefore not Calvary. That battle has already been won. The battlefield is your understanding of who you are in Christ. Throughout Scripture, one truth becomes unmistakably clear. People live according to the identity they believe about themselves.

A prince raised to believe he is a beggar will live like a beggar.

A slave who believes he has no freedom will continue living as though chains still bind him, even after they have been removed.

Likewise, a believer who continues to define himself by Adam rather than by Christ will struggle to enjoy what Christ has already secured.

This chapter therefore asks one simple but life-changing question: Who does God now say you are? Not who your past says you are. Not who your failures say you are. Not who your emotions say you are. Not even who other Christians say you are.

Who does God say you are?

The answer to that question has the power to transform every area of your life.

But before we discover what God says about you, we must first understand something else.

If Christ has truly made you a new creation, why does the enemy continue fighting so fiercely over your identity?

That question lies at the heart of the Christian life.

And Scripture gives a clear answer.

The Miracle of the New Creation

The greatest miracle of salvation is not that your sins have been forgiven.

As wonderful as forgiveness is, it is not the highest achievement of the Cross.

Neither is the greatest miracle that your name has been written in heaven.

Nor is it simply that you have escaped eternal judgment.

The greatest miracle is that God has made you a completely new creation in Christ.

This truth is so profound that if it is not understood, the Christian life becomes little more than an endless attempt to improve the old person God has already brought to an end.

Many believers sincerely believe that salvation means God forgives them, helps them become better people, and gradually turns them into what they ought to be.

Scripture presents something far more radical. God does not improve the old creation. He creates a new one. Paul writes,

“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Notice Paul's language carefully. He does not say, "If anyone is in Christ, he is becoming a new creation." Neither does he say, "He is an improved creation." He says,

"He is a new creation."

That is God's declaration. It is not based upon your feelings. It is not based upon your spiritual maturity. It is based entirely upon your union with Christ. This is one of the greatest differences between religion and the Gospel.

Religion tells you to improve yourself.

The Gospel tells you that in Christ, God has already made you new.

The question is therefore no longer, "How can I become a new person?" The question becomes, "Do I believe what God says I already am?" This truth runs throughout the New Testament. Paul writes,

“Knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with...”

Romans 6:6

Notice that Paul does not say the old man should eventually die. He says the old man was crucified. This happened in Christ. It is part of His finished work. Again he says,

“I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me...”

Galatians 2:20

Paul does not describe Christianity as self-improvement.

He describes it as a complete change of identity.

The person who once stood before God in Adam has now died with Christ.

A new life has begun.

Paul develops this further when writing to the Colossians.

“For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.”

Colossians 3:3

Notice the certainty. Not, "You will die." Not, "You should die." But,

"You died."

The old identity reached its end at the Cross. God now sees you entirely in Christ. This is why the New Testament repeatedly uses the expression,

"in Christ."

You are justified in Christ. Accepted in Christ. Redeemed in Christ. Made alive in Christ. Seated with Christ. Blessed in Christ.

Everything God says about you is now rooted in your union with His Son.

This also explains why Paul writes,

“...even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ... and raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus.”

Ephesians 2:5–6

Notice how completely God identifies the believer with Christ. His death. His resurrection. His life. His victory.

His standing before the Father. Your identity is no longer determined by what happened in Adam.

This is why understanding the difference between the old man, the flesh, and the new creation is so important.

These are not identical. The old man is who you were in Adam before Christ. According to Romans 6:6, that old man was crucified with Christ.

The new creation is who you have now become because you are united with Christ.

According to 2 Corinthians 5:17, you are already that new creation.

The flesh, however, refers to the old way of thinking and living independently of God, which still seeks expression until your mind is brought into agreement with God's truth.

This distinction is crucial.

Many believers wrongly conclude that because they still experience temptation or struggle, they must still be the same old person.

Scripture never says that. The presence of temptation does not determine your identity. God's Word does.

The enemy would love nothing more than for you to define yourself by your struggles instead of by Christ.

Why?

Because people always live according to the identity they believe.

If you believe you are still fundamentally a hopeless sinner, you will struggle to live like a saint.

If you believe you are still rejected, you will never approach God with confidence.

If you believe you are powerless, you will rarely expect victory.

But if you begin to believe what God says about you, your entire outlook begins to change.

You no longer fight for acceptance. You live from acceptance. You no longer pursue righteousness hoping to obtain it. You pursue holiness because you have already been made righteous in Christ. You no longer obey to become God's child.

The Christian life begins, not with what you do for God, but with believing what God has already done for you in Christ.

That is why the new creation is not merely a doctrine to be admired.

It is the foundation upon which the entire Christian life is built.

The next question therefore becomes unavoidable.

If this is truly who you are in Christ, what exactly does God now say is true about you?

What God Now Says About You

One of the greatest questions every believer must answer is this:

Who has the right to define me?

Will it be my past? My failures? My emotions? My family history? My circumstances?

The opinions of others?

Or will it be the God who created me and redeemed me through His Son?

The answer to that question determines how you will live.

When God changes a person's standing before Him, He also changes the way that person must now see himself.

The Christian life therefore begins, not by trying to become someone different, but by believing what God has already declared to be true.

Imagine a prince who was kidnapped as a child and raised as a beggar.

Although he is legally the heir to the throne, he continues to think like a beggar.

He refuses to enter the palace. He feels unworthy of the King's table. He doubts that the King could really be his father. Has his identity changed? No.

His understanding has not caught up with reality. Many believers live exactly like this.

Christ has changed their position before God, but they continue seeing themselves through the eyes of Adam instead of through the finished work of Christ.

The Scriptures repeatedly declare who the believer now is.

The question is whether we will believe God's declaration.

You are now a child of God.

John writes,

“But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name.”

John 1:12

Notice that Scripture does not merely say that God allows you to approach Him.

It says He has made you His child. Children do not earn a place in the family. They are born into it. Likewise, your relationship with God is not built upon your performance. It is built upon the new birth.

Paul confirms this.

“The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God.”

Romans 8:16

God is not merely your Creator.

Through Christ, He is now your Father.

You are accepted.

Many believers secretly live with the fear that God merely tolerates them. They believe He loves them because He has to. Scripture says something entirely different. Paul writes,

“...He made us accepted in the Beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

God does not reluctantly receive you. He joyfully accepts you because you are united with His Son. The acceptance Christ enjoys before the Father has become your acceptance.

You are forgiven.

Forgiveness is not something God occasionally grants depending upon your performance. It is one of the blessings secured through Christ. Paul declares,

“In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins...”

Ephesians 1:7

Notice the words,

"we have."

Not, "We hope to receive." Not, "We may receive." We have it now.

You are justified.

Paul writes,

“Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

Romans 5:1

Justification is more than forgiveness. A forgiven criminal is still guilty. A justified person is declared righteous before the Judge. God no longer relates to you as one under condemnation.

He relates to you as one who has been declared righteous because of Christ.

You are the righteousness of God in Christ.

This may be one of the most difficult truths for many believers to accept.

Yet it is exactly what Scripture declares.

“For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Notice that Paul does not merely say you have received righteousness. He says you have become the righteousness of God in Christ. This is not your own righteousness. It is Christ's righteousness credited to you. Your standing before God is no longer determined by your performance.

It is determined by Christ's finished work.

You have been brought near.

Many Christians still pray as though God is far away.

Yet Paul says,

“But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

Notice the words,

"have been brought near."

Not, "You are trying to get closer." The Cross removed the distance.

You have bold access to the Father.

Because many believers still see themselves as unworthy, they approach God timidly. Scripture teaches the opposite. The writer of Hebrews declares,

“Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace...”

Hebrews 4:16

Why boldly? Not because we trust ourselves. Because we trust Christ. Again Hebrews says,

“...having boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus...”

Hebrews 10:19

The veil has been removed. The way has been opened. You no longer stand outside hoping to be admitted. In Christ, you have already been welcomed.

You are no longer condemned.

One of Satan's favourite weapons is condemnation.

Yet Paul begins Romans 8 with this glorious declaration.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus...”

Romans 8:1

Notice where this freedom is found.

In Christ.

Your failures may accuse you. But God's verdict is final. There is now no condemnation.

You are seated with Christ.

Paul makes another astonishing statement.

“...and raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus.”

Ephesians 2:6

Spiritually speaking, your life is no longer defined by the earth alone. Your citizenship is in heaven. Your authority is in Christ. Your identity is in Him. Everything God now says about you begins with two words:

In Christ.

That is the only place from which your true identity can be understood.

Every other identity is temporary.

Only this one is eternal.

And it is precisely because these truths are so powerful that the enemy works tirelessly to make believers doubt them.

He knows that if he can keep you believing you are still the old person, you will never fully enjoy the life Christ has already secured for you.

That is why the battle for identity continues long after the battle for salvation has been won.

The Devil's Continuing Strategy: Stealing Your Identity

One of the greatest mistakes a believer can make is to assume that because Christ has finished His work, the enemy has stopped his.

He has not.

He has simply changed his strategy.

Before you came to Christ, Satan sought to keep you from believing the Gospel.

After you come to Christ, he seeks to keep you from believing the implications of the Gospel.

He knows he cannot undo your salvation. He cannot remove your name from the Father's heart. He cannot make God reject those whom He has accepted. So what does he do? He attacks your understanding.

He attacks your confidence. He attacks your identity. This has always been his strategy. It has never changed.

In the Garden of Eden, he did not begin by offering Eve the fruit.

He first questioned God's Word.

“Has God indeed said...?”

Genesis 3:1

The attack was against God's revelation. When God's Word became doubtful, disobedience became inevitable. Thousands of years later, we see exactly the same strategy. Immediately after Jesus was baptised, the Father declared,

“This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:17

Notice what happened next. Jesus was led into the wilderness to be tempted. The first recorded words of Satan were,

“If You are the Son of God...”

Matthew 4:3

That statement was not merely a temptation to perform a miracle. It was an attack upon identity. The Father had already spoken. The devil immediately questioned what God had declared. Jesus did not defend Himself with feelings.

He did not argue. He answered every temptation with God's Word.

“It is written...”

Matthew 4:4, 7, 10

The battle was won because Jesus refused to accept any identity other than the one His Father had declared.

The enemy still uses exactly the same strategy today. He no longer asks, "Did God really say there was a tree?" Now he whispers, "Are you really forgiven?" "Has God really accepted you?" "If you were truly God's child, would you still struggle?" "If God really loved you, why would this happen?" "Can someone like you really be righteous?" Notice what all these questions have in common. They are not attacks against your circumstances. Jesus described Satan's nature perfectly.

“He was a murderer from the beginning, and does not stand in the truth... for he is a liar and the father of it.”

John 8:44

The devil's greatest weapon is not power. It is deception. He succeeds only when people believe lies instead of truth. Paul recognised this danger. Writing to the Corinthians, he said,

“But I fear, lest somehow, as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness, so your minds may be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.”

2 Corinthians 11:3

Notice Paul's concern. He does not say Satan will remove believers from Christ. He warns that their minds may become corrupted. The battle is not over Christ's victory. The battle is over whether believers will continue believing Christ's victory.

Peter experienced this personally. Jesus warned him,

“Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail...”

Luke 22:31–32

Notice what Jesus protected. Not Peter's reputation. Not Peter's comfort. His faith. Why?

Because when faith remains, restoration remains possible. Even after Peter failed, his identity as Christ's disciple had not changed. His failure did not redefine him. Christ's calling did. The same is true for every believer.

The devil wants you to interpret your failures as your identity. God calls them interruptions in your growth. The devil says, "You failed; therefore you are a failure." God says, "A righteous man may fall seven times and rise again." (Proverbs 24:16) The devil says, "God is finished with you." God says,

“If anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous.”

1 John 2:1

The devil says, "You are condemned." God says,

“There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.”

Romans 8:1

The devil is called,

“...the accuser of our brethren...”

Revelation 12:10

Notice that he is not called the judge. Only God is Judge. The devil accuses. God justifies. The devil condemns.

The Holy Spirit convicts in order to restore. The devil reminds you of your past to keep you in bondage. The Holy Spirit reminds you of Christ to lead you into freedom. This is why believers must learn to recognise the enemy's voice. Anything that contradicts what God has declared in Christ must be rejected.

Not because it feels wrong. But because it is contrary to the truth.

The greatest victory in the Christian life is not winning an argument with the devil.

It is refusing to believe him.

The battle for your identity will continue for as long as you live.

But the outcome has already been decided. God has spoken. The Cross has settled the matter. The resurrection has confirmed it. The Holy Spirit bears witness to it.

The only question that remains is this:

Whose voice will you believe?

False Identities Many Believers Still Carry

One of the greatest evidences that the battle over identity is real is that many sincere believers continue to speak about themselves in ways that God never does.

Listen carefully to the conversations of Christians. You will often hear statements like these: "I am just a sinner trying to make heaven." "God is probably disappointed with me again." "I don't think I can ever overcome this." "I know God forgives me, but I can never forgive myself." "God uses other people, but not someone like me." "I have to work harder before God can really accept me." "I'm too broken for God to use." These statements sound humble. Some even sound spiritual. Yet humility is not believing less about yourself than God says.

Humility is agreeing with God's verdict.

The question therefore is not, "Does this sound humble?" The question is,

"Is it true?"

The Christian life is built upon truth, not sincerity.

A sincere lie is still a lie.

Let us examine some of the most common false identities believers continue to carry.

"I am just a sinner."

This may be one of the most common descriptions believers use for themselves.

Yet notice how the New Testament describes Christians.

Paul begins many of his letters with words such as,

“To the saints...”

Romans 1:7; 1 Corinthians 1:2; Ephesians 1:1; Philippians 1:1

He was writing to ordinary believers.

Not to a special class of exceptionally holy Christians.

The word saint simply means one who has been set apart for God.

Yes, believers still sin. Yes, believers still need daily cleansing and repentance. But Scripture never teaches that your identity is still "a sinner."

Rather, it teaches that you are a saint who may still stumble while growing in Christ.

Identity must never be confused with behaviour.

A child who falls while learning to walk does not cease to be a child.

Likewise, when a believer sins, he has not ceased to be God's child.

"God merely tolerates me."

Many Christians secretly imagine that God reluctantly accepts them. They believe He loves them because He must. The Scriptures paint an entirely different picture. Paul writes,

“...having predestined us to adoption as sons... to the praise of the glory of His grace, by which He made us accepted in the Beloved.”

Ephesians 1:5–6

Notice the language. You are not merely permitted into God's family. You are accepted. Completely. Joyfully.

Because you are united with Christ. The Father does not see you apart from His Son.

"I must earn God's acceptance."

This lie has exhausted countless believers. They constantly feel they have not prayed enough. Served enough. Given enough. Suffered enough.

Performed well enough. Yet Scripture declares,

“For by grace you have been saved through faith... not of works, lest anyone should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Your acceptance was never earned. It was given. Good works follow salvation. They never purchase it.

"I am still condemned."

Nothing robs believers of confidence more quickly than condemnation.

Many wake every morning feeling as though God is still angry with them.

Yet Paul begins one of the greatest chapters in the Bible with these words:

“There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.”

Romans 8:1

Notice the certainty. Not, "Less condemnation." Not, "Occasional condemnation."

No condemnation.

Why?

Because Christ bore it.

God will never require you to pay twice for what His Son has already paid in full.

"I will never overcome."

This is another lie many believers quietly accept. The struggle becomes their identity. The weakness becomes their name. The failure becomes their expectation. Yet Scripture says,

“Yet in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him who loved us.”

Romans 8:37

Notice that Paul does not say believers become conquerors after they have won every battle.

He says they already are more than conquerors through Christ.

Victory begins with believing God's declaration before circumstances appear to confirm it.

When Our Language Contradicts God's Word

Sometimes we unknowingly reinforce these false identities through the language we repeatedly use.

Words matter. They shape expectation. They reinforce belief.

If you continually describe yourself in ways that God never does, you gradually train your own heart to believe those descriptions.

The issue is not positive thinking. Neither is it pretending that struggles do not exist. The issue is agreeing with God. The believer should learn to ask,

"Can I support this statement about myself from Scripture?"

If the answer is no, then no matter how familiar the statement sounds, it must be rejected.

When Our Songs Shape Our Theology

This is equally true of the songs we sing.

Music is one of God's greatest gifts.

Throughout Scripture, God's people sang to remember His works, celebrate His faithfulness and declare His truth.

At the same time, songs are powerful teachers.

The words we sing repeatedly often shape the way we think about God, ourselves and the Christian life.

For that reason, every song should ultimately be tested by Scripture. Some songs beautifully celebrate the finished work of Christ. Others honestly express seasons of grief, repentance or longing. There is a proper place for both. Yet problems arise when emotional language becomes our permanent theology.

Sometimes believers repeatedly sing themes that emphasise only their weakness while saying very little about Christ's victory.

Sometimes songs dwell continually upon guilt but rarely celebrate justification.

Sometimes they speak as though believers remain permanently distant from God instead of rejoicing in the bold access secured through the blood of Christ (Hebrews 10:19–22).

Sometimes they leave the impression that Christians remain nothing more than miserable sinners, while giving little attention to the glorious truth that those who are in Christ are now new creations, children of God and the righteousness of God in Him.

This is not a call to become critical of worship music. Every sermon. Every tradition. Every devotional. Every conversation.

Every song. Must ultimately submit to the authority of God's Word. Our emotions are real. But they are not our authority. God's Word is.

If a thought, a habit of speech or even a cherished lyric causes you to think less of what God has accomplished in Christ than Scripture declares, then love for the truth requires that you hold on to Scripture and let everything else be corrected by it.

For in the end, the believer is not called to live according to feelings, traditions or familiar expressions.

The believer is called to live according to every word that proceeds from the mouth of God (Matthew 4:4).

And that is exactly why the enemy works tirelessly to keep believers from agreeing with God's verdict concerning them.

Vigilance: Standing Firm in Who You Are

Understanding who you are in Christ is not the end of the battle.

It is the beginning of living from that reality. The New Testament repeatedly calls believers to remain vigilant. Not because Christ's victory is uncertain. Not because the devil is stronger than Christ. Not because believers should live in fear.

But because the enemy is relentless in his attempts to separate believers from the truth they have received.

Peter writes,

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.”

1 Peter 5:8

Notice that Peter does not say the devil devours everyone. He seeks whom he may devour. His success is not automatic. His effectiveness depends largely upon deception. James therefore gives believers a very different response from fear.

“Therefore submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

The order is important.

Submission comes before resistance.

Victory begins, not by focusing upon the devil, but by remaining under God's authority.

That is exactly what Adam failed to do. Adam abandoned dependence upon God. The believer is called to remain in it. Paul makes a similar appeal.

“Put on the whole armour of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.”

Ephesians 6:11

Notice the purpose of the armour. Not to earn victory. To stand in the victory Christ has already won.

Paul does not describe believers advancing against Satan to obtain acceptance before God.

He describes believers standing firmly in what Christ has already accomplished.

The armour itself reminds us of our identity.

The belt of truth reminds us that our lives must be secured by God's Word rather than by deception.

The breastplate of righteousness reminds us that our confidence before God rests in the righteousness of Christ, not in our own performance.

The shield of faith extinguishes the enemy's lies before they become unbelief.

The helmet of salvation protects our thinking by reminding us that we belong to Christ.

The sword of the Spirit is the Word of God, the very weapon Jesus used in the wilderness when His identity was challenged.

Every piece of the armour points us back to the finished work of Christ.

It is therefore significant that Paul repeatedly says,

“Stand.”

Ephesians 6:11, 13, 14

He never says, "Create your own victory." Nor, "Earn your position." He says,

Stand.

Stand in what Christ has already secured. Stand in what God has already declared. This is why Paul warns believers,

“...lest Satan should take advantage of us; for we are not ignorant of his devices.”

2 Corinthians 2:11

Ignorance is dangerous. Not ignorance of the devil's power. But ignorance of his schemes. His greatest scheme has never been to create a new truth. He simply disguises lies as truth.

He did it in Eden. He attempted it with Jesus. He continues doing it today. Sometimes he whispers, "God has abandoned you." Sometimes, "You will never change." Sometimes, "Your past is stronger than God's grace." Sometimes, "This struggle proves you are not really God's child." Every one of those lies has one objective. To separate your experience from God's declaration.

The believer therefore lives with two voices continually competing for attention. One voice reminds you of what God has said. The other continually questions it. One produces faith. The other produces unbelief.

One produces confidence. The other produces condemnation. One leads you into freedom.

The other seeks to draw you back into the slavery from which Christ has already delivered you.

This is why vigilance is essential. Not because we are afraid of losing Christ. But because we refuse to lose sight of Him. Not because we fear the enemy's authority. But because we recognise the subtlety of his deception.

The safest believer is not the one who talks most about spiritual warfare.

The safest believer is the one who remains firmly established in God's Word concerning Christ and His finished work.

The more clearly you see yourself through God's eyes, the less persuasive the enemy's accusations become.

The more firmly your identity is established in Christ, the less attractive his lies appear.

This brings us to one final observation.

Knowing these truths is not, by itself, enough.

Many believers sincerely believe portions of these realities, yet still find themselves thinking, speaking and reacting as though they were still living under their old identity.

Why does this happen?

Why does God's declaration sometimes seem so different from the believer's daily experience?

Those questions are too important to answer briefly.

They deserve a chapter of their own.

In the next chapter, we will discover why God places such emphasis on the renewing of the mind, and how transformed thinking becomes the bridge between the finished work of Christ and the believer's daily experience.

Only when the mind is continually renewed by the truth of God's Word does the believer begin to consistently live from the glorious realities already secured in Christ.

Chapter Summary

Throughout this chapter, one truth has been established repeatedly from Scripture:

God no longer defines you by Adam. He defines you by Christ.

The moment you believed the Gospel, something far greater happened than the forgiveness of your sins.

You became a new creation. The old man was crucified with Christ. You were reconciled to the Father. You were brought near by the blood of Christ. Your identity changed because your relationship with God changed.

These realities are not future promises. They are present truths.

Yet the Scriptures also reveal that the enemy has never abandoned his original strategy.

In Eden he questioned God's Word. In the wilderness he questioned Jesus' identity. Today he continues questioning the believer's identity. He cannot undo the finished work of Christ. He cannot make God reject those whom Christ has redeemed.

His strategy is therefore deception.

He seeks to persuade believers to live according to what they once were rather than according to what they have now become.

He attacks confidence. He stirs condemnation. He magnifies failure.

He encourages believers to define themselves by their struggles instead of by God's Word.

This is why every believer must become vigilant.

Not living in fear of the enemy.

But refusing to accept any thought, belief, tradition or experience that contradicts what God has declared in Christ.

The Christian life begins with agreeing with God's verdict. Your past does not define you. Your emotions do not define you. Your failures do not define you. Even your present struggles do not define you.

God's Word defines you.

When God calls you His child... believe Him. When God calls you righteous in Christ... believe Him. When God calls you accepted... believe Him. When God calls you holy... believe Him. When God says you are a new creation... believe Him.

Faith always begins by agreeing with what God has said.

And because identity determines expectation, decisions and behaviour, believing God's declaration concerning you is one of the greatest acts of worship you can offer Him.

The enemy wants you to spend your life trying to become what Christ has already made you.

Grace invites you to stop striving for an identity and begin living from the identity your Father has already given you in His Son.

Reflection Questions

  1. Which description do you believe most often defines your life—what God says about you in Christ, or what your past, emotions and failures say about you?
  1. Are there areas where you still approach God as though you must earn His acceptance instead of resting in the acceptance Christ has already secured for you?
  1. Which false identities discussed in this chapter have quietly influenced the way you think about yourself?
  1. How can you become more intentional about measuring your beliefs, conversations, favourite teachings and even the songs you sing against the truth of Scripture?
  1. What would change in your daily life if you consistently believed everything God says is already true about you in Christ?

Prayer

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for loving me enough to send Your Son, not merely to forgive my sins, but to make me a new creation.

Thank You that my identity is no longer determined by Adam, by my past, by my failures or by my feelings.

Thank You that in Christ I am Your child, accepted, forgiven, justified, reconciled and made righteous before You.

Forgive me for every time I have believed the enemy's accusations more readily than I have believed Your Word.

Forgive me for defining myself by my weaknesses instead of by Your finished work.

Help me to reject every thought, every belief and every influence that contradicts what You have declared concerning me.

Chapter 6 – The Renewed Mind

Knowing who you are in Christ is essential. But another question immediately follows: if these realities are already true, why do many believers still think, speak and live as though they are not? The answer is not that God's declaration has failed, but that this life was never designed to be produced by human effort. The next chapter introduces the Person through whom it is actually lived.
