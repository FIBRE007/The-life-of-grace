🎧 1. The World You Cannot See

The World You Cannot See

Introduction

Every day we make decisions based on what we can see.

We look at our bank accounts before deciding whether we can afford something. We read medical reports before concluding whether we are healthy or ill. We observe the economy before predicting our future. We judge relationships by people's behaviour, opportunities by visible circumstances and success by measurable results.

Our senses have become the primary source of how we interpret reality.

Yet Scripture repeatedly challenges this way of thinking.

The Bible presents a world that is far larger than the one our eyes can perceive. It teaches that behind the visible universe exists an unseen spiritual realm that is every bit as real as the physical world. In fact, it reveals that the visible world cannot be fully understood apart from the unseen.

Throughout the Bible, God occasionally pulls back the curtain separating these two realms. What people believed were ordinary circumstances suddenly became extraordinary when they discovered what was happening beyond human sight. Battles were influenced by invisible armies. Prayers were affected by unseen conflict. Kings made decisions while heavenly councils deliberated. Men believed they stood alone until God opened their eyes to see that they were surrounded by His heavenly hosts.

These accounts are not isolated miracles inserted into Scripture to entertain the reader. They reveal how reality truly is.

The unseen realm is not imaginary. It is not symbolic. It is not merely a poetic way of describing spiritual truths. It is real.

The greatest mistake we can make is assuming that what we can see is all that exists.

Living from the Fourth Dimension begins by rejecting that assumption.

It begins by recognising that visible circumstances never tell the whole story because Scripture reveals that there is always a greater reality beyond what human eyes can perceive.

This chapter is therefore not an invitation to become fascinated with angels, demons or extraordinary spiritual experiences. Its purpose is much greater than that.

It is to help us see the world as God reveals it.

For only when we understand the reality of the unseen can we begin to understand how God calls us to live within the visible.

The Assumption Nobody Examines

Most people rarely question the way they interpret life. When business succeeds, they look for economic explanations. When relationships fail, they examine personalities and circumstances. When nations prosper or decline, they analyse politics, education or military strength. When sickness comes, they seek medical explanations.

None of these explanations are wrong.

Scripture never denies the reality of natural causes, human decisions or physical processes.

The problem arises when we assume that these visible explanations tell the entire story.

Modern society has trained us to believe that reality consists only of what can be measured, observed and explained.

If something cannot be touched, tested or photographed, many assume it cannot be real.

Even believers sometimes think this way.

We pray on Sunday but spend the rest of the week interpreting life as though the physical world were all that exists.

Yet the Bible consistently presents a completely different picture.

From Genesis to Revelation, God reveals that visible events are often connected to realities hidden from human sight.

Again and again, people discover that what they thought was merely physical was also spiritual.

The servant of Elisha believed they were surrounded by an enemy army until God opened his eyes.

Daniel believed heaven had delayed answering his prayer until an angel revealed the unseen conflict that had taken place.

Job believed he was suffering unexplained disasters until God revealed events occurring beyond human sight.

Peter thought he was about to face ordinary persecution until Jesus revealed that Satan had sought permission to sift him.

In every case, the visible story was only part of the story.

There was another dimension to reality that could not be seen with natural eyes.

This forces us to ask an important question.

What if many of the circumstances we experience every day are not fully explained by what we can see?

What if the visible world is not the whole world?

What if what appears to be the beginning of a story is actually its final chapter?

Scripture repeatedly invites us to ask these questions because it reveals a universe that is far richer and far deeper than our physical senses can perceive.

Before we examine those examples, however, we must begin at the beginning. Where did the visible world itself come from? The answer provides the foundation for everything else we shall study.

The Visible Came Out of the Invisible

If the physical world is not the highest reality, then where did it come from?

The answer is one of the most profound truths in all of Scripture.

The universe did not begin with matter. It did not begin with energy. It did not begin with time. It began with God. The opening words of the Bible declare,

“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.”

Genesis 1:1

Before there was a beginning, there was God. Before there were stars, oceans, mountains or mankind, God already existed. This immediately tells us something remarkable. The spiritual did not emerge from the physical. The physical emerged because of the activity of the eternal God.

Creation did not begin with material substance but with the Creator Himself. The Bible continues,

“Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light.”

Genesis 1:3

Notice the sequence. God spoke. Then creation responded. The Word preceded the world. Speech preceded substance.

The invisible activity of God produced visible reality.

Everything we now see owes its existence to what could not be seen.

The writer of Hebrews makes this truth unmistakably clear.

“By faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that the things which are seen were not made of things which are visible.”

Hebrews 11:3

This verse completely reverses the way many people think. The visible did not produce the visible. The seen came from the unseen. Creation itself bears witness that the invisible realm is the greater reality. John begins his Gospel by taking us even further back.

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made.”

John 1:1–3

Everything that exists owes its existence to God. Nothing in creation is self-existent. Nothing exists independently of Him. Nothing sustains itself. Paul says the same thing when speaking about Christ.

“For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth, visible and invisible... All things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.”

Colossians 1:16–17

Notice Paul's language carefully.

He speaks of things visible and invisible.

Scripture does not divide reality into what is real and what is imaginary.

It divides creation into what is visible and what is invisible. Both are real. Both were created by God. Both exist because He wills them to exist. Paul goes even further.

He says that in Christ "all things consist."

In other words, creation is not merely something God made long ago and left to operate by itself.

The universe continues because God continually sustains it. Every heartbeat. Every sunrise. Every season. Every law of nature.

Every galaxy. Every atom. Everything continues because God upholds His creation. The physical universe is therefore not independent. It depends entirely upon the unseen God who created and sustains it.

This truth changes the way we view reality.

Many people think of the spiritual realm as something distant or secondary, while the physical world feels immediate and permanent.

Scripture presents the opposite picture. The physical world is temporary. The unseen God is eternal. The physical world is dependent. God is self-existent.

Creation changes. God never changes. What we see is real, but it is not ultimate. The visible world is the expression of a greater reality.

It points beyond itself to the God who spoke it into existence and who continues to uphold it by His power.

If this is true of creation itself, then we should not be surprised to discover that throughout Scripture many events in the visible world are connected to realities that cannot be seen.

Again and again, God lifts the curtain and allows us to glimpse what is happening beyond human sight.

When He does, we discover that the visible story is never the whole story.

The events taking place on earth are often connected to realities in the unseen realm.

It is to those remarkable moments that we now turn.

Decided There, Seen Here

If the visible world were all that existed, then every event could be explained entirely by what happened on earth.

But Scripture repeatedly challenges that assumption.

Again and again, God allows us to see that visible events are often connected to realities beyond human sight.

The Bible does not reveal these moments to satisfy our curiosity about the unseen. Rather, it teaches us that what we experience in the physical world is not always the complete picture.

One of the clearest examples is found in the life of Job.

From Job's perspective, disaster struck without explanation.

In rapid succession, he lost his livestock, his servants, his children and eventually his health.

Anyone observing these events from earth could only speculate about their cause. Some would have blamed misfortune. Others would have blamed the weather. Some might even have concluded that God had abandoned Job.

Yet the reader of the book knows something Job himself did not know.

Before any of those visible events occurred, there had already been activity in the unseen realm.

Job 1 opens not on earth but in the presence of God. There we are told that Satan appeared before the Lord and sought permission concerning Job. What unfolded in Job's life was therefore not an isolated series of earthly tragedies. There was an unseen dimension to the story that Job could not perceive.

This does not mean Satan acted independently. Far from it. The account repeatedly shows that he could do only what God permitted. Even in the unseen realm, God's authority remained absolute. The important lesson is this:

The visible events in Job's life could not be fully understood without recognising the unseen reality behind them.

The story teaches us to be cautious about interpreting life solely from appearances.

Another remarkable example is found in the book of Daniel. Daniel devoted himself to prayer and fasting, seeking understanding from God. After three weeks, an angel finally appeared and explained what had happened.

“Do not fear, Daniel, for from the first day that you set your heart to understand... your words were heard... But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me twenty-one days...”

Daniel 10:12–13

What an astonishing revelation. From Daniel's perspective, nothing appeared to be happening. For twenty-one days there was silence. Had God heard? Was heaven ignoring his prayer?

The answer was neither. The answer had been given from the very first day. Yet events in the unseen realm affected when Daniel received the messenger. Without this revelation, Daniel could easily have misunderstood his circumstances. The physical evidence suggested delay.

The unseen reality revealed that God had already responded.

How many times do we conclude that nothing is happening simply because we cannot yet see the outcome?

Daniel's experience reminds us that visible silence does not necessarily mean divine inactivity.

God may be working in ways that remain hidden from human sight. A different lesson emerges from the experience of Elisha's servant. When the Syrian army surrounded the city, the servant panicked. Everything he could see pointed towards defeat. The enemy was larger.

Stronger. Better equipped. From every visible perspective, the situation appeared hopeless. Elisha, however, remained completely calm. Why?

Because he understood that visible reality was not the whole reality. He prayed,

“Lord, I pray, open his eyes that he may see.”

2 Kings 6:17

God answered immediately.

The servant looked again.

This time he saw that the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire surrounding Elisha.

Notice something important. God did not create the heavenly army at that moment. It was already there. The only thing that changed was the servant's ability to see it. The unseen reality existed before his eyes were opened.

His fear had been based, not on what was false, but on what was incomplete.

He had seen the Syrian army. He had not seen the greater reality surrounding God's servant. This is often our experience as well. We see the problem. We do not always see God's provision.

We see opposition. We do not always see God's protection. We see delay. We do not always see God's activity.

The Bible reminds us that our inability to see the unseen does not mean it is absent.

Jesus revealed this same principle when speaking to Peter shortly before His arrest.

He said,

“Simon, Simon! Indeed, Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail...”

Luke 22:31–32

Peter was completely unaware of this conversation. He thought the coming hours would simply involve difficult circumstances. Jesus revealed that there was more taking place than Peter realised. Yet notice once again that Satan did not act independently. He had to ask.

Christ remained fully aware. Christ also interceded.

Peter's coming trial therefore involved both the visible events he would experience and the unseen realities Jesus revealed.

Finally, Paul draws the curtain back for every believer when he writes,

“For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this age, against spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places.”

Ephesians 6:12

Paul does not deny that believers encounter opposition from people.

Rather, he teaches that human conflict is not the deepest level of reality.

Behind many visible struggles lies a spiritual dimension that cannot be ignored.

This is not an invitation to blame every difficulty on spiritual forces.

Neither is it a call to become preoccupied with the kingdom of darkness.

It is a reminder that life is deeper than appearances.

The Bible repeatedly lifts the curtain just enough to teach us one enduring lesson:

The visible story is never the whole story.

Again and again, Scripture shows us that events on earth are connected to realities beyond human sight.

The unseen does not make the visible unreal. It explains why the visible can never be understood in isolation. Recognising this changes the way we approach every circumstance.

Instead of asking only, "What do I see?" we begin to ask, "What has God revealed?"

That question becomes the beginning of living from the Fourth Dimension.

Even the Ordinary Serves God's Purpose

By now, someone may ask an important question.

If the spiritual realm influences the physical world, does that mean every event has a direct supernatural cause?

Scripture's answer is no.

The Bible never encourages believers to abandon wisdom, reject natural explanations or see every circumstance as the direct activity of angels or demons.

God is the Creator of the natural order.

The laws that govern creation exist because He established them.

The changing seasons, the movement of the stars, the growth of living things and the processes of the human body all testify to His wisdom.

David beautifully declares,

“The heavens declare the glory of God; And the firmament shows His handiwork.”

Psalm 19:1

Nature is not separate from God. It continually bears witness to Him. Yet Scripture also teaches that God is never absent from His creation. The ordinary and the extraordinary both remain under His sovereign government. Jesus demonstrated this truth in a remarkable conversation with His disciples.

As they encountered a man who had been blind from birth, the disciples immediately searched for a visible explanation.

They asked,

“Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?”

John 9:2

Their question reflected a common way of thinking. Surely there must be an obvious cause. Someone must be responsible. Jesus answered,

“Neither this man nor his parents sinned, but that the works of God should be revealed in him.”

John 9:3

Notice what Jesus did. He did not deny the reality of the man's blindness. Neither did He become preoccupied with tracing every detail of its origin. Instead, He directed His disciples' attention to God's purpose. The blindness was real.

The suffering was real. But the visible condition did not tell the whole story. God's purpose was greater than the disciples could see. The same truth appears in the account of Lazarus. When Jesus received news that His friend was seriously ill, He said,

“This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God may be glorified through it.”

John 11:4

From the perspective of those around Lazarus, the situation seemed simple. A man had become ill. His condition worsened. He died. The visible facts were undeniable.

Yet Jesus revealed that there was a greater purpose unfolding. God's glory would ultimately be displayed through circumstances that appeared hopeless. Again, the visible facts were true. But they were not the complete truth. This teaches us an important lesson.

Believers should never live in one of two extremes. One extreme sees only natural causes and forgets God entirely. The other attributes every ordinary event to unusual spiritual activity. Scripture avoids both errors.

It teaches that God is Lord over both the ordinary and the extraordinary.

Medical science may explain how a disease develops. Economics may explain why markets rise and fall. Meteorology may explain why storms occur. History may explain why nations rise and decline. These explanations are valuable.

But they are never complete without recognising that the whole creation remains under God's sovereign rule.

Nothing in creation exists outside His knowledge. Nothing unfolds beyond His authority. Nothing frustrates His ultimate purpose. This perspective changes the way believers respond to life's circumstances. Instead of asking only, "What is happening?" we begin asking, "What is God accomplishing?"

Instead of concluding that visible circumstances have the final word, we learn to trust that God remains at work even when His activity is hidden from our eyes.

The Bible therefore calls us to respect the realities of the physical world without becoming imprisoned by them.

We acknowledge facts. We seek wisdom. We make responsible decisions. Yet we refuse to believe that visible circumstances alone determine reality. The God who created the visible world continues to govern it.

And because He does, there is always more taking place than our eyes alone can perceive.

That confidence becomes the foundation upon which faith stands.

The Battle for Human Agreement

If the unseen spiritual realm is real, then one important question remains.

How does that unseen realm influence human life?

Scripture gives a remarkable answer.

Again and again, it shows that the greatest battlefield is not geography, politics or military strength.

It is the human heart and mind. The struggle is ultimately a battle over truth. A battle over whose voice we will believe. From the very beginning of Scripture, we see two voices. God speaks.

Then another voice speaks.

In the Garden of Eden, God had already revealed His will to Adam.

His Word was clear. His instruction was sufficient. Then the serpent entered the story. His first weapon was neither force nor violence. It was deception.

He asked,

“Has God indeed said...?”

Genesis 3:1

With one question, he attempted to undermine confidence in God's Word. Notice what happened. He did not force Eve to disobey. He persuaded her to question what God had said. The battle began with words.

With truth. With belief.

Although we will examine the events and consequences of the Fall in detail in Chapter Three, this passage already teaches us an important principle.

The unseen realm often seeks influence through what people choose to believe. God speaks truth. The enemy speaks lies. Human beings choose which voice they will trust. That pattern continues throughout the rest of Scripture.

Moses stood before Israel and declared,

“I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing; therefore choose life...”

Deuteronomy 30:19

Joshua later challenged the nation,

“Choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve...”

Joshua 24:15

Both men understood something profound. God does not treat people as machines. He addresses them as moral beings capable of responding to His Word. Every invitation to trust Him is also an invitation to choose. The New Testament develops this principle even further.

Paul writes,

“So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

Faith is not wishful thinking. It is the response of the heart to what God has spoken. God's Word produces confidence because it reveals reality as He sees it. The enemy, however, works in the opposite direction. His greatest weapon is deception.

He seeks to replace God's truth with falsehood. He exaggerates fear. He magnifies doubt. He distorts God's character. He persuades people to trust appearances instead of God's promises.

James explains how temptation unfolds.

“But each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin...”

James 1:14–15

James does not describe temptation as irresistible. He describes a process. A person is enticed. The heart responds. A choice is made.

Scripture therefore presents human beings neither as helpless victims nor as completely independent.

Our choices matter. What we believe matters. Whose voice we trust matters.

Every day we decide whether we will interpret life through what our circumstances are saying or through what God has revealed in His Word.

This is one of the most practical implications of the unseen realm. The greatest spiritual battles are often fought long before they become visible. They begin in the thoughts we entertain. The beliefs we accept. The words we agree with.

The conclusions we draw about God, ourselves and our circumstances. When fear says, "There is no hope," God's Word says,

“For with God nothing will be impossible.”

Luke 1:37

When circumstances appear permanent,

God reminds us that He is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think.

When visible evidence seems overwhelming, faith asks a different question:

"What has God said?"

That question changes everything.

It shifts our attention from appearances to God's revelation.

It reminds us that the highest authority is not our emotions, our circumstances or even our experiences.

The highest authority is the Word of God.

Living from the Fourth Dimension therefore begins with learning to value God's Word above everything our natural senses report.

It means allowing His truth to shape our thinking before our circumstances shape our conclusions.

For the person who believes God's Word begins to interpret life differently.

Not because the visible world has disappeared.

But because they have learned that the visible world is not the final authority on reality.

The unseen God has already spoken.

And His Word remains the surest guide for understanding the world we can see.

Clearing the Ground: What This Is Not

Before we go further, a few things must be said plainly, because the phrase the Fourth Dimension carries baggage. If the wrong picture settles in your mind now, you will misread everything that follows.

This is not the fourth dimension of physics. Science uses that term for time, or for a spatial dimension beyond the three we experience. That is a legitimate use of the words. It is simply not what this book means.

This is not mysticism, and it is not the New Age. There is no technique here for unlocking hidden human potential, no higher consciousness to attain, no journey the mind takes out of the body. Those systems place the power in you. Scripture places it in God, and calls you to know Him rather than to master a method.

“Let no one cheat you of your reward, taking delight in false humility and worship of angels, intruding into those things which he has not seen.”

Colossians 2:18

This is not the paranormal. It has nothing to do with ghosts, mediums or contacting the dead. Scripture does not merely discourage such things; it forbids them, and for our protection.

“And when they say to you, 'Seek those who are mediums and wizards, who whisper and mutter,' should not a people seek their God? Should they seek the dead on behalf of the living?”

Isaiah 8:19

There is a real spiritual world, and precisely because it is real, God has determined how His people may approach it — through Christ, and not otherwise.

This is not escapism. Living from the Fourth Dimension never means pretending the diagnosis is not real or the debt does not exist. Faith does not deny facts. It refuses to let facts be the highest authority.

This is not reserved for special people. Not for mystics, not for prophets, not for those who have walked with God for decades. Every believer has the same Spirit, the same access and the same standing in Christ.

This does not require extraordinary experiences. No visions are necessary. No trances, no dramatic manifestations. Most of the life described in this book is lived quietly, by ordinary people who simply took God at His word.

This is not a place you visit. You do not enter it and return. It is a reality you live from, in the middle of school runs, invoices and difficult conversations.

And this is not a preoccupation with darkness. Some believers become so absorbed with the enemy that they grow more conscious of him than of God. That is not maturity. It is fear wearing spiritual language.

What, then, does the phrase mean here?

The Fourth Dimension is simply a name for the unseen spiritual realm that Scripture reveals — the realm from which the visible world came, in which God reigns, and from which the believer is called to see, think, speak and live. It is not a technique, an experience or a destination. It is the reality you already inhabit, and the perspective you are invited to live from.

“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

2 Corinthians 5:7

Ordinary walking. In an unseen reality. Every day.

The Kingdom of God and the Kingdom of Darkness

One of the clearest conclusions we reach from reading Scripture is that there is more than one spiritual kingdom influencing events in the visible world.

On one hand, the Bible reveals the activity of the Kingdom of God.

On the other, it reveals the activity of the kingdom of darkness.

This does not mean there are two equal kingdoms competing for ultimate control of the universe.

Scripture never presents such an idea.

Rather, it shows that while God reigns over all creation, there also exists an organised kingdom of rebellion that seeks to oppose His purposes and influence humanity.

The important point for this chapter is not to explain how these kingdoms came to exist or why the kingdom of darkness has influence. Those questions will be addressed in later chapters. For now, it is enough to observe that Scripture repeatedly reveals both kingdoms producing real and observable effects in the physical world.

Jesus often spoke about the Kingdom of God as a present reality.

He did not merely describe it as something belonging to the distant future.

He demonstrated its power in the present.

When accused of casting out demons by demonic power, Jesus replied,

“But if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you.”

Matthew 12:28

Notice what Jesus teaches. The deliverance of a person from demonic oppression was visible. People witnessed it with their own eyes.

Yet Jesus explained that what they had seen was evidence of something greater.

The Kingdom of God had broken into the visible world. The physical event pointed to a deeper spiritual reality. The same pattern appears in the book of Acts. When Peter was imprisoned by Herod, the situation seemed hopeless. The guards were numerous.

The prison was secure. Humanly speaking, there appeared to be no possibility of escape. Yet while the Church prayed, God acted. An angel entered the prison. Peter's chains fell off.

The prison doors opened. Peter walked out free.

To those who saw Peter standing outside, it appeared to be an astonishing escape.

Scripture reveals that behind the visible miracle was the activity of God's Kingdom.

Once again, what happened on earth was connected to realities beyond human sight.

Scripture also reveals that the kingdom of darkness produces visible effects. Jesus spoke of a woman who had been crippled for eighteen years. Rather than describing her condition merely in physical terms, He said,

“So ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound—think of it—for eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the Sabbath?”

Luke 13:16

Jesus did not deny the reality of her physical condition.

But He revealed that there was more to her situation than human eyes alone could perceive.

Paul likewise wrote,

“Whose minds the god of this age has blinded, who do not believe...”

2 Corinthians 4:4

Here the blindness is not physical.

It is spiritual.

People may possess healthy eyes and brilliant minds, yet remain unable to recognise the truth of the Gospel.

Again, Scripture points beyond visible reality.

Peter gives another sobering reminder.

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.”

1 Peter 5:8

Peter does not write these words to create fear.

He writes them to awaken spiritual awareness.

Believers are called to be alert because life involves more than what can be observed with natural eyes.

These passages establish an important truth.

The Bible does not describe the physical world as an isolated system operating independently.

It presents a world in which the Kingdom of God is actively at work and in which the kingdom of darkness also seeks to influence people and events.

Yet Scripture never leaves us wondering which kingdom is greater. Throughout the Bible, God's authority remains unquestioned. He is never portrayed as struggling to maintain control. He never reacts in panic. He never loses His throne.

The kingdom of darkness may oppose. It may deceive. It may tempt. It may seek to destroy. But it never operates outside God's sovereign government.

That truth should shape the believer's perspective. We are not called to become preoccupied with darkness. Neither are we called to ignore its reality. Instead, we are called to become conscious of God.

Our confidence rests, not in our ability to understand every spiritual activity, but in the certainty that God reigns.

Recognising the reality of these two kingdoms should therefore produce neither fear nor fascination.

It should produce faith. Faith that sees beyond appearances. Faith that trusts God's sovereign rule. Faith that recognises that the visible world is not the whole story.

And faith that lives every day in the light of the greater reality revealed in God's Word.

The Two Kingdoms Are Not Equal

One thing must be settled before this chapter closes, or you will finish it carrying fear instead of confidence.

These two kingdoms are not rivals.

Scripture never presents God and Satan as opposing powers of comparable strength, locked in a contest whose outcome is uncertain. That is the story mythology tells. It is not the story the Bible tells.

Satan is a created being. God is the Creator. There is no category in which they can be compared.

His power is derived, because he has nothing that was not first given. His knowledge is limited, which is why he probes and tempts rather than simply knowing. His authority is permitted, and it has boundaries he cannot cross. When he came against Job, he could act only within the limits God set.

“And the LORD said to Satan, 'Behold, all that he has is in your power; only do not lay a hand on his person.'”

Job 1:12

Notice who sets the boundary. His time is short, and he knows it. And his defeat is not pending. It is finished.

“Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it.”

Colossians 2:15

Disarmed. Made a public spectacle. Triumphed over. Paul writes in the past tense about a victory already secured at the Cross.

This is why the believer's posture is confidence rather than dread.

“He who is in you is greater than he who is in the world.”

1 John 4:4

Greater. Not slightly stronger. Not narrowly ahead. Greater.

And you are no longer where you were.

“He has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love.”

Colossians 1:13

You have already been moved. That transfer is not something you are working toward; it is something Christ accomplished and God performed.

So when we speak in this book of two kingdoms affecting the visible world, hear it correctly. One is a Kingdom. The other is a rebellion. One reigns. The other operates only within limits set by the One who reigns.

You do not fight for victory.

You live from it.

Why This Matters

At this point, someone may ask,

"What difference does all of this make to my everyday life?"

The answer is:

It changes everything.

If the physical world is all there is, then our lives will always be governed by what we can see.

Our confidence will rise when circumstances improve. Our peace will disappear when circumstances worsen. Our decisions will be controlled by appearances.

Our prayers will often become expressions of desperation rather than acts of faith.

But Scripture repeatedly calls us to live differently. Not because the visible world is unimportant. But because it is not the highest reality.

The believer learns to respect visible facts without allowing them to become the final authority.

Facts matter. But God's Word carries greater authority than facts. Circumstances are real. But they do not always reveal the whole story. The doctor's report may describe your present condition.

The financial statement may accurately reflect your current situation. The opposition you face may be genuine. The delay may be real. The disappointment may be painful. Scripture never asks us to deny these realities.

Instead, it teaches us that they are not the whole reality.

The life of faith begins when we recognise that God's revelation is more trustworthy than our limited perspective.

Paul expressed this beautifully when he wrote,

“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

2 Corinthians 5:7

Faith is not pretending that difficulties do not exist. Neither is it refusing to acknowledge visible circumstances. Faith simply refuses to make visible circumstances the highest authority. Faith begins with what God has revealed. Then it interprets visible circumstances through that truth.

This is how Abraham continued believing when his body suggested otherwise. This is how the apostles remained joyful even under persecution. Their confidence did not come from favourable circumstances.

It came from confidence in the God who rules beyond what human eyes can see.

This is the invitation of the Fourth Dimension. It is an invitation to see life differently. To think differently. To pray differently. To speak differently.

To live differently.

Not because we have escaped the physical world, but because we have learned that the physical world is not independent of the spiritual reality God has revealed.

Every day we will face situations that tempt us to judge reality by appearances alone.

Every day we will be tempted to conclude that what we see is all that God is doing.

This chapter reminds us that Scripture repeatedly says otherwise.

Again and again, God pulls back the curtain.

Again and again, He reminds us that His purposes often unfold beyond human sight.

Again and again, He teaches us that visible events are frequently connected to unseen realities.

The greatest lesson of this chapter is therefore not simply that there is an unseen realm.

It is that God has revealed enough about that unseen realm for us to live with confidence rather than confusion.

We do not need to fear what we cannot see. Neither do we need to become fascinated by it. We simply need to trust the One who sees all things perfectly. The visible world is real. But it is not the highest reality.

The unseen spiritual realm is real. It continually intersects with the visible world.

Scripture repeatedly reveals that what happens on earth cannot always be understood by looking at earth alone.

Living from the Fourth Dimension begins when we stop asking, "What do my eyes tell me?" and begin asking,

"What has God revealed?"

That question marks the beginning of a completely different way of living.

One that will shape every chapter that follows.

Chapter Summary

This chapter began by challenging one of the most common assumptions people make—that the visible world is the whole of reality. While our daily lives are lived within the physical realm, Scripture consistently reveals that there is an unseen spiritual realm that is just as real and that continually influences what takes place in the world we can see.

We first discovered that the physical universe did not produce itself. Before there was time, space or matter, God already existed. Through His spoken Word, the visible world came into being. The physical realm therefore owes its existence to the unseen God who created and continues to sustain it.

We then examined several biblical accounts in which God temporarily drew back the curtain separating the visible from the invisible. Job's suffering, Daniel's delayed answer to prayer, Elisha's heavenly protection, Peter's coming trial and Paul's teaching on spiritual warfare all demonstrated that visible events are often connected to realities beyond human sight. Again and again, Scripture reminded us that the visible story is never the whole story.

We also learned that recognising the spiritual realm does not require rejecting natural explanations. God's sovereign rule extends over both the ordinary and the extraordinary. Medical conditions, weather, economics and human decisions remain real, yet they all exist within His government and purpose.

Next, we saw that one of the greatest arenas of spiritual activity is the human heart. Throughout Scripture, God speaks truth while the enemy seeks to deceive. Human beings continually choose which voice they will believe. Faith begins by agreeing with God's Word rather than allowing visible circumstances to become the final authority.

Finally, we discovered that Scripture reveals both the Kingdom of God and the kingdom of darkness actively producing visible effects in the world. Yet these kingdoms are never presented as equals. God alone reigns absolutely. The kingdom of darkness possesses influence but never independent authority. Therefore, believers are called neither to fear darkness nor to become fascinated by it, but to place their confidence in God's sovereign rule.

We also cleared the ground of some common misunderstandings. The Fourth Dimension is not the fourth dimension of physics, not mysticism, not the paranormal, not escapism, not reserved for the spiritually advanced, and not a preoccupation with darkness. It is simply a name for the unseen realm Scripture reveals, and the perspective from which the believer is invited to live.

The greatest lesson of this chapter is simple but life-changing:

The visible world is real, but it is not the highest reality.

Scripture repeatedly teaches that life cannot be interpreted correctly by appearances alone. The unseen spiritual realm continually intersects with the physical world, and God has graciously revealed enough about that unseen reality for His people to live by faith rather than by sight.
