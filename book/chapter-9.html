Thanksgiving, Peace, and Rest

Introduction

In the previous chapter we discovered that God’s sons can live confidently in the season between the promise and its manifestation, because the Father’s faithfulness never depends upon how long we wait. But confidence of that kind does not remain hidden in the heart. It changes the way a believer carries himself through ordinary days.

One of the greatest evidences that a believer is living from the Fourth Dimension is not merely what he believes, but the atmosphere he carries.

Many people associate spirituality with outward activities. They measure spiritual growth by knowledge, gifts, ministry or visible achievements. While these things have their place, the New Testament repeatedly reveals something deeper.

God's sons carry an atmosphere. Where they go, they should carry peace instead of anxiety. They should carry thanksgiving instead of complaining. They should carry rest instead of striving. This atmosphere does not originate from favourable circumstances.

Neither is it the result of a naturally positive personality. It is the fruit of living consciously in union with Christ.

Throughout this book we have discovered that God's sons possess a new identity. We have seen that they live from the Fourth Dimension—the unseen reality of God's Kingdom—rather than merely reacting to the visible world. We have learned that faith responds to God's character instead of circumstances and that confidence rests upon the Father's faithfulness rather than visible evidence.

Now we arrive at a beautiful consequence of those truths. When a believer truly knows the Father... When he understands his union with Christ... When he becomes conscious of the Holy Spirit within... When he lives from the finished work of redemption...

His life begins to produce an entirely different atmosphere. Thanksgiving becomes natural. Peace becomes continual. Rest becomes his way of living. These are not spiritual techniques.

They are not emotional experiences that come and go. They are the normal atmosphere of sons who know their Father. This does not mean God's sons never face difficulties. Jesus Himself said,

“In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Notice that Jesus did not promise the absence of pressure. He promised His victory in the midst of it. Likewise, thanksgiving is not reserved for days when everything appears perfect. Peace is not dependent upon the absence of conflict. Rest is not the absence of work.

Rather, these realities flow from knowing that Christ has already overcome the world and that His sons now share in His victory.

One of the greatest misconceptions among believers is the idea that peace will come after every problem has disappeared.

Scripture teaches the opposite. Peace comes because Christ is present. Thanksgiving comes because the Father is good. Rest comes because the work of redemption has been completed. The visible world may still contain uncertainty.

The Father's Kingdom does not. The Third Dimension may still present challenges. The Fourth Dimension remains unchanged. This is why God's sons can live differently. Their lives are governed by another reality.

They interpret circumstances through God's Word instead of interpreting God's Word through circumstances.

As believers become increasingly conscious of their union with Christ, something remarkable happens.

Fear gradually loses its influence. Complaining gives way to gratitude. Striving gives way to confidence. Anxiety gives way to peace.

The believer begins to reflect the atmosphere of Heaven while still living on earth.

This chapter explores that atmosphere.

Not as something believers struggle to manufacture.

But as the natural expression of sons who remain conscious of the Father's presence, Christ's finished work and the continual ministry of the Holy Spirit.

Thanksgiving—The Language of Faith

Every family has a language.

Not merely the language they speak, but the atmosphere they create through their words.

In the family of God, one of the distinguishing languages of sons is thanksgiving.

Thanksgiving is much more than good manners.

It is much more than saying "thank You" after receiving a blessing.

It is one of the clearest expressions of faith found anywhere in Scripture.

The believer who continually gives thanks is declaring something profound. He is declaring that God's goodness is not determined by today's circumstances. He is declaring that God's faithfulness remains constant. He is declaring that Christ's finished work is sufficient.

He is declaring that the Father is worthy of praise before circumstances change.

This is why thanksgiving occupies such an important place throughout the New Testament.

Paul writes,

“Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.”

Philippians 4:6

Notice something remarkable. Paul does not say, "Pray first, then give thanks after the answer comes." He places thanksgiving alongside prayer. Why? Because thanksgiving is itself an expression of faith. It acknowledges God's goodness before visible evidence appears.

This truth beautifully complements what we discovered in the previous chapter.

God's sons continue thanking the Father because they already know He is faithful.

Their gratitude is not postponed until circumstances improve. It flows from confidence in His character. Immediately after this instruction Paul adds,

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:7

There is a beautiful progression. Prayer. Thanksgiving. Peace. The thankful heart becomes the peaceful heart.

Not because thanksgiving changes God's character. But because thanksgiving continually reminds the believer of God's character. Paul gives another remarkable command.

“In everything give thanks; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:18

Notice carefully what he says. He does not say, "Give thanks for everything." He says,

"In everything give thanks."

There is an important difference. Scripture never teaches believers to celebrate evil. We do not thank God for sin. We do not thank Him for injustice.

We do not thank Him for sickness or suffering as though these things originate from Him.

Rather, in every circumstance we continue thanking Him because He remains good, faithful and sovereign.

Our thanksgiving is directed towards the Father's unchanging character. Not towards every circumstance. Paul again writes,

“Giving thanks always for all things to God the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

Ephesians 5:20

Notice the emphasis. Always. Not occasionally. Thanksgiving is not an event. It is a lifestyle.

It becomes the continual language of sons. The same truth appears in Colossians. Paul repeatedly uses expressions such ...meditate on these things." Then he makes an extraordinary promise:

“The things which you learned and received and heard and saw in me, these do, and the God of peace will be with you.”

Philippians 4:9

Notice the progression. The peace of God guards the believer. Peace is therefore not merely an emotional state. It is the continual experience of fellowship with God.

This is one of the reasons thanksgiving occupies such an important place in the believer's life.

Thanksgiving continually directs the heart towards God's goodness. Prayer expresses dependence upon the Father. Meditation renews the mind with His truth.

Together they create an atmosphere in which the peace of Christ increasingly governs the believer's life.

This peace is also one of the ministries of the Holy Spirit.

Earlier chapters established that the Holy Spirit continually reminds believers of their union with Christ and reveals spiritual realities to them.

As we yield to His ministry, He continually turns our attention away from fear and back to Christ.

He reminds us that we are sons. He reminds us that we have received every spiritual blessing in Christ. As these truths become increasingly real to us, peace naturally increases. This is why peace cannot be separated from sonship. Servants often live in fear because they are uncertain of their standing.

Sons live in peace because they know they belong. Servants wonder whether they have done enough. Sons rest in what Christ has already accomplished. Servants are driven by performance. Sons are established in relationship.

Peace is therefore one of the evidences that the believer is living consciously from his identity in Christ.

Jesus reinforces this truth when He says,

“These things I have spoken to you, that in Me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation; but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Notice the contrast.

In Me... peace.

In the world... tribulation.

Jesus never promised His followers a life free from difficulty. He promised something infinitely greater. He promised His presence in the midst of difficulty. The believer therefore does not deny the existence of trouble. Neither does he pretend that challenges do not exist.

Instead, he lives from a higher reality.

The Fourth Dimension continually reminds him that Christ has already overcome the world.

This changes the believer's perspective entirely. Problems no longer become the defining reality. Christ does. Conflict no longer governs the heart. Christ's victory does.

The believer learns to interpret every circumstance through the finished work of Christ rather than interpreting Christ's finished work through present circumstances.

This is why God's sons are able to remain calm when others become overwhelmed.

It is not because they possess greater natural strength.

It is because their confidence rests in a Kingdom that cannot be shaken.

Hebrews describes believers as those who have received,

“a kingdom which cannot be shaken.”

Hebrews 12:28

If the Kingdom cannot be shaken, then the sons who live consciously from that Kingdom need not be shaken either.

Their peace is anchored in something far more permanent than the changing world around them.

Practically, this means that believers must intentionally guard the focus of their minds.

Every day the world presents reasons to fear. News headlines. Economic uncertainty. Personal disappointments. Unexpected challenges.

If these become the primary focus of the mind, anxiety will naturally increase.

But when believers continually meditate upon God's Word, remember His faithfulness, thank Him for His grace and remain conscious of His presence, the peace of Christ increasingly governs their hearts.

Peace is therefore not passive.

It is the continual result of living consciously in union with Christ.

It is cultivated as believers remain sensitive to the Holy Spirit, renew their minds with Scripture and continually remember the Father's goodness.

This peace does not merely bless the believer. It becomes a testimony to the world. People notice when someone remains calm in circumstances that normally produce fear. They notice when joy remains in the midst of difficulty. They notice when hope refuses to disappear.

Such peace cannot be explained merely by personality. It reveals the presence of Christ.

As God's sons increasingly allow the peace of Christ to rule in their hearts, they discover another beautiful reality.

Peace naturally leads into rest.

The believer who knows the Father's peace gradually ceases striving to earn what Christ has already secured.

He begins to live from the finished work rather than labouring to achieve it.

This brings us to one of the most beautiful invitations in all of Scripture:

the invitation to enter the rest of God.

Entering the Rest of God

One of the greatest invitations ever extended to humanity is the invitation to enter God's rest.

Yet few biblical truths have been more misunderstood.

Many assume that rest means inactivity.

Others imagine that resting in God means withdrawing from responsibility or abandoning diligent work.

Still others think rest is simply an emotional feeling that comes after problems have disappeared.

The Scriptures teach something entirely different. God's rest is not the absence of activity. It is the absence of striving.

It is the settled confidence of sons who know that the finished work of Christ has accomplished what human effort never could.

Throughout this book we have repeatedly discovered that the Christian life begins, continues and matures by grace.

The believer does not earn sonship. He receives it. He does not achieve righteousness. He receives it. He does not strive to become accepted by the Father.

He has already been accepted in Christ.

Rest therefore begins when believers become conscious of what Christ has already accomplished.

Jesus extends this beautiful invitation:

“Come to Me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Notice where rest begins. Not with a method. Not with a principle. Not even with a doctrine. Rest begins with a Person.

Jesus says,

"Come to Me."

True rest is found in continual fellowship with Christ. Everything else flows from that relationship. Jesus continues,

“Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light.”

Matthew 11:29

This appears almost paradoxical. Jesus speaks about both a yoke and rest. How can carrying a yoke produce rest? The answer is found in whose yoke it is. The believer is no longer carrying life alone.

He is joined to Christ. The burden no longer rests upon human strength.

The believer now walks in partnership with the One who has already overcome the world.

His yoke is easy because His grace is sufficient. His burden is light because He carries what we never could. This is one of the greatest privileges of sonship. God never intended His children to carry the weight of life independently. He intended them to live in continual dependence upon Him.

The writer of Hebrews develops this truth in remarkable detail. Reflecting upon Israel's failure to enter the Promised Land, he writes,

“There remains therefore a rest for the people of God.”

Hebrews 4:9

Notice the tense. There remains a rest. Not merely a future rest in eternity. A present spiritual reality available to God's people. He continues,

“For he who has entered His rest has himself also ceased from his works as God did from His.”

Hebrews 4:10

This does not mean believers cease working.

It means they cease relying upon their own works to accomplish what only Christ can accomplish.

Religion continually says, "Do more." Grace continually says, "Christ has finished the work." Religion produces striving. Religion leaves believers wondering whether they have done enough. Grace reminds them that Jesus has done what they never could.

This is why the finished work of Christ is central to the Christian life.

When Jesus cried,

“It is finished,”

John 19:30

He declared that the work of redemption had been completed. The debt of sin had been paid. The way into the Father's presence had been opened. The believer's acceptance had been secured. Nothing can be added to Christ's perfect sacrifice.

Nothing needs to improve upon His righteousness. Nothing can strengthen what He has already completed.

God's sons therefore live from the finished work rather than striving to complete it.

This truth transforms every area of life.

The believer serves God, not to earn His love, but because he has already received it.

He obeys, not to become God's son, but because he already is.

He prays, not to persuade the Father to accept him, but because he has already been welcomed into His presence.

He worships, not to obtain God's favour, but in grateful response to grace already received.

Everything flows from rest. Psalm 23 beautifully illustrates this life. David begins,

“The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want.”

Psalm 23:1

Notice where confidence begins. Not with possessions. Not with circumstances. But with relationship.

"The Lord is my shepherd."

Because the Shepherd is sufficient, the sheep can rest.

David continues,

“He makes me to lie down in green pastures; He leads me beside the still waters. He restores my soul.”

Psalm 23:2–3

The Shepherd does not merely provide direction. He provides rest. The soul that continually follows Christ finds refreshment. It discovers quietness in His presence. It experiences restoration through fellowship with Him.

This is the atmosphere of the Fourth Dimension.

While the world continually strives for significance, security and acceptance, God's sons have already found these realities in Christ.

Therefore they labour from rest. This distinction is crucial. The believer still works diligently. He still studies. He still fulfils his responsibilities with excellence.

The apostle Paul himself laboured more abundantly than many others. Yet even Paul could say,

“Yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.”

1 Corinthians 15:10

His labour flowed from grace. Not from striving. Likewise, sons are called to excellence. But their excellence is not driven by fear. It is inspired by gratitude.

They work wholeheartedly because they represent their Father. They serve joyfully because Christ lives within them. They persevere because the Holy Spirit strengthens them. Everything flows from the security of sonship.

Remaining in God's rest therefore requires continual consciousness of our union with Christ.

The more we remember who we are in Him, the less we strive to establish ourselves.

The more we remember what He has accomplished, the less we depend upon our own efforts.

The more we walk with the Holy Spirit, the more His peace and confidence govern our lives.

Rest is therefore not a destination reached after life's struggles disappear. It is a present reality enjoyed in the midst of life's responsibilities. It is the settled confidence that Christ is sufficient.

As believers increasingly live from this place of rest, another remarkable transformation occurs.

The burdens that once produced anxiety gradually lose their power.

The believer learns to cast every care upon the Father because he knows he no longer has to carry life alone.

This naturally leads us to the next subject.

How do God's sons live above anxiety in a world filled with fear?

Living Above Anxiety

If thanksgiving is the language of faith and peace is the atmosphere of God's sons, then anxiety is one of the greatest enemies of both.

Anxiety is one of the most common experiences in the world today. People worry about their health, finances, careers, families, the future and countless other uncertainties. The world has become accustomed to anxiety, almost treating it as a normal part of life.

Yet Jesus presents an entirely different way of living. He does not merely tell His disciples not to worry. He reveals why God's sons need not live in anxiety. In His Sermon on the Mount, Jesus says,

“Therefore I say to you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or what you will drink; nor about your body, what you will put on.”

Matthew 6:25

This instruction is remarkable. Jesus is not denying that these needs exist. Neither is He teaching carelessness or irresponsibility.

He is teaching that anxiety should never become the governing atmosphere of God's sons.

He then asks,

“Is not life more than food and the body more than clothing?”

Matthew 6:25

Immediately, Jesus redirects His disciples from earthly concerns to the Father's care.

He points to the birds.

“Look at the birds of the air, for they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns; yet your heavenly Father feeds them.”

Matthew 6:26

Notice what Jesus wants His disciples to see. Not merely birds. The Father. The birds become evidence of the Father's continual care. Then Jesus asks,

“Are you not of more value than they?”

Matthew 6:26

This question lies at the heart of the believer's freedom from anxiety.

The issue is not whether life contains challenges.

The issue is whether God's sons truly believe that their Father cares for them.

Throughout His teaching, Jesus continually directs believers back to the Father's character.

He says,

“Therefore do not worry, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?'”

Matthew 6:31

Then He explains,

“For your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things.”

Matthew 6:32

The Father already knows. How similar this is to what Jesus taught concerning prayer. Your Father knows before you ask. Your Father provides. Everything returns to the Father's character.

Then Jesus gives one of the greatest priorities of the Christian life:

“But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.”

Matthew 6:33

Notice the order. Not anxiety first. Not provision first. The Kingdom first.

When God's sons live consciously from the realities of the Kingdom, the concerns of the Third Dimension lose their controlling influence.

This does not mean responsibilities disappear. It means they no longer dominate the heart. The believer lives from a higher reality. Peter teaches exactly the same truth. He writes,

“Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

What a beautiful invitation. Peter does not say, "Manage your anxieties." He says,

"Cast them."

The word suggests throwing a burden onto someone else.

Why?

Because God never intended His children to carry burdens that belong to Him.

Anxiety often reveals that we are attempting to carry tomorrow before tomorrow arrives.

We attempt to control outcomes that belong to the Father. We assume responsibilities that belong to His wisdom. We become occupied with questions only God can answer. The result is a heart weighed down with fear. Faith responds differently.

Faith says, "Father, this belongs to You." This is not passivity. Neither is it irresponsibility. The believer still obeys. He still works diligently. He still plans wisely.

But he no longer carries the burden alone. He entrusts it to the Father. Paul develops this practical lifestyle in Philippians.

“Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.”

Philippians 4:6

Notice that Paul does not merely command believers to stop worrying. He replaces anxiety with something better. Prayer. Supplication. Thanksgiving.

These become the believer's response to pressure.

Instead of rehearsing fearful thoughts, God's sons continually bring everything before the Father.

Instead of imagining the worst possible outcome, they remember the Father's goodness.

Instead of allowing fear to dominate the mind, they allow God's Word to renew it.

Immediately Paul adds,

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:7

This is God's divine exchange. The believer gives the Father his anxiety. What a wonderful exchange. The peace that follows is not produced by favourable circumstances. It is produced by continual fellowship with God.

Paul then gives one of the most practical instructions for maintaining peace.

“Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there is any virtue and if there is anything praiseworthy—meditate on these”

Philippians 4:8

...meditate on these things."

The mind is the doorway through which either peace or anxiety enters the heart.

If believers continually meditate upon fearful reports, negative imaginations and uncertain circumstances, anxiety gradually gains influence.

If, however, they continually fill their minds with God's truth, His promises and His faithfulness, the peace of Christ increasingly governs their lives.

This is why the renewing of the mind is essential to living from the Fourth Dimension.

A renewed mind becomes the environment in which peace flourishes.

The Holy Spirit continually works through the Word to remind believers of who they are in Christ.

He reminds us that we are sons. He reminds us that our Father is faithful. As these realities fill the heart, anxiety gradually loses its influence. This does not happen because life's challenges disappear.

It happens because the believer begins interpreting life from God's perspective rather than from the perspective of fear.

One of the greatest causes of anxiety is attempting to control what belongs to God.

Human nature constantly desires certainty about tomorrow. We want to know exactly how every situation will unfold. We want guarantees before we obey. We want visible evidence before we trust. But faith does not operate that way.

Faith trusts the Father's character even when tomorrow remains unseen. This is why Jesus concluded His teaching by saying,

“Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble.”

Matthew 6:34

Jesus is not teaching believers to ignore the future. The Father gives grace for today. Tomorrow's grace will arrive with tomorrow.

God's sons therefore learn to live one day at a time, continually depending upon the Father's daily provision.

This was beautifully illustrated when God provided manna for Israel in the wilderness.

They were instructed to gather enough for each day. When some attempted to store tomorrow's provision out of fear, it spoiled. God was teaching His people daily dependence. Likewise, Jesus taught His disciples to pray,

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11

Notice the emphasis.

This day.

The Father delights in daily fellowship with His children.

He desires that they continually depend upon Him rather than upon their own understanding.

Living above anxiety therefore is not simply the absence of worry. It is the continual practice of trusting the Father. It is remembering that Christ has already overcome the world. As believers increasingly live this way, they begin to discover something remarkable. Peace becomes more natural than fear.

Confidence becomes stronger than uncertainty. Hope becomes greater than discouragement.

The atmosphere of Heaven increasingly governs their hearts even while they continue living in a fallen world.

People around them begin to notice that they respond differently to pressure. They remain calm when others panic. They remain hopeful when others despair. They remain thankful when others complain. This difference cannot be explained merely by temperament.

It is the evidence of a son who is living from the Fourth Dimension.

Such a believer becomes a living testimony that the Kingdom of God is not merely a future reality.

It is already at work within those who belong to Christ.

As thanksgiving, peace and freedom from anxiety increasingly shape the believer's life, they combine to produce a distinctive atmosphere.

It is an atmosphere that reflects the very nature of the Kingdom of God.

This is the atmosphere God's sons are called to carry wherever they go.

That is the focus of our next section.

Renewing the Mind

We have seen that the mind is the doorway through which either peace or anxiety enters the heart. That raises a question worth pausing over, because the whole of the Christian life turns upon it. If our thinking governs so much, how is thinking itself changed?

The Apostle Paul answers directly:

“And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.”

Romans 12:2

Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say that God will renew our minds for us while we remain passive. Neither does he suggest that renewal happens automatically with the passing of time. The command is addressed to believers, and it assumes our participation.

Notice also the word conformed. It describes a mind that has been pressed into the mould of the surrounding world, shaped by its assumptions until it can no longer imagine anything higher. That pressure is constant. It comes through what we watch, what we read, what we listen to, and the endless commentary of a world that measures everything by what can be seen.

Transformation is the slow reversal of that pressure. The word Paul uses describes a change that begins on the inside and works outward. It is not behaviour modification. It is the reshaping of the way we think until our thinking increasingly agrees with the truth of who we already are in Christ.

This is why our identity changed the moment we believed, yet our thinking did not. God instantly gave us a new nature. He patiently transforms the way we think. Many believers grow discouraged at exactly this point, wondering why old fears and old patterns persist when Scripture declares them to be new creations. Nothing is wrong with them. Their spirit was changed in an instant; their mind is being renewed over a lifetime.

Paul says the same thing to the Ephesians, and adds a detail worth noticing:

“and be renewed in the spirit of your mind.”

Ephesians 4:23

The renewal happens in the spirit of the mind, that is, at the level of our deepest assumptions rather than merely our surface opinions. This is why information alone never transforms anyone. A believer may know a doctrine perfectly and still panic at the first bad report, because knowledge lives in the mind while assumption lives beneath it.

So how does the renewing take place? Through the Word of God, received not as information but as truth we submit to. Through meditation, which is simply the deliberate dwelling upon what God has said until it becomes more real to us than what we can see. Through the Holy Spirit, who takes the Word and applies it to the places where our thinking is still bent. And through obedience, because we rarely believe a truth deeply until we have acted upon it.

Isaiah captures the result in a single line:

“You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You.”

Isaiah 26:3

Perfect peace is not promised to those who face no trouble. It is promised to the one whose mind is stayed, fixed, settled upon God. Peace is not the absence of storm. It is the presence of a mind anchored to Someone who cannot be shaken.

Here, then, is the connection this chapter has been building toward. Thanksgiving, peace and rest are not moods we manufacture. They are the natural fruit of a mind increasingly filled with the truth about God. Change what fills the mind, and in time the atmosphere of the whole life changes with it.

Living in the Atmosphere of the Fourth Dimension

Every kingdom has an atmosphere.

When people enter a nation, they encounter its culture, values, language and way of life.

Likewise, the Kingdom of God has an atmosphere.

It is an atmosphere of righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.

Paul writes,

“For the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.”

Romans 14:17

Notice that these realities are not merely future blessings awaiting believers in eternity.

They are present realities experienced through the indwelling Holy Spirit.

The Kingdom has already entered the life of every believer through union with Christ.

Consequently, God's sons are called to carry the atmosphere of the Kingdom wherever they go.

Throughout this book we have discovered that the believer lives from the Fourth Dimension.

The unseen realities of God's Kingdom become the source from which his visible life flows.

As this consciousness grows, something remarkable begins to happen. Thanksgiving becomes natural. Peace becomes continual. Rest becomes habitual. Joy becomes steadfast.

Hope becomes unwavering. Confidence becomes settled. These are not personality traits. Neither are they emotional highs that come and go. They are the atmosphere produced by continual fellowship with Christ.

This is why the Holy Spirit plays such an essential role in the believer's daily life.

He continually reveals Christ. He continually renews our minds through the Word.

As we walk in fellowship with Him, the atmosphere of the Kingdom increasingly governs our hearts.

Earlier in this book we established that imagination enables believers to see beyond the limitations of the Third Dimension and perceive spiritual realities revealed by the Holy Spirit.

That same spiritual consciousness now influences the believer's daily life. Instead of imagining failure, he increasingly sees the Father's faithfulness. Instead of imagining defeat, he remembers Christ's victory. Instead of imagining abandonment, he remembers the Father's presence. His inner world becomes governed by the realities of God's Kingdom.

Eventually those unseen realities begin to influence his visible conduct. This is how transformation occurs. It begins within. Then it becomes visible without. Jesus taught,

“A good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good.”

Luke 6:45

Everything outward flows from what fills the heart.

If the heart is continually filled with fear, fear eventually appears in words, attitudes and decisions.

If the heart is continually filled with Christ, Christ increasingly becomes visible.

This is why Proverbs declares,

“Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

The believer's greatest responsibility is not merely controlling outward behaviour.

It is continually guarding the atmosphere of his heart.

As God's sons remain conscious of their union with Christ, they begin to carry His atmosphere into every sphere of life.

Their homes become places of peace. Their workplaces become places of integrity. Their friendships become places of encouragement. Their churches become places of hope. Their conversations become filled with grace.

People begin to sense something different. Not because believers are pretending to be positive. Not because they ignore life's difficulties. But because another Kingdom governs their lives. This was evident throughout the ministry of Jesus.

Everywhere He went, people encountered the atmosphere of the Father's Kingdom. Fear gave way to faith. Despair gave way to hope. Confusion gave way to truth. Bondage gave way to freedom.

The presence of Christ transformed the atmosphere around Him. The same Christ now lives within His sons through the Holy Spirit.

Therefore, wherever God's sons go, the atmosphere of Christ should increasingly become evident.

This does not mean believers never experience moments of weakness. Neither does it mean they never face sorrow or disappointment. Even Jesus wept. Paul spoke of being sorrowful, yet always rejoicing. The Christian life is not emotional denial.

Rather, it is the continual triumph of the realities of the Fourth Dimension over the changing circumstances of the Third.

Joy becomes greater than sorrow. Peace becomes greater than fear. Hope becomes greater than discouragement. Thanksgiving becomes greater than complaint. The atmosphere of the Kingdom increasingly governs every response.

This is why Paul repeatedly encourages believers to rejoice.

“Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice!”

Philippians 4:4

Notice where joy is found.

In the Lord.

Joy rooted in circumstances is temporary. Joy rooted in Christ remains. Likewise, peace rooted in favourable situations is fragile. Peace rooted in Christ is enduring. Rest rooted in success disappears when success fades.

Rest rooted in the finished work remains unshaken. The believer who continually lives from these realities becomes spiritually stable. His emotions no longer dictate his faith. His circumstances no longer determine his identity. His environment no longer governs his atmosphere.

Instead, the atmosphere of the Kingdom begins to influence his environment. This is one of the greatest evidences of spiritual maturity. Mature sons are not merely influenced by the world around them. They increasingly influence the world around them. They become carriers of God's presence.

Where there is fear, they bring peace. Not because of personal ability. But because Christ lives in them. This is the life to which every believer has been called. Not merely surviving life.

But revealing the atmosphere of the Father's Kingdom.

The more conscious believers become of their union with Christ, the more naturally this atmosphere flows from their lives.

Eventually, people begin to recognise that they have encountered something the world cannot produce.

They have encountered the life of Christ Himself.

This naturally brings us to the final truth of this chapter.

The atmosphere of God's Kingdom is never intended to remain a private experience.

It is meant to become a visible testimony to the world of the Father's presence through His sons.

Manifesting the Father's Presence

The truths we have explored throughout this chapter were never intended to remain private experiences.

God does not fill His sons with thanksgiving merely for their own benefit.

He does not establish them in peace merely so they may enjoy quiet hearts.

Neither does He bring them into His rest simply to make life easier.

Everything God accomplishes within His sons is ultimately intended to reveal His Son through them.

From the very beginning, God's purpose has been to make Himself known in the earth through His people.

Under the New Covenant, this purpose reaches its fullest expression because Christ now lives within every believer through the Holy Spirit.

The world therefore encounters Christ, not because He is physically walking the earth, but because His life is being manifested through His sons.

This is why Jesus declared,

“You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden.”

Matthew 5:14

Notice that Jesus did not say, "You will become the light." He said,

"You are the light."

Light is not something believers strive to produce. It is who they have become through union with Christ. The same Christ who declared,

“I am the light of the world.”

John 8:12

That light now shines through those who belong to Him.

Jesus continues,

“Nor do they light a lamp and put it under a basket, but on a lampstand, and it gives light to all who are in the house.”

Matthew 5:15

Light exists to be seen.

Likewise, the realities we have received in Christ are meant to become visible.

The world should see thanksgiving where it expects complaint. In seeing these realities, people catch a glimpse of the Father's Kingdom. Jesus concludes,

“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven.”

Matthew 5:16

Notice the purpose. Not that people would admire us. But that they would glorify the Father.

The atmosphere carried by God's sons should continually direct people's attention towards Him.

This is one of the greatest ministries every believer possesses.

Long before we preach a sermon, people experience the atmosphere we carry.

Long before we explain the Gospel, people observe how we respond to life's pressures.

They notice whether our words produce hope or fear. They notice whether gratitude or complaint fills our conversations. They notice whether peace governs our decisions. The atmosphere of our lives often speaks before our mouths do. Jesus also said,

“By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.”

John 13:35

Love, peace, thanksgiving and rest are all expressions of the life of Christ.

They become visible evidence that believers truly belong to Him. This is why the Christian life is never merely about avoiding sin. It is about revealing Christ.

The more conscious believers become of their union with Him, the more naturally His character becomes visible.

Paul gives another practical instruction.

He writes,

“Do all things without complaining and disputing.”

Philippians 2:14

Why?

“That you may become blameless and harmless, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world.”

Philippians 2:15

Notice the connection. Complaining hides the light. Thanksgiving reveals it.

When God's sons continually complain like the world around them, the distinctiveness of the Kingdom becomes difficult to see.

But when they remain thankful, peaceful and full of hope in difficult circumstances, they shine like lights in darkness.

This does not mean believers pretend that problems do not exist.

Rather, they continually demonstrate that the Father's presence is greater than those problems.

People begin asking, "How can you remain at peace?" "Why are you still thankful?" "Where does your hope come from?" The answer is always the same.

Christ.

He is our peace. He is our life. Everything flows from Him.

This is why remaining conscious of our union with Christ is so essential.

The Christian life was never designed to be lived independently.

Jesus declared,

“Without Me you can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Everything that has been discussed throughout this chapter flows from abiding in Him.

As believers continually fellowship with the Holy Spirit, meditate upon God's Word and remember the Father's faithfulness, they increasingly manifest the life of Christ.

The atmosphere of Heaven becomes visible on earth. Homes are transformed because sons carry peace into them. Workplaces are transformed because sons carry integrity and hope into them. Churches are strengthened because sons carry thanksgiving instead of complaint. Communities are influenced because sons reveal the Father's character through their conduct.

This is the practical outworking of living from the Fourth Dimension.

The unseen realities of God's Kingdom become visible through ordinary believers who remain conscious of their union with Christ.

Every conversation becomes an opportunity to reveal His grace. Every success becomes an opportunity to give Him thanks.

Gradually, the believer realises that the greatest testimony he carries is not merely what he says.

It is the atmosphere of Christ that continually surrounds his life.

Wherever he goes, people encounter something that cannot be produced by human effort.

They encounter the life of the Kingdom. This is the Father's purpose. Not merely that His sons would know His Kingdom. But that through them, His Kingdom would become visible to the world.

As believers remain conscious of their union with Christ, thanksgiving becomes their language, peace becomes their atmosphere, and rest becomes their way of life.

The invisible Kingdom increasingly becomes visible through ordinary men and women who have learned to live from the realities of the Fourth Dimension.

The atmosphere of God's Kingdom is already present within every son through the indwelling Holy Spirit. As believers remain conscious of their union with Christ, thanksgiving, peace and rest become the visible testimony of the invisible Kingdom wherever they go.

Chapter Summary

Throughout this chapter we have discovered that thanksgiving, peace and rest are not spiritual achievements believers struggle to attain. They are the natural atmosphere of God's sons who remain conscious of their union with Christ.

We began by learning that thanksgiving is the language of faith. God's sons do not merely give thanks after receiving answers to prayer. They continually thank the Father because they know His character. Thanksgiving magnifies God's goodness rather than present circumstances, and continually reminds believers of the grace they have already received through Christ.

We then discovered that Christ has given His own peace to His people. This peace is not dependent upon favourable circumstances but upon continual fellowship with Him. As believers renew their minds with God's Word and remain conscious of the Father's presence through the Holy Spirit, the peace of Christ increasingly governs their hearts and minds.

Next, we entered the rest of God. We learned that rest is not inactivity but confidence in the finished work of Christ. God's sons do not labour to earn acceptance; they labour from acceptance. Religion produces striving, but grace produces rest because Christ has already accomplished everything necessary for our redemption.

We paused to consider how thinking itself is changed. Our identity was transformed the moment we believed, but our thinking was not. God gives a new nature instantly and renews the mind patiently, through His Word, through meditation, through the Holy Spirit and through obedience, until our thinking increasingly agrees with the truth of who we already are in Christ. A mind stayed upon God is kept in perfect peace.

We also saw that anxiety loses its influence when believers continually cast their cares upon the Father. Rather than allowing fear to govern their thoughts, God's sons respond to life's pressures through prayer, thanksgiving, meditation on God's Word and continual dependence upon the Holy Spirit. As their minds remain fixed upon the realities of the Fourth Dimension, the peace of Christ guards their hearts.

From there we discovered that believers are called to live in the atmosphere of the Kingdom. Thanksgiving, peace, joy, confidence and rest are not merely inward experiences; they become the atmosphere surrounding sons who remain conscious of their union with Christ. Wherever they go, they increasingly reveal the culture of God's Kingdom.

Finally, we learned that this atmosphere is not for personal enjoyment alone. God's sons are called to manifest the Father's presence in the world. Their thanksgiving glorifies the Father. Their peace points people to Christ. Their rest demonstrates confidence in the finished work. Their lives become visible testimonies of the invisible Kingdom.

The believer who lives consciously from the Fourth Dimension therefore carries Heaven's atmosphere into the Third Dimension.

Thanksgiving becomes his language. Peace becomes his atmosphere. Rest becomes his way of life. And Christ becomes increasingly visible through him.

Reflection Questions

  1. Is thanksgiving my continual response to the Father's goodness, or do I only give thanks when circumstances are favourable?
  1. What has been determining my peace recently—the circumstances around me or my consciousness of my union with Christ?
  1. Am I serving God from the rest of Christ's finished work, or am I still striving to earn what He has already freely given me?
  1. When anxiety arises, do I respond with prayer, thanksgiving and dependence upon the Father, or do I allow fear to govern my thinking?
  1. Does the atmosphere of my life consistently reflect the realities of God's Kingdom so that others encounter the peace, joy and hope of Christ through me?
