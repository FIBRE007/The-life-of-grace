Living Confidently in God's Timing

Introduction

In the previous chapter we discovered that faith is the language of God’s Kingdom—the settled response of a son who trusts his Father even when the visible world contradicts what God has spoken. Yet faith raises a further question, and it is one every believer eventually faces. What happens when we have believed, prayed and obeyed, and still nothing appears to change?

One of the greatest tests of faith is not whether God is able, but whether we will continue trusting Him when His promises are not yet visible.

Every believer who has walked with God for any length of time has experienced moments when the visible world appears inconsistent with what God has spoken. We pray, believe, confess His promises, and yet circumstances sometimes seem unchanged. During such seasons, questions naturally arise. Has God heard me? Is He still working? Why has nothing changed? Have I missed His will? Has my faith failed?

These are not new questions. They did not begin with believers today. The first generation of the Church also experienced suffering, persecution, prolonged trials and circumstances that seemed difficult to reconcile with God's promises. Some were imprisoned. Others were martyred. Many endured hardship while waiting for the fulfilment of God's promises. Yet when we read the New Testament Epistles, something remarkable stands out.

The apostles never built a theology around unanswered prayer. They never encouraged believers to lower their expectations of God. They never taught that God's children should approach prayer with uncertainty. Instead, they continually directed believers back to four unchanging realities: the Father's character; the finished work of Christ; the certainty of God's promises; and the ministry of the Holy Spirit.

Their concern was never merely to explain difficult experiences. Their concern was to preserve the confidence of God's sons.

This is exactly what this chapter seeks to do.

This chapter is not an attempt to explain every circumstance that believers may experience. There are mysteries that belong to God's infinite wisdom. Rather, our purpose is to discover how God's sons should live while His purposes unfold within history.

As we discovered in previous chapters, the believer lives from the Fourth Dimension—the unseen reality of God's Kingdom—while walking through the visible world. The Father's purposes are already established in His eternal counsel. The believer therefore learns to interpret life, not merely by what is visible, but by what God has spoken.

This perspective changes everything.

Instead of allowing circumstances to determine our confidence, we allow the Father's character to determine our confidence.

Instead of asking whether God has forgotten His promise, we remember that He cannot lie.

Instead of becoming discouraged by what we do not yet see, we become established in what Christ has already accomplished.

Faith therefore is not suspended until manifestation appears.

Faith continues because the Father remains faithful.

By the end of this chapter, my prayer is that you will understand why the apostles consistently encouraged believers to continue praying, continue believing, continue rejoicing, continue thanking God, continue asking, and continue holding fast their confidence. They understood something every son of God must also learn:

The Father's faithfulness never changes because circumstances change. Our confidence is not built upon visible evidence.

God Is Lord Over Time

To understand why God's sons can remain confident regardless of circumstances, we must first understand God's relationship with time.

Unlike man, God is not confined by time. Time belongs to creation. God does not. He existed before time began. He remains the same throughout all generations.

He sees every moment of history with perfect clarity because He is the Creator of history itself.

Through the prophet Isaiah, God declares:

“Remember the former things of old, for I am God, and there is no other; I am God, and there is none like Me, declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times things that are not yet done, saying, 'My counsel shall stand, and I will do all My pleasure.'”

Isaiah 46:9–10

What an astonishing declaration.

Only God can announce the end before the beginning because nothing lies outside His knowledge. Human beings discover life one day at a time. God sees the completed picture while we are still living one chapter of the story.

This truth should never intimidate the believer.

It should produce confidence.

If God already knows the end, then nothing in our lives can surprise Him.

No difficulty catches Him unprepared. No circumstance forces Him to revise His plans. No opposition can overthrow His purpose. Everything remains under His sovereign authority. Solomon also reminds us:

“To everything there is a season, a time for every purpose under heaven.”

Ecclesiastes 3:1

Notice that Scripture connects time with purpose. History is not random. Neither is your life. God governs history according to purpose. Every season has meaning.

Every stage of His work serves His eternal wisdom. Paul demonstrates this beautifully when describing the coming of Jesus Christ:

“But when the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law.”

Galatians 4:4

Christ did not come too early. Neither did He arrive too late. He came at the precise moment appointed by the Father. For centuries Israel waited for the promised Messiah. Prophets announced His coming.

Generations hoped. Many died without seeing His arrival. Yet God's promise never failed.

When the appointed time arrived, the Father fulfilled exactly what He had promised.

This reveals an important principle. God's timing is never uncertainty. It is the perfect expression of His wisdom. Peter reinforces this truth:

“But, beloved, do not forget this one thing, that with the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.”

2 Peter 3:8

Peter is not teaching us to calculate prophetic dates.

Rather, he reminds us that God does not experience time as we do.

We often become impatient because we measure God's activity by clocks and calendars.

God measures everything by His eternal wisdom. For the believer, this truth brings tremendous comfort. If God governs time, then time can never work against His purposes. The passing of days does not weaken His promises. The passing of years does not diminish His power.

The passing of decades does not erase His covenant. Time changes us. It never changes God.

This perspective becomes even more meaningful when viewed through the lens of the Fourth Dimension.

Throughout this book we have discovered that the unseen Kingdom of God is the ultimate reality.

The visible world does not govern God's Kingdom.

Rather, God's Kingdom governs the visible world.

God's purposes originate in the Fourth Dimension before they become visible in the Third.

Consequently, God's sons refuse to judge reality merely by what they presently see.

They remember that the Father is continually accomplishing His purposes, even when the visible world has not yet revealed their full manifestation.

This is why confidence is so important. Confidence is not pretending that difficulties do not exist. Confidence is knowing that difficulties do not determine the outcome. The Father does.

When believers lose sight of God's sovereignty over time, they often become discouraged by what appears to be slow progress.

They begin counting days instead of remembering God's faithfulness. They compare their lives with others. They become anxious because visible circumstances seem unchanged. But sons who understand their Father's relationship with time think differently. They know that God's purposes are never threatened by time.

Therefore, they refuse to allow time to become the measure of God's faithfulness.

Instead, they measure God's faithfulness by His character.

The Father's promises remain just as certain today as the day He first spoke them.

His love remains unchanged. His wisdom remains perfect. His power remains unlimited. His covenant remains secure.

Because of this, God's sons continue walking confidently, knowing that the One who declared the end from the beginning is faithfully accomplishing His purposes.

Yet an important question remains.

If God governs time perfectly, why do believers sometimes experience a difference between God's promise and its visible manifestation?

How should God's sons understand this without allowing their confidence in the Father to weaken?

That is the question we will answer next.

Understanding the Difference Between God's Answer and Its Manifestation

One of the greatest causes of discouragement in the Christian life is the tendency to equate God's answer with its immediate manifestation.

Many believers unconsciously assume that if they cannot yet see what they have prayed for, then perhaps God has not heard them. Others begin to wonder whether they have not prayed long enough, believed strongly enough, or somehow failed to meet God's expectations. Gradually, their confidence begins to shift from the Father's character to their circumstances.

This is precisely where the Scriptures bring light.

One of the clearest examples is found in the life of Daniel.

Daniel had been fasting and praying for understanding concerning the vision God had shown him. For twenty-one days there was no visible answer. From Daniel's perspective, it appeared as though nothing had happened. Then an angel appeared and made one of the most encouraging statements recorded in Scripture:

“Do not fear, Daniel, for from the first day that you set your heart to understand, and to humble yourself before your God, your words were heard; and I have come because of your words.”

Daniel 10:12

Notice what the angel emphasised first. Not the conflict. Not the delay. Not the opposition. The first truth Daniel needed to hear was this: God heard you from the first day.

That statement alone should strengthen every believer. God did not hear Daniel on the twenty-first day. The Father's willingness never changed. His attention never shifted. His purpose never became uncertain.

The angel then explained what had taken place in the unseen realm:

“But the prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me twenty-one days...”

Daniel 10:13

This verse has often caused believers to become fearful, imagining that every prayer they offer may be delayed by spiritual forces. Yet that is not the emphasis of the passage.

The emphasis is God's faithfulness. Daniel 10 was never written to make believers question whether God hears. It was written to assure us that He does. The conflict did not prevent God from hearing Daniel. It did not alter God's decision.

It did not cause God to reconsider His answer.

It simply revealed that God's purposes unfold within a world where spiritual conflict exists.

Earlier in this book, we established that the Fourth Dimension is real. God's Kingdom is real, but Scripture also teaches that there are spiritual forces of darkness actively opposing God's purposes. Nevertheless, those forces never possess ultimate authority.

Christ has already triumphed over principalities and powers.

He is seated far above all rule and authority.

Therefore, spiritual opposition should never become greater in our thinking than God's sovereignty.

Daniel's confidence was not meant to rest upon understanding every detail of spiritual warfare.

It was meant to rest upon one unchanging fact: God heard him immediately. That is the truth every son of God should carry into prayer. The Father hears His children. This principle is consistent throughout the New Testament. John writes:

“Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us.”

1 John 5:14

John does not write, "Perhaps He hears us." Nor does he say, "If enough time passes, He may eventually hear us." He says,

“He hears us.”

1 John 5:14

That confidence becomes the foundation of the believer's prayer life.

Jesus Himself taught:

“Your Father knows the things you have need of before you ask Him.”

Matthew 6:8

Our prayers do not inform God. Neither do they persuade Him to become compassionate. The Father already knows. The Father already invites His sons to come boldly into His presence.

Understanding this changes the way we interpret seasons in which God's purposes have not yet become visible.

Instead of asking, "Has God heard me?" Faith asks, "Since my Father has heard me, how should I continue living?" That is Fourth Dimension thinking.

It shifts the focus away from uncertainty and places it upon the Father's faithfulness.

This distinction becomes especially important when we consider the lives of other believers in Scripture.

Hannah prayed for a son year after year, weeping before the Lord while her circumstances remained unchanged.

In the process of time, Samuel was born.

The certainty of God's purpose never depended upon how long she waited.

Simeon was told by the Holy Spirit that he would not die before he had seen the Lord's Christ.

He waited through the long silence of his old age, coming daily to the temple.

Yet God's word to him did not fail. Joseph received prophetic dreams long before they became reality. Neither slavery nor imprisonment cancelled what God had spoken. History was simply moving toward the manifestation of God's purpose.

These accounts teach us that God's promises are not validated by how quickly they become visible.

They are validated by the character of the God who made them.

For this reason, God's sons must resist the temptation to interpret His faithfulness through the lens of time.

The passing of time proves nothing about God's willingness. God's character remains unchanged.

This is why the apostles continually directed believers back to the Father rather than to their circumstances.

When they wrote to churches enduring persecution, suffering and prolonged trials, they did not encourage believers to question whether God had heard them.

Instead, they strengthened their confidence in the Father's love, Christ's finished work, God's promises and the ministry of the Holy Spirit.

That observation is profoundly important.

The New Testament never encourages God's sons to interpret apparent delays as evidence that God has become reluctant.

Instead, it continually teaches believers to remain confident in the Father while His purposes unfold according to His perfect wisdom.

Having established this foundation, we are now ready to address the passages that many believers find most difficult—those Scriptures that appear to describe unanswered prayers, hindered prayers or requests that were not granted as expected. Rather than weakening our confidence, we shall discover that they reveal even more clearly the faithfulness, wisdom and love of our heavenly Father.

Difficult Scriptures That Strengthen Our Confidence

Every believer eventually encounters passages of Scripture that seem difficult to reconcile with the confidence that Jesus and the apostles encourage us to have in prayer. Some have concluded from these passages that God is often unwilling to answer, that prayer is uncertain, or that believers should lower their expectations when approaching God.

However, when we examine these passages carefully, a different picture emerges.

The issue of prayers that appear unanswered, hindered or differently answered did not begin with believers today. The first generation of the Church also lived with questions. They experienced persecution, suffering, imprisonment, unanswered expectations and prolonged trials. Yet when the apostles addressed these believers in their epistles, they never built a theology around unanswered prayer. Instead, they strengthened the Church by directing believers back to God's character, His covenant, Christ's finished work and the ministry of the Holy Spirit.

Rather than asking, "Why didn't God answer?" they consistently asked, "How should God's sons continue living?" That is a significant difference.

The apostles understood that confidence in God is far more important than satisfying human curiosity.

Let us therefore examine these passages from the perspective of the New Covenant.

Moses and the Promised Land

Near the end of his life, Moses pleaded with God:

“O Lord God, You have begun to show Your servant Your greatness and Your mighty hand... I pray, let me cross over and see the good land beyond the Jordan...”

Deuteronomy 3:24

God refused his request.

At first glance, this appears discouraging.

However, this event occurred under the Old Covenant after God had already declared the consequence of Moses' disobedience at the waters of Meribah.

God was not ignoring Moses. Neither was He becoming unfaithful. He remained perfectly consistent with His own righteousness.

More importantly, Moses' experience cannot become the pattern by which New Covenant sons understand prayer, because we now approach the Father through the finished work of Christ.

This passage therefore teaches us God's holiness, not His unwillingness to hear His children.

David and His Child David fasted and prayed that the child born through Bathsheba might live. The child died.

Again, this event took place after God had already announced His judgment through the prophet Nathan.

David was not praying from the position of a New Covenant believer who had been united with Christ.

He was living under the Mosaic Covenant after a prophetic judgment had already been declared.

Remarkably, once the child died, David arose, worshipped the Lord and accepted God's righteousness.

Even here, the emphasis is God's justice and David's submission—not uncertainty concerning God's character.

Paul's Thorn

Perhaps no passage has generated more discussion than Paul's thorn in the flesh.

Paul writes:

“Concerning this thing I pleaded with the Lord three times that it might depart from me.”

2 Corinthians 12:8

Many stop there.

But notice what happened next.

“And He said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.'”

2 Corinthians 12:9

God answered. Paul did not receive silence. Neither was his prayer ignored.

The answer differed from Paul's request, but it was perfectly suited to God's purpose for his life.

Instead of becoming discouraged, Paul reached a remarkable conclusion:

“Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Paul's confidence actually increased because he came to know the sufficiency of God's grace.

The lesson is not that God refuses to answer prayer.

The lesson is that God's wisdom always accomplishes something greater than our limited understanding.

Jesus in Gethsemane

No prayer is more sacred than our Lord's prayer before the Cross.

“O My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will.”

Matthew 26:39

Some have used this prayer to suggest that believers should expect God to refuse their requests.

But this prayer occupies a unique place in the history of redemption. Jesus was not simply asking for personal deliverance. He stood at the centre of God's eternal purpose to redeem humanity. Had the cup passed from Him, there would have been no Cross. Without forgiveness, there would be no sonship.

Without sonship, there would be no confidence in prayer.

Far from weakening our confidence, Gethsemane becomes the very reason we now approach the Father boldly.

Christ willingly embraced the Father's will so that we might become God's sons.

The Cry of the Martyrs

In Revelation, John sees the souls of those who had been martyred for the testimony of Jesus.

They cry,

“How long, O Lord, holy and true, until You judge and avenge our blood...?”

Revelation 6:10

God does not ignore them.

Neither does He deny their request.

Instead, they are given white robes and told to rest a little while longer until God's redemptive plan reaches its appointed fulfilment.

Their prayer is acknowledged. Their request is certain. Its fulfilment belongs to God's perfect plan for history.

The passage therefore strengthens our confidence in God's justice rather than weakening our confidence in prayer.

Saul's Silence

There is one passage that explicitly states God did not answer.

“And when Saul inquired of the Lord, the Lord did not answer him, either by dreams or by Urim or by the prophets.”

1 Samuel 28:6

This is an extraordinary passage precisely because it is exceptional. Saul had persistently rejected God's word. He had repeatedly resisted correction. He had hardened his heart over many years. His experience was one of divine judgment, not covenant fellowship.

It must never become the pattern by which New Covenant sons interpret prayer.

We approach the Father through Christ. Saul did not. His silence belongs to a completely different covenantal context. What Do the New Testament Epistles Actually Teach? Having considered these passages, we must now ask an important question.

When the apostles addressed the Church, what reasons did they actually give for hindered prayers or requests that were not received?

Surprisingly, they mention very few.

James writes:

“You ask and do not receive, because you ask amiss, that you may spend it on your pleasures.”

James 4:3

The problem was not God's unwillingness. The problem was selfish motives. Prayer was never intended to satisfy the desires of the flesh. It is the privilege of sons who seek the Father's will. James also teaches believers who lack wisdom:

“But let him ask in faith, with no doubting...”

James 1:6

The context is asking for wisdom during trials. James encourages wholehearted trust in God's generous character. Peter writes to husbands:

“...giving honour to the wife... that your prayers may not be hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

Here the issue is broken relationships.

The Father desires His sons to reflect His own character within their homes.

Jesus teaches:

“And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive...”

Mark 11:25

Unforgiveness is inconsistent with the Father's forgiving heart.

John then brings us to one of the greatest truths concerning prayer:

“Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us.”

1 John 5:14

Notice John's emphasis. Not uncertainty. Confidence. Not guessing. Knowing.

Not perhaps. He hears us. John's concern is not to make believers question prayer. His concern is to establish them in confidence before the Father.

Throughout the New Testament, the apostles repeatedly direct believers toward the same conclusion.

Pray. Continue praying. Pray without ceasing. Pray with thanksgiving. Cast your cares upon God.

Do not cast away your confidence. If any of you lacks wisdom, ask. Approach the throne of grace boldly.

Nowhere do they encourage God's sons to lower their expectations because of difficult experiences.

Instead, they continually strengthen believers by pointing them to the Father's love, Christ's finished work, God's promises and the Holy Spirit's continual help.

This is perhaps one of the greatest lessons the New Testament teaches concerning prayer.

The apostles never strengthened the Church by explaining every unanswered request. They strengthened the Church by continually directing believers back to the Father.

Having removed these misconceptions, we are now ready to answer one of the most practical questions in the Christian life:

What should every son of God confidently expect when he prays?

What Should God's Sons Expect When They Pray?

Having removed many of the misconceptions surrounding prayer, we now arrive at one of the most important questions in this chapter.

What should God's sons actually expect when they pray?

The answer must not be built upon human experience.

Neither should it be formed by isolated passages of Scripture.

Our expectations must be shaped by the clear teaching of Jesus Christ and the consistent doctrine of the New Testament.

One of the greatest tragedies in the Church today is that many believers approach prayer with uncertainty rather than confidence. Instead of approaching their Father with joyful expectation, they wonder whether He is willing to answer. Some even wonder whether He has heard them at all.

Yet this uncertainty is completely foreign to the New Testament.

The apostles consistently encouraged believers to approach God with confidence because of who the Father is and because of what Christ has accomplished.

Prayer is therefore not an attempt to persuade a reluctant God.

Prayer is the joyful privilege of sons who have already been accepted in Christ.

Sons Pray from Acceptance

One of the greatest truths revealed under the New Covenant is that believers do not pray for acceptance.

They pray from acceptance. This distinction changes everything. Under religion, people attempt to gain God's favour.

Under grace, God's sons approach the Father because they have already received His favour in Christ.

The writer of Hebrews declares,

“Seeing then that we have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession.”

Hebrews 4:14

He immediately adds,

“Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Notice why believers come boldly. Not because they have lived perfectly. Not because they have prayed enough. Not because they possess extraordinary faith. They come boldly because Jesus Christ has become their great High Priest.

Our confidence rests entirely upon Him.

Every prayer offered by God's sons is founded upon the finished work of Christ.

The Father Himself Loves You

Perhaps one of the most beautiful statements Jesus ever made concerning prayer is found in John's Gospel.

He says,

“For the Father Himself loves you, because you have loved Me, and have believed that I came forth from God.”

John 16:27

These words should transform the believer's prayer life.

Many Christians unconsciously think they must convince God to become favourable towards them.

Jesus removes that fear completely. He does not merely say that God loves humanity in general. He says,

"The Father Himself loves you."

Prayer therefore begins, not with uncertainty, but with love. The believer kneels before a Father who delights in His children. Everything Christ accomplished was intended to bring us into this relationship. We are not attempting to gain the Father's attention. We already possess it.

We are not attempting to persuade Him to care. He already does. Prayer therefore becomes the joyful fellowship of sons with their Father.

Ask, Seek and Knock

Jesus taught,

“Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you.”

Matthew 7:7

The original language carries the idea of continual action. Keep asking. Keep seeking. Keep knocking.

Unfortunately, many have interpreted this to mean that believers must continually persuade God until He eventually decides to answer.

That is not Jesus' point.

Immediately after giving this instruction, Jesus explains why believers should continue asking.

“For everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks finds, and to him who knocks it will be opened.”

Matthew 7:8

Then He illustrates His point by comparing earthly fathers with our heavenly Father.

“If a son asks for bread from any father among you, will he give him a stone?”

Luke 11:11

Finally He concludes,

“If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give good things to those who ask Him!”

Matthew 7:11

The emphasis is not God's reluctance. The emphasis is the Father's goodness. God's sons continue asking because they know their Father is good. Persistent prayer is therefore not repeated persuasion. It is continual dependence.

It is continual fellowship. It is continual expectation. Every time the believer prays, he declares, "Father, I trust You."

Asking According to God's Will

John writes,

“Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us.”

1 John 5:14

Some believers become discouraged by the phrase, "according to His will." Yet John intends exactly the opposite. He begins with the word,

confidence.

Throughout his epistle, God's will is closely connected with abiding in Christ, believing in His Son and walking in His truth.

As believers grow in fellowship with Christ, their desires are increasingly shaped by God's Word.

Consequently, their prayers increasingly reflect the Father's own heart.

Praying according to God's will is therefore not a restriction designed to weaken confidence.

It is a privilege that strengthens confidence because God's sons increasingly know the Father's heart.

John continues,

“And if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we have asked of Him.”

1 John 5:15

Notice John's progression. We ask. God hears. Therefore we know. There is no uncertainty.

There is no fear. The Father's hearing becomes the foundation of the believer's confidence.

Continue Asking

The New Testament repeatedly encourages believers to continue praying.

Paul writes,

“Continue earnestly in prayer, being vigilant in it with thanksgiving.”

Colossians 4:2

Again he says,

“Pray without ceasing.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17

He also instructs believers,

“In everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.”

Philippians 4:6

Notice what Paul never says. He never says, "Continue praying because perhaps God has not heard you." Neither does he say, "Continue praying because you must eventually persuade God." Prayer is not an attempt to overcome divine reluctance. Prayer is the continual expression of dependence upon the Father.

It is the lifestyle of sons who remain conscious of their union with Christ.

Every prayer deepens fellowship. Every prayer strengthens dependence. Every prayer declares confidence in the Father's faithfulness.

Even when the visible manifestation has not yet appeared, God's sons continue praying because prayer is the language of relationship.

The New Testament Pattern

When we bring together the teaching of Jesus and the apostles, a consistent pattern emerges.

God's sons should expect:

  • that the Father loves them;
  • that the Father hears them;
  • that they are welcomed into His presence;
  • that they may ask boldly because of Christ;
  • that the Holy Spirit helps them in their weakness;
  • that God's promises in Christ are certain;
  • and that prayer is a continual expression of fellowship rather than an attempt to persuade God.

This is why the New Testament consistently encourages believers to continue asking. Not because God is reluctant. But because sons delight in continual communion with their Father.

The believer who understands this no longer measures prayer merely by visible outcomes.

He understands that every prayer is an act of worship, faith, dependence and fellowship with the Father.

From that place of confidence, God's sons continue living joyfully between the promise and its visible manifestation, knowing that the Father who hears them is also faithfully accomplishing His purposes.

Living Confidently Between the Promise and the Manifestation

Having established what God's sons should expect when they pray, an important question still remains.

How should believers live after they have prayed but before what they have believed God for becomes visible?

This is where many believers either grow stronger in faith or gradually become discouraged.

Some continue looking to the Father. Others begin looking at circumstances. Some continue giving thanks. Others begin questioning God's faithfulness.

The New Testament consistently encourages believers to remain steadfast in their confidence because faith is not measured by what is seen but by the certainty of God's character.

This principle can be seen throughout the Scriptures.

Abraham received God's promise concerning Isaac long before he held him in his arms. During those years, Abraham had every natural reason to become discouraged. His body grew older. Sarah's womb remained barren. Everything visible appeared to contradict God's promise.

Yet Scripture says,

“He did not waver at the promise of God through unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, and being fully convinced that what He had promised He was also able to perform.”

Romans 4:20

Notice that Abraham's confidence rested upon God's ability, not upon visible evidence. Time did not weaken God's promise. Neither did it weaken Abraham's confidence. Instead, he continued giving glory to God.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations that thanksgiving is an expression of faith, a truth we shall develop more fully in the next chapter.

Joseph provides another remarkable example.

God revealed through dreams that Joseph would occupy a position of leadership. Yet before those dreams were fulfilled, Joseph experienced betrayal, slavery, false accusation and imprisonment.

Had Joseph interpreted God's faithfulness through his circumstances, he would have concluded that God's promise had failed.

Instead, the Scriptures repeatedly declare,

“The Lord was with Joseph.”

Genesis 39:2

That statement is profoundly significant.

God's presence was the evidence of His faithfulness long

The Certainty of the Father's Faithfulness

As we come to the close of this chapter, one truth shines above every other:

The confidence of God's sons is rooted in the unchanging faithfulness of their Father.

Throughout this chapter we have considered God's relationship with time, the distinction between His answer and its manifestation, difficult passages concerning prayer, and the consistent teaching of the New Testament concerning confident prayer.

All these truths lead to one conclusion.

God is faithful.

Not occasionally. Not when circumstances appear favourable. Not only when we understand His ways. He is faithful because faithfulness is His nature.

The believer's confidence therefore does not rest upon the strength of his own faith.

It rests upon the character of the One in whom he believes.

This is why Paul writes,

“For all the promises of God in Him are Yes, and in Him Amen, to the glory of God through us.”

2 Corinthians 1:20

What an extraordinary declaration. Paul does not say that some promises are "Yes." Neither does he suggest that God's promises have become uncertain. He points believers to Christ. Everything God promised concerning redemption has found its fulfilment in His Son.

Therefore, every promise that belongs to those who are in Christ belongs to God's sons today.

This does not mean every promise in Scripture may be removed from its covenantal context and applied indiscriminately.

Rather, it means that every promise fulfilled and secured through Jesus Christ belongs to every believer united with Him.

Our forgiveness. Our acceptance. Our sonship. Our righteousness. Our access to the Father.

The gift of the Holy Spirit. Our inheritance. Our eternal life. Our authority in Christ. Our peace.

Our hope. These are not future possibilities. They are present realities.

Earlier in this book we learned that believers have already been blessed with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ.

We have already become partakers of the divine nature. We have already received all things that pertain to life and godliness. We have already become heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ.

Because these realities are already ours in the Fourth Dimension, God's sons do not live as though they are trying to persuade the Father to bless them.

They live from what the Father has already accomplished through Christ. This changes the believer's entire approach to prayer. Prayer ceases to become an attempt to convince God. Instead, it becomes joyful fellowship with the Father. It becomes agreement with His Word.

It becomes participation in His purposes. It becomes the continual expression of sonship. This is why the apostles repeatedly encouraged believers to continue praying. Paul says,

“Pray without ceasing.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17

He also writes,

“Continue earnestly in prayer, being vigilant in it with thanksgiving.”

Colossians 4:2

The writer of Hebrews says,

“Let us hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering, for He who promised is faithful.”

Hebrews 10:23

Peter says,

“Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

John says,

“This is the confidence that we have in Him...”

1 John 5:14

Notice the remarkable consistency.

Although the apostles wrote to churches facing persecution, hardship, imprisonment and suffering, none of them encouraged believers to lower their expectations of God.

None of them taught that difficult experiences should weaken confidence in prayer.

Instead, every one of them pointed believers back to the same unchanging realities:

The Father is faithful. Christ has finished the work. The Holy Spirit helps us. God's promises remain certain. Hold fast your confidence.

Continue praying. Continue believing. Continue thanking God. Continue trusting.

This is perhaps one of the greatest lessons the New Testament teaches concerning prayer.

The apostles never strengthened believers by explaining every unanswered request.

They strengthened believers by continually directing them back to the Father.

This observation should profoundly shape the way we respond to our own circumstances.

There will always be questions we cannot answer.

There will be seasons in which we do not understand everything God is doing.

There will be situations where our knowledge remains incomplete. Yet none of these things changes the Father's character. His love remains constant. His wisdom remains perfect. His promises remain sure.

His covenant remains secure. His purpose remains unchanged. His presence remains with His sons. This is why confidence is possible. Not because we possess every answer.

But because we know the One who does. Paul expressed this beautifully when he wrote,

“For I know whom I have believed and am persuaded that He is able to keep what I have committed to Him until that Day.”

2 Timothy 1:12

Notice Paul's confidence. He does not say, "I know everything God is doing." He says,

"I know whom I have believed."

His confidence was relational. He knew the Father. He knew Christ. He knew the faithfulness of God. The same confidence belongs to every son today.

The believer who lives from the Fourth Dimension therefore refuses to interpret life merely through visible circumstances.

He continually interprets circumstances through the Father's character. When circumstances change... God has not changed. When emotions fluctuate... God has not changed.

When understanding is incomplete... God has not changed. When the visible manifestation has not yet appeared... God has not changed. This is the certainty upon which sons build their lives.

Their confidence is not optimism. It is not positive thinking. It is not denial of reality.

It is the settled assurance that the Father who revealed Himself in Jesus Christ is perfectly faithful.

Therefore God's sons continue praying boldly. They continue asking confidently. They continue expecting confidently. Not because they understand every circumstance, but because they know their Father. This is what it means to live confidently in God's timing.

It is living every day from the certainty that the Father who called us, redeemed us, adopted us and united us with Christ can never fail to accomplish every purpose He has spoken concerning His children.

As God's sons become established in this truth, fear gradually loses its influence.

Anxiety gives way to peace. Striving gives way to rest. Complaining gives way to thanksgiving. Uncertainty gives way to confidence.

And confidence gives birth to a life marked by continual worship, joyful expectation and unwavering trust in the Father's unfailing love.

Faith does not become weaker because time passes. Faith becomes stronger because it continually remembers who the Father is.

Chapter Summary

In this chapter we have discovered that God's sons are called to live confidently, not because every circumstance is immediately understood, but because the Father is perfectly faithful.

We began by seeing that God is Lord over time. Unlike man, He is not governed by time but rules over it according to His eternal wisdom. Every act of redemption has unfolded at His appointed time, and every purpose He has spoken concerning His children remains under His sovereign care.

We then distinguished between God's answer and the manifestation of His purpose. Daniel's experience teaches us that God heard him from the very first day. God's faithfulness was never in question. His purpose simply unfolded within history while spiritual conflict took place. This teaches believers never to mistake the absence of visible manifestation for the absence of God's activity.

We also examined several passages that have often weakened believers' confidence in prayer. Rather than building a theology around unanswered prayer, we discovered that the New Testament writers consistently strengthen believers' confidence in the Father. When they discuss hindered prayers, they point to selfish motives, divided hearts, broken relationships, unforgiveness or praying outside God's revealed will—not to a reluctant God. Even Paul's thorn was not met with silence but with a gracious and personal answer from the Lord.

From there we discovered what God's sons should expect when they pray. Because of the finished work of Christ, believers approach the Father boldly. They pray from acceptance rather than for acceptance. They continue asking, not because the Father is reluctant, but because prayer is the continual expression of dependence, fellowship and confidence. God's sons know that the Father loves them, hears them and faithfully accomplishes His purposes.

Finally, we learned how to live confidently between the promise and its manifestation. Like Hannah, Joseph, Simeon and the early Church, believers continue praying, believing, worshipping, serving and giving thanks because their confidence rests in God's unchanging character rather than in changing circumstances. The apostles consistently strengthened suffering believers by directing them back to the Father's love, Christ's finished work, the certainty of God's promises and the ministry of the Holy Spirit.

The life of faith is therefore not merely believing that God is able.

It is confidently knowing that the Father is faithful.

Reflection Questions

  1. When I pray, is my confidence rooted in the Father's character or in how quickly I see visible results?
  1. Have difficult experiences or misunderstood Scriptures weakened my confidence in prayer, and how has this chapter corrected my understanding?
  1. Do I approach God as a son who knows he is loved and accepted through Christ, or do I still find myself trying to earn the Father's favour?
  1. Which New Testament truth most strengthens my confidence in prayer: the Father's love, Christ's finished work, God's promises or the Holy Spirit's help? How can I live more consciously from that reality?
  1. What practical changes will I make so that my life increasingly reflects confidence in the Father's faithfulness while His purposes continue to unfold?

Prayer

Father,

Thank You because You are faithful in all Your ways. Thank You that You never change, Your promises never fail, and Your love for Your children is constant. Thank You for sending Your Son, Jesus Christ, through whom I have been forgiven, accepted, justified and made Your son.

Thank You that I can approach You boldly, not because of my own righteousness, but because of the finished work of Christ. Thank You that You hear me whenever I come to You according to Your will, and that Your wisdom is always perfect.

Strengthen my confidence in Your character. Help me never to interpret my circumstances apart from Your love or Your promises. Teach me to continue praying, believing, obeying and giving thanks with joyful expectation, knowing that You are always faithful to Your Word.

Holy Spirit, continually remind me of my union with Christ and help me to live every day from the reality of the Fourth Dimension. Guard my heart from fear, anxiety and discouragement, and establish me in unwavering confidence in my Father's faithfulness.

May my life continually testify that You are good, that Your Word is true, and that Your promises in Christ are certain.

In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
