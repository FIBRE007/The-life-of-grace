🎧 10. Living to Please the Father

Living to Please the Father

Introduction

In the previous chapter we discovered that thanksgiving, peace and rest are the natural atmosphere of God's sons. As we remain conscious of our union with Christ, we begin to carry the atmosphere of the Kingdom wherever we go. That naturally raises an important question:

If we are God's beloved sons, what should become the greatest ambition of our lives every day?

For many believers, the Christian life has become a list of things to avoid and responsibilities to fulfil. Success is often measured by how much ministry we do, how many people admire us, how much influence we possess or how many spiritual activities fill our calendars.

Yet when we examine the life of Jesus, we discover a very different ambition.

Jesus did not wake each morning asking how He could become more popular.

He did not seek the applause of the crowds. He never measured success by the size of His following. He lived for one audience. The Father. Everything He said...

Every decision He made... Every miracle He performed... Every conversation He had... Flowed from one overwhelming desire:

To please His Father.

This is the invitation of sonship. God has not merely called us to believe in Him. He has called us to walk with Him. He has not merely redeemed us from sin. He has brought us into His family.

And within every healthy family, love naturally creates a desire to bring joy to those we love.

A child who knows he is deeply loved does not ask, "What is the least I can do?" Instead he asks, "How can I make my father smile?" This is the spirit of sonship. The believer no longer obeys because he fears rejection. Neither does he serve because he hopes to earn acceptance. Through Christ, the Father has already accepted us. We are His beloved sons.

Now our obedience becomes the joyful response of children who know they are loved.

This changes the entire Christian life. Work becomes worship. Relationships become opportunities to reflect the Father's heart. Ordinary conversations become acts of obedience. Private decisions become expressions of love.

Even the smallest responsibilities become opportunities to bring pleasure to our Father. The Christian life is therefore not lived one Sunday at a time. This chapter is about those ordinary days.

It is about learning to wake each morning with one simple prayer upon our hearts:

"Father, how can I please You today?"

What Pleases the Father?

Before we ask how to please the Father, we must first understand something far more important.

The Father's pleasure begins with His Son.

At the beginning of Jesus' public ministry, before He preached a sermon, healed the sick or performed a miracle, the heavens opened and the Father declared:

“This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:17

These words reveal something beautiful about the Father's heart. Notice when He spoke them. Not after Jesus' miracles. Not after the crowds followed Him. Not after His resurrection.

Before any public ministry had begun. The Father's delight was first rooted in relationship. Jesus was His beloved Son. Everything else flowed from that relationship. Later, on the Mount of Transfiguration, the Father repeated the same declaration.

“This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!”

Matthew 17:5

The Father's opinion of His Son never changed.

He delighted in Him completely.

This truth becomes even more wonderful when we remember what Paul writes concerning believers.

“...to the praise of the glory of His grace, by which He made us accepted in the Beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

What an astonishing truth. We have not merely been accepted by God. We have been accepted in the Beloved. Our acceptance is inseparable from our union with Christ. The Father looks upon us through His Son.

This means the Christian life never begins with us trying to gain God's approval.

It begins with us receiving His grace. Religion says, "Work harder so God may accept you." The Gospel says,

"Christ has brought you into the Father's family. Live as the son you already are."

This distinction changes everything. Imagine a child constantly wondering whether his father truly loves him. Every act of obedience would become an attempt to earn affection. Every mistake would create fear. Every failure would produce insecurity.

Now imagine another child who knows without question that he is deeply loved.

His obedience comes from security. His desire is not to earn love. That is the difference between religion and sonship. The Father has already settled our acceptance through Jesus Christ. Now He invites us to enjoy fellowship with Him.

Does this mean the way we live no longer matters? Certainly not.

The New Testament repeatedly teaches that the Father delights in lives that reflect His Son.

The writer of Hebrews says concerning Enoch:

“By faith Enoch was taken away so that he did not see death... for before he was taken he had this testimony, that he pleased God.”

Hebrews 11:5

What an extraordinary testimony. Not that he became famous. Not that he built a great ministry. Not that he accumulated wealth. Simply this:

He pleased God.

The next verse tells us why.

“But without faith it is impossible to please Him...”

Hebrews 11:6

Faith pleases the Father because faith trusts Him. Faith believes His character. Faith rests in His promises. Faith depends upon Him rather than upon self.

This reminds us that the Father is not first looking for impressive achievements.

He is looking for hearts that trust Him. He delights in humility. He delights in children who walk with Him. This should free every believer from the exhausting pursuit of performance. The Father is not asking us to impress Him.

He is inviting us to walk with Him.

As we become increasingly conscious of our union with Christ, pleasing the Father stops feeling like a burden.

It becomes our greatest joy.

But this immediately raises another question.

If pleasing the Father is the desire of God's sons, what does that actually look like during an ordinary day?

How do everyday moments become opportunities to bring joy to our Father's heart?

The Father Rewards Those Who Please Him

We have said that faith pleases God. But we have not yet said what follows from that, and what follows changes everything.

Read the verse again slowly, and notice that it has two halves:

“But without faith it is impossible to please Him, for he who comes to God must believe that He is, and that He is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him.”

Hebrews 11:6

Most believers know the first half. Without faith it is impossible to please Him.

Far fewer live from the second half.

He is a rewarder.

Not merely a judge. Not merely an observer. Not a distant Father who receives our obedience and offers nothing in return.

A rewarder.

And notice what the verse requires you to believe. Two things, and both are necessary. That He is — that He exists, that He is real, that He is there. And that He rewards those who diligently seek Him.

You cannot please God while quietly believing He gives nothing back.

That may be the most overlooked truth in this chapter. Many sincere Christians have made peace with a God who demands but does not respond. They serve faithfully and expect nothing. They would call that humility. Scripture calls it something else, because a God who does not reward is not the God who is actually there, and faith that expects nothing from Him is not faith in Him at all.

Why the Miraculous Follows a Pleased Father

Here we must be careful, because this truth is easily distorted in two opposite directions.

The first distortion turns God into a vending machine. Please Him, and He must produce. Obey correctly, give generously, believe hard enough, and the miracle is owed. That is not sonship. It is trade.

The second distortion is quieter and far more common among sincere believers. It concludes that God no longer responds at all, that the age of His intervention has passed, and that the Christian life is now simply endurance until heaven.

Both are wrong, and Hebrews 11:6 corrects both.

God rewards. That settles the second error.

He rewards those who diligently seek Him — not those who perform correctly. That settles the first.

Notice the object of the seeking. Not the reward. Him.

The miraculous has never been the goal of the Christian life. It is the response of a Father to a child who wants Him. And that ordering matters enormously, because the moment the miracle becomes the object, we have stopped seeking the Father and started using Him.

But when He is genuinely the object, something begins to happen that most believers only notice in hindsight.

You begin to experience Him.

The Daily Miraculous

We have made the word miracle too narrow. We reserve it for the dramatic, the medically inexplicable, the sort of thing that would make the news. And because we define it that way, most believers conclude that they have never seen one.

Consider what has actually been happening in you.

A temper that once ruled you no longer does. That is not self-improvement. That is God.

A bitterness you nursed for years toward someone who genuinely wronged you has quietly lost its grip, and you did not decide to forgive so much as discover one day that you had.

A fear that once dictated your decisions has loosened. Peace comes now in situations that would have destroyed you five years ago. You give without the old anxiety. You wait without the old panic. You face a bad report and find, to your own surprise, that something in you is steady.

The provision came, and you still cannot fully explain how.

The relationship healed when you had privately concluded it would not.

The door you could not open opened, and the one you were desperate to walk through closed, and you can now see why.

None of this made the news.

All of it was God.

The Father who rewards those who diligently seek Him has been rewarding you continually, and much of it has gone unrecognised because you were watching for lightning while He was reshaping your life.

Learn to see it. Keep a record if you must. A believer who cannot name what God has done this year is not a believer God has ignored; he is usually a believer who has not been paying attention.

And yes — He still does the dramatic. He still heals bodies, still provides beyond explanation, still opens what men have sealed. Nothing in Scripture retires Him from that. But the daily miraculous is the wider river, and it flows through the life of every son who genuinely seeks the Father.

Living Every Ordinary Day for the Father

The Christian life is often imagined as a series of extraordinary moments.

People think of revival meetings, mission trips, powerful sermons, miraculous healings or major life-changing decisions.

While God certainly works through extraordinary moments, they are not where most of life is lived.

Most of life happens in ordinary moments. It happens on Monday morning. It happens during meetings, classrooms, markets, offices, kitchens and homes.

It happens while replying to a message, paying a bill, helping a neighbour or listening to a friend.

These ordinary moments are where sons learn to please their Father.

One of the greatest misconceptions in the Christian life is the idea that God is only interested in what happens inside a church building.

The Scriptures present a very different picture.

Paul writes,

“And whatever you do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through Him.”

Colossians 3:17

Notice the phrase:

"Whatever you do."

Nothing is excluded. Whether speaking or remaining silent. Whether working or resting. Whether leading or serving. Whether making important decisions or carrying out ordinary responsibilities.

Everything can be done for the Father.

The Christian life is therefore not divided into sacred activities and ordinary activities.

Everything becomes sacred because everything belongs to Him.

Paul reinforces this truth elsewhere.

“Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God.”

1 Corinthians 10:31

Even eating and drinking—the most ordinary activities of life—can glorify God. This transforms the believer's perspective completely. Life is no longer measured only by the "big moments." Instead, every ordinary moment becomes an opportunity to honour the Father. Imagine beginning each day with one simple question:

"Father, how can I please You today?"

That single question changes how we approach every responsibility.

When the alarm sounds in the morning, rather than beginning the day with complaints, the son remembers that another day is another opportunity to walk with his Father.

As he prepares for work or school, he chooses gratitude instead of murmuring because gratitude reflects the Father's heart.

When traffic is heavy and frustration begins to rise, he remembers that patience also pleases the Father.

When a difficult customer speaks harshly, he chooses gentleness instead of anger.

When no one notices his hard work, he continues serving faithfully because he is ultimately serving the Lord.

Suddenly, ordinary life becomes deeply spiritual. Consider the workplace. Many believers separate their careers from their faith. Yet Paul writes,

“And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men.”

Colossians 3:23

This means the Christian teacher teaches for the Father. The accountant balances the books for the Father. The driver drives for the Father. The cleaner cleans for the Father. The nurse cares for patients for the Father.

The student studies for the Father. The business owner serves customers for the Father. The pastor preaches for the Father. The value of the work is not determined by the occupation. It is determined by the One for whom it is done.

This truth removes the false distinction between "spiritual" work and "ordinary" work. There is no insignificant work when it is offered to the Father. The ordinary office desk becomes an altar. The classroom becomes an opportunity for worship. The workshop becomes a place where Christ is honoured.

The kitchen becomes a place where love is expressed. The Father delights in sons who honour Him through ordinary faithfulness. The same principle applies to our conversations. Every day we speak hundreds or even thousands of words. Some encourage.

Some discourage. Some heal. Some wound. Before speaking, sons quietly ask themselves,

"Father, will these words bring You pleasure?"

That one question changes conversations. It affects how we speak to our spouses. How we correct our children. How we disagree with people. Even silence sometimes becomes the response that pleases the Father.

The same principle applies to our finances. Every purchase. Every gift. Every act of generosity. Every financial decision.

Can become an act of worship. Instead of asking, "Can I afford this?" the son also asks,

"Father, does this decision honour You?"

Integrity becomes worship. Likewise, time becomes worship. How we spend our evenings. How we use our weekends. How much attention we give to our families.

Whether we keep our promises. Whether we arrive on time. Whether we complete what we begin.

These seemingly small decisions reveal whether we are living consciously before our Father.

This does not mean sons become anxious about every decision. Neither does it produce legalism. Love does not create anxiety. Love creates attentiveness. When someone deeply loves another person, they naturally become thoughtful.

Not because they fear rejection.

But because bringing joy to that person has become one of life's greatest pleasures.

This is exactly how sons relate to their Father. Their daily question is not, "What is the minimum I must do?" It is,

"Father, what would bring You joy?"

That question gradually reshapes the entire Christian life. Ordinary routines become opportunities for worship. Ordinary conversations become opportunities to reveal Christ. Ordinary responsibilities become opportunities to demonstrate faithfulness.

The believer begins to discover that walking with God is not confined to quiet times or church services.

It is the continual privilege of living every ordinary moment before the Father's face.

Yet there is another place where the reality of sonship is tested even more deeply.

Not in public.

But in private.

Who we are when no one is watching often reveals whether our desire is truly to please the Father or merely to impress people.

That is where we now turn our attention.

Pleasing the Father When No One Is Watching

One of the greatest tests of sonship is not what we do in public, but who we are in private.

Public life is visible. Private life is known only to God. It is possible to impress people while grieving the Father.

It is equally possible to go unnoticed by people while bringing immense pleasure to the Father.

This is why Jesus repeatedly drew attention to the hidden life.

In the Sermon on the Mount, He warned His disciples against performing righteous acts merely to be seen by others. Speaking about giving, praying and fasting, He repeatedly said,

“And your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly.”

Matthew 6:4

Again He says,

“But you, when you pray, go into your room, and when you have shut your door, pray to your Father who is in the secret place; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.”

Matthew 6:6

Notice what Jesus emphasises. Not simply the action. But the audience. The Father sees.

Long before anyone notices our gifts, our achievements or our ministry, the Father has already seen the hidden life.

He sees every unseen act of obedience. Every private prayer. Every quiet decision to forgive. Every temptation resisted. Every act of honesty.

Every sacrifice made for His sake. Nothing escapes His attention. This is deeply encouraging. Many believers faithfully serve in places where few people notice them. A mother caring for her children.

A teacher preparing lessons late into the night. An employee refusing dishonest shortcuts. A student choosing integrity during an examination.

A young person walking away from temptation when no one else would ever know.

The world may never applaud these decisions. But the Father sees every one of them. David understood this truth when he wrote,

“O Lord, You have searched me and known me.”

Psalm 139:1

He continues,

“You comprehend my path and my lying down, And are acquainted with all my ways.”

Psalm 139:3

Nothing about our lives is hidden from the Father. He knows our words before we speak them. He knows our thoughts before we express them. He knows our motives before they become actions. For some people this thought creates fear.

For sons, it creates comfort.

The One who sees everything is the same Father who loves us perfectly.

Because He knows us completely, we no longer need to pretend. We do not need to wear spiritual masks. We do not need to appear stronger than we are.

We come before Him honestly, knowing that His grace is greater than our weakness.

Private faithfulness begins in the heart. Long before actions become visible, attitudes are formed within. This is why Solomon wrote,

“Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

The heart is the source from which our words, attitudes and actions flow.

If the heart delights in the Father, obedience gradually becomes natural.

If the heart seeks the approval of people, even good actions can become self-centred.

The Father therefore cares not only about what we do but why we do it.

Consider honesty. Returning extra change when a cashier makes a mistake. Refusing to exaggerate a report at work. Admitting an error that no one else noticed. Declaring all our income honestly.

Keeping our promises even when it becomes inconvenient. These decisions may never become public testimonies. Yet every one of them delights the Father's heart. Or consider purity.

In a world where temptation is only a few clicks away, the believer often faces battles that no one else will ever know about.

Choosing purity in secret is not merely avoiding sin.

It is saying,

"Father, You are more precious to me than this temptation."

That brings Him pleasure. The same is true of our thoughts. Not every battle is outward. Sometimes the greatest victories happen within. Choosing gratitude instead of resentment.

Choosing forgiveness instead of bitterness. Choosing hope instead of despair. Choosing to pray instead of complain. These victories may never be seen by another human being.

Yet they are precious to the Father because they reveal a heart that is being transformed into the likeness of Christ.

Paul also speaks about serving with sincerity.

“Bondservants, obey in all things your masters according to the flesh, not with eyeservice, as men-pleasers, but in sincerity of heart, fearing God.”

Colossians 3:22

The phrase "not with eyeservice" is especially challenging.

Some people only work diligently when someone is watching.

They are productive when the manager is present but careless when left alone.

Paul says God's sons are different.

Their excellence is not determined by supervision.

It is determined by the awareness that they are always serving the Lord.

The Father is their ultimate audience. Whether anyone notices or not, they remain faithful. This principle extends to every area of life. How we browse the internet. How we use opportunities that could easily be abused.

These moments reveal whether our desire is truly to please the Father. Private faithfulness is often the soil from which public usefulness grows.

Before David stood before Goliath, he faithfully cared for sheep where no one celebrated him.

Before Joseph governed Egypt, he honoured God in a prison cell.

Before Jesus' public ministry, He spent years in quiet obedience in Nazareth.

God often prepares His sons in hidden places before entrusting them with visible responsibilities.

Never despise the ordinary, unseen moments of faithfulness.

The Father does not.

He delights in the son who remains honest when dishonesty would be easier.

He delights in the daughter who chooses purity when compromise would be unnoticed.

He delights in the believer who worships Him sincerely when no one else is present.

For sons, integrity is not simply doing the right thing.

It is living every moment before the loving gaze of the Father.

When we remember that He sees us—not with condemnation but with the affection of a perfect Father—we begin to realise that the hidden places of life are some of the greatest opportunities to bring Him pleasure.

Yet there is another arena where our love for the Father becomes visible every day.

It is not only in private decisions.

It is in the way we treat the people He places in our lives.

Pleasing the Father Through People

One of the clearest ways to know whether we are living to please the Father is by observing how we treat the people He has placed in our lives.

It is often easier to express love towards God than it is to demonstrate His love towards difficult people.

Yet the two can never be separated.

The Apostle John writes,

“If someone says, 'I love God,' and hates his brother, he is a liar...”

1 John 4:20

Our relationship with the Father is revealed in the way we relate to others.

Every conversation. Every disagreement. Every misunderstanding. Every opportunity to serve. Every act of forgiveness.

Every word we speak.

Becomes an opportunity either to reflect the Father's heart or to misrepresent Him.

Jesus gave His disciples a new commandment.

“A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another.”

John 13:34

Notice the standard. Not merely loving people the way they deserve. Not loving them according to how they treat us. But loving them as Christ has loved us. This kind of love is impossible through human effort alone. It is the fruit of Christ living within us.

When we remember how patiently the Father has dealt with us, we begin to extend that same patience to others.

When we remember how freely He has forgiven us, forgiveness becomes easier.

When we remember His kindness towards us, kindness begins to shape our own responses.

The Father's pleasure is often found in the ordinary ways we love people.

Sometimes we imagine that pleasing God requires extraordinary sacrifices. Yet very often it is found in the smallest acts of love. Listening without interrupting. Speaking gently instead of harshly. Keeping a promise.

Showing appreciation. Helping someone without expecting recognition. Encouraging a discouraged friend. Praying for someone who has offended us. Choosing understanding instead of judgement.

These are not small things in the Father's eyes. They reveal the character of His Son. Paul paints a beautiful picture of this life.

“Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering; bearing with one another, and forgiving one another...”

Colossians 3:12–13

Notice where Paul begins.

"Holy and beloved."

Identity always comes before instruction. Because we are beloved, we can live differently. He then describes the clothing of God's sons. Not physical garments. But spiritual character.

Tender mercies. Kindness. Humility. Gentleness. Patience.

Forgiveness.

Every morning, believers have the opportunity to "put on" these qualities as they walk with the Father.

These virtues become especially important in the home. Our families often experience the real version of us. The version that appears after a long day. The version that responds when we are tired. The version that no longer needs to impress anyone.

It is there that sonship becomes wonderfully practical. The husband who chooses patience instead of irritation. The wife who responds with gentleness instead of harshness. Parents who correct their children without anger. Children who honour their parents even when they disagree.

These ordinary moments bring pleasure to the Father because they reflect His heart.

The same applies in the workplace. It is easy to work diligently when everyone is cooperative. But what happens when colleagues become difficult? When someone else receives the promotion? When your efforts go unnoticed?

When criticism feels unfair? The natural response is often resentment. The response of a son is different. He remembers that he ultimately serves the Father. Therefore he chooses integrity instead of revenge.

Grace instead of bitterness. Faithfulness instead of discouragement. Not because people deserve it. But because he desires to please his Father. Paul writes,

“Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, and evil speaking be put away from you, with all malice.”

Ephesians 4:31

Then he immediately tells us what should replace them.

“And be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you.”

Ephesians 4:31–32

Notice the foundation. We forgive because we have been forgiven. We extend grace because grace has been extended to us. Everything flows from what the Father has already done.

One of the greatest tests of sonship is how we respond to people who cannot benefit us.

How do we treat the cleaner? The security guard? The waiter? The driver? The elderly neighbour?

The person who disagrees with us? The stranger who can offer us nothing in return?

These moments reveal whether we truly see people as the Father sees them.

Jesus never overlooked people. He stopped for the blind. He spoke with children. He welcomed those whom society rejected. He noticed those others ignored.

His life teaches us that every person matters because every person bears the image of God.

As sons, we are called to carry that same heart into our daily lives.

This also affects the way we speak. Words have extraordinary power. A careless sentence can wound someone for years. A gracious word can strengthen a weary heart. Before speaking, sons increasingly learn to ask,

"Father, will these words reflect Your heart?"

Sometimes the answer is to speak. Sometimes the answer is to remain silent. Sometimes it is to encourage. Sometimes it is simply to listen. Walking with the Father teaches us the wisdom to know the difference.

Perhaps one of the greatest ways we please the Father through people is by forgiving them.

Forgiveness does not excuse wrong. Neither does it deny pain. It releases the desire for revenge and entrusts justice to God. Every act of forgiveness declares, "Father, because You have shown me mercy, I choose to show mercy." That greatly delights His heart.

As believers grow in sonship, they begin to realise that every person they meet is an opportunity to represent their Father.

The neighbour. The colleague. The customer. The spouse. The child.

The stranger.

Each encounter becomes an opportunity to reveal the kindness, patience and love of Christ.

This is not merely good behaviour. It is the life of the Father being expressed through His children. Yet loving people becomes especially challenging when life itself becomes difficult.

How do sons continue pleasing the Father when prayers seem unanswered, pressure increases and circumstances become painful?

That is the question we must now consider.

Pleasing the Father in Difficult Seasons

It is relatively easy to say we want to please the Father when life is peaceful.

It is much harder when life becomes painful. Anyone can worship when prayers are answered quickly. Anyone can rejoice when everything seems to be going well. But what happens when life becomes difficult? What happens when the promotion does not come?

When the doctor's report is discouraging? When a trusted friend disappoints us? When finances become tight? When our prayers seem to be taking longer than we expected? These are the moments when our love for the Father is refined.

They reveal whether our relationship with Him depends upon circumstances or upon His unchanging character.

One of the greatest misunderstandings in the Christian life is the belief that pleasing the Father means we will never experience difficulty.

The Scriptures never teach this.

Jesus Himself perfectly pleased the Father, yet He experienced rejection, misunderstanding, suffering and ultimately the Cross.

The Father's pleasure did not exempt His Son from suffering. Rather, it sustained Him through it. James writes,

“My brethren, count it all joy when you fall into various trials.”

James 1:2

At first this instruction seems almost impossible.

James is not asking believers to enjoy suffering.

He is teaching them to recognise that God is still at work even in difficult seasons.

The son who continues trusting his Father during adversity brings great pleasure to Him because such trust declares,

"Father, I know You are still good even when I do not understand everything."

That is faith.

Earlier in this book we discovered that faith responds to God's character rather than to circumstances.

This truth becomes especially important during seasons of pressure. Anyone can believe when everything is visible. Faith continues believing when the outcome has not yet appeared. Paul writes,

“And we know that all things work together for good to those who love God, to those who are the called according to His purpose.”

Romans 8:28

Notice carefully what Paul says.

He does not say that all things are good.

He says that God works through all things for the good of those who love Him.

The Father is never absent from the difficult seasons of His children. He remains present. He remains good. Knowing this changes the believer's response to adversity. Instead of asking, "Father, why is this happening to me?" he begins to ask,

"Father, how can I honour You in this situation?"

That single question transforms suffering. The circumstance may remain difficult. But the heart now has a purpose. Consider Joseph. He was betrayed by his brothers.

Sold into slavery. Falsely accused. Forgotten in prison.

Yet throughout every painful chapter of his life, the Scriptures repeatedly remind us that the Lord was with him.

Joseph did not allow bitterness to define him. Neither did he abandon his integrity because life seemed unfair. He continued to honour God. His faithfulness during hidden seasons eventually prepared him for public responsibility. The same principle appears in the life of Job.

Job did not understand everything that was happening around him. He wrestled with difficult questions. He experienced profound grief. Yet he continued to acknowledge God's greatness. His circumstances changed, but God's character did not.

This is one of the greatest lessons sons can learn. The Father's goodness is not measured by today's circumstances. It is revealed perfectly in Jesus Christ.

If the Cross proves anything, it proves that the Father's love remains constant even when the path is difficult.

Peter gives believers another beautiful invitation.

“Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

Notice the reason. We cast our cares upon Him because He cares for us. Not because we are strong enough. Not because we understand everything. But because our Father cares. Sons do not carry burdens alone.

They continually place them into the Father's hands. This does not mean they stop working. It means they stop carrying anxieties that belong to the Father.

One of the greatest ways believers please the Father during difficult seasons is by refusing to allow hardship to change their view of Him.

Pressure should never become our theologian. The Father's Word remains our foundation. When disappointment comes, we still worship. When answers seem delayed, we still trust. When people misunderstand us, we still love.

When opportunities disappear, we still remain faithful. Not because pretending is easy. But because we know our Father. This is where the truths from the previous chapters become wonderfully practical. The son who has learned thanksgiving continues giving thanks.

The son who has learned peace continues resting in Christ. The son who has learned faith continues believing the Father's promises. Every previous lesson now finds expression in everyday life.

Even our responses to suffering become opportunities to bring pleasure to the Father.

This does not mean believers never cry. Jesus wept. It does not mean we never ask questions. Many faithful servants of God did. It does not mean we never feel weak.

Paul himself spoke openly about weakness. Rather, it means that beneath every emotion remains an unshakable confidence:

"My Father is faithful."

That confidence produces remarkable stability. It enables believers to forgive instead of becoming bitter. To remain generous when resources seem limited. To continue serving when recognition never comes. To continue praying when the answer has not yet appeared.

To continue loving when love is not returned.

Such faith brings pleasure to the Father because it reflects the very life of His Son.

Perhaps no season reveals the heart of a believer more than adversity. Success often reveals our gifts. Difficulty reveals our trust.

When sons continue walking with their Father through life's valleys, they demonstrate that their relationship with Him is not based upon convenience but upon love.

One day every difficult season will come to an end.

But something far greater will remain.

The testimony that through every joy and every sorrow, through every victory and every trial, we chose to live for the Father's pleasure.

That leads us to the final question of this chapter.

If God's sons live for the Father's pleasure, whose approval should matter most?

When the Chains Do Not Break Easily

There is a subject we must handle honestly, because a chapter about pleasing the Father and receiving His reward can wound the very people who most need it.

What about the believer who is genuinely bound?

The addiction that has survived a hundred prayers. The habit confessed and confessed again. The compulsion that returns after every fresh resolve. Many sincere Christians carry something like this, and they carry it in silence, because a chapter like this one seems to be describing a life they cannot reach.

If that is you, hear this carefully.

Your struggle is not proof that God is displeased with you.

That lie has kept more believers in bondage than the substance ever did. It reasons like this: if the Father rewarded those who please Him, and I am still bound, then I must not please Him. And so shame closes the mouth, isolation deepens, and the very help God provides is avoided.

Reject that reasoning entirely.

The Father's pleasure in you was never established by your victory. It was established by His Son.

“This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:17

The Father said that before Jesus had preached a sermon, healed anyone or gone to the Cross. And you are in Christ. The Father's pleasure toward you rests where His pleasure toward His Son rests — not upon achievement, but upon relationship.

Now hear the truth about the bondage itself.

“Therefore if the Son makes you free, you shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

“For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace.”

Romans 6:14

Sin shall not have dominion. That is a promise, not a slogan, and it is spoken over you now.

But how does that freedom usually come?

Sometimes instantly. God does at times break a chain in a moment, and the person never touches the thing again. Those testimonies are real and we should thank God for every one of them.

More often, freedom comes progressively — and this is where many believers lose heart, because they have been taught to expect only the sudden.

Consider how God has always worked. He gave Israel the land, and then had them possess it city by city. He gives the believer a new nature instantly, and renews the mind over years. Deliverance frequently follows the same pattern: the authority is settled at once, and the experience of it unfolds.

That is not a lesser deliverance. It is the ordinary shape of God's dealing with His children.

The Means God Uses

Here we must say something that some Christians resist, and it needs saying plainly.

God's provision is not less spiritual for arriving through ordinary means.

Scripture never presents faith as the refusal of help. It presents faith as trusting God — and God has appointed means through which He commonly works.

He has appointed the body of Christ:

“Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed.”

James 5:16

Notice what that verse assumes. That healing is connected to being known. Not to praying harder in private, but to bringing what is hidden into the light with people who will pray for you. Addiction thrives in secrecy, and God's prescription attacks the secrecy directly.

“Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Burdens are not meant to be carried alone. A believer who insists on fighting privately is not exercising great faith; he is refusing the help God has already provided.

And when the struggle has physical dimensions — as addiction very often does — medical help, counselling and treatment are not evidence of weak faith. They are provision. The same God who heals directly also gave us physicians, and the believer who takes the medicine is not thereby doubting God any more than the believer who eats bread is doubting the God who provides daily bread.

Do not let a false spirituality keep you from the help God has put within your reach.

“No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Will also make the way of escape. Look for it. It is frequently a person, a conversation, a door out of the room, a number to call. God rarely writes the escape route in the sky. He usually places it in front of you and waits to see whether you will take it.

When You Fall Again

You may fall. And when you do, everything depends on what you do in the next hour.

The enemy's strategy after a fall is never subtle. He does not tempt you to sin again immediately. He tempts you to hide.

You have done it again. There is no point praying now. Wait until you have got yourself under control, then come back to God.

That counsel sounds like reverence. It is the oldest trap in Scripture — Adam sewing leaves and stepping into the trees.

Come immediately. Not when you are clean. Now.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.”

Romans 8:1

Now. Not eventually. Not after a probation period.

“For a righteous man may fall seven times and rise again.”

Proverbs 24:16

Notice how the righteous man is identified in that verse. Not by never falling. By rising again.

And notice who finishes the work:

“He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.”

Philippians 1:6

He began it. He will complete it. Your part is not to complete it by willpower; your part is to keep coming to the One who will.

Paul was given a thorn he asked three times to have removed. God's answer was not removal:

“My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

There are seasons in which God's reward is not the removal of the struggle but a supply of grace within it that you did not previously know existed. That, too, is the miraculous. A man who should have collapsed and did not has seen the power of God as surely as a man whose chains fell off.

So do not measure the Father's pleasure by the presence of your struggle.

Measure it by the Cross, where it was settled before you were born.

And keep seeking Him — diligently, daily, and without shame — because He is a rewarder, and He has never once failed to reward a son who came.

Chapter Summary

Throughout this chapter we have discovered that the greatest ambition of God's sons is not to gain the approval of people but to live in a way that brings pleasure to their Father. This desire does not arise from fear or the pressure to earn acceptance. It flows from the wonderful truth that we have already been accepted in Christ, the Beloved. Because we know we are loved, pleasing the Father becomes the joyful response of our hearts.

We began by learning that the Father's delight is first revealed in His Son. Before Jesus performed a single miracle or preached a single sermon, the Father declared, "This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." Through our union with Christ, we too have been brought into that relationship of grace. Our identity is established before our activity, and our acceptance becomes the foundation from which we now live.

We then discovered that everyday life is the primary place where sons please the Father. The Christian life is not confined to church services or extraordinary moments. It is lived in ordinary conversations, daily work, family life, financial decisions and countless unseen opportunities to honour God. Every ordinary responsibility becomes an act of worship when it is done with the desire to bring pleasure to the Father.

Next, we saw that the hidden life matters deeply to God. The Father sees what no one else sees. He delights in secret faithfulness, quiet obedience, integrity, purity and sincere devotion. While people often celebrate what is public, God values the heart that remains faithful when no one is watching.

We also learned that our relationships provide daily opportunities to reveal the Father's heart. Every act of forgiveness, kindness, patience, humility and love reflects the life of Christ within us. As sons, we please the Father by treating people with the same grace and mercy that He has shown us.

We then considered the difficult seasons of life. Trials, disappointments and delays do not prevent believers from pleasing the Father. Instead, they become opportunities to trust His character more deeply. When God's sons continue to worship, obey, forgive and hope in the midst of adversity, they demonstrate a faith that delights their Father and bears witness to His faithfulness.

Finally, we discovered that God's sons live for one audience. The world constantly seeks recognition, applause and approval, but the believer's greatest ambition is to hear the Father's commendation. Success is no longer measured by popularity, influence or possessions, but by faithful fellowship with the Father. At the end of each day, the greatest question is not, "How much did I accomplish?" but, "Did I walk with my Father today?"

Living to please the Father is therefore not another burden to carry. It is the joyful privilege of sons who know they are loved. Every thought, every decision, every relationship and every ordinary moment becomes an opportunity to respond to His grace. As we continue abiding in Christ and walking with the Holy Spirit, our lives increasingly reflect the One in whom the Father is well pleased.

We also saw that Hebrews 11:6 has two halves, and that most believers live from only the first. Without faith it is impossible to please Him — but He is also a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him. You cannot please God while quietly believing He gives nothing back.

The miraculous is therefore not the goal of the Christian life but the response of a Father to a child who wants Him. And much of that reward is daily rather than dramatic: a temper that no longer rules, a bitterness that has lost its grip, peace in situations that would once have destroyed us. A believer who cannot name what God has done this year is usually not a believer God has ignored, but one who has not been paying attention.

Finally, we faced honestly the struggle that does not quickly yield. A continuing battle is not proof that the Father is displeased. His pleasure toward us rests where His pleasure toward His Son rests. Freedom sometimes comes instantly and more often progressively, and God's provision is not less spiritual for arriving through ordinary means — confession to trusted believers, the help of the body of Christ, and the treatment He has made available. When we fall, the enemy's first strategy is to make us hide. We come immediately instead, because there is now no condemnation, and because He who began the good work will complete it.

Reflection Questions

  1. Is my greatest ambition to please the Father, or have I been seeking the approval and recognition of people more than His pleasure?
  1. In what ways have I been trying to earn God's acceptance instead of living from the assurance that I am already accepted in Christ?
  1. Which area of my everyday life—my work, my home, my relationships, my words or my private life—needs to become a more intentional expression of my love for the Father?
  1. How do I respond when no one notices my faithfulness? Am I content knowing that my Father sees every act of obedience done in secret?
  1. Do I genuinely believe that God is "a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him," or have I quietly made peace with a God who demands but does not respond?
  1. What has God actually done in me over the past year that I have never named as miraculous — a fear loosened, a bitterness lifted, a provision I still cannot explain?
  1. Is there a struggle I have kept hidden because I assumed it proved God was displeased with me? What would it mean to bring it into the light this week?
  1. Beginning today, what one practical change will help me live more consciously before my Father's presence and make pleasing Him the governing ambition of my life?

Father,

Forgive me for the times I have lived for the approval of people more than for Your pleasure. Forgive me for measuring success by recognition, achievement or comparison instead of by faithful fellowship with You. Thank You that Your grace continually calls me back to Yourself.

Teach me to live each ordinary day with a heart that delights in pleasing You. May my thoughts, my words, my decisions and my actions reflect the life of Christ within me. Help me to honour You in public and in private, in times of joy and in seasons of difficulty, when others are watching and when no one else knows what I choose.

Holy Spirit, continually remind me that I am united with Christ and empower me to walk as a true son of the Father. Produce within me the character of Jesus so that pleasing the Father becomes the natural desire of my heart. Lead me in every conversation, every decision and every responsibility so that my whole life becomes an act of worship.

Father, may the testimony of my life not be built upon earthly success but upon faithful fellowship with You. Let my greatest joy be knowing that I have walked with You, trusted You and honoured You. May my life continually bring glory to Your name and reflect the beauty of Your Son.

In the name of Jesus Christ,

Amen.
