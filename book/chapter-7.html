Living by Faith: Participating in the Realities of the Fourth Dimension

Introduction

If God's Kingdom is real... If God truly reigns supreme in the Fourth Dimension... If Christ has completely finished the work of redemption... If you have become a new creation... If your mind is being renewed...

Then one question remains.

How do these realities become your daily experience?

How does what is already true in Heaven become evident in your life?

How does God's will become visible on earth? How do unseen realities shape visible circumstances? These are not merely theological questions. They are intensely practical. Every believer eventually asks them.

Some ask them in the middle of sickness. Others in financial difficulty. Others while waiting for God's promises. Others during seasons of uncertainty. Some even ask them quietly after years of faithfully serving God.

If all these things are true, why does my experience sometimes seem so different?

The answer the Scriptures consistently give is remarkably simple.

Faith.

Yet few biblical words have been more misunderstood than this one. For some, faith means optimism. For others, it means positive thinking. Some reduce faith to positive confession. Others imagine faith is a force that compels God to act.

Some think faith ignores reality. Others believe faith is simply wishing hard enough until something happens. But are any of these ideas actually taught in Scripture?

If faith is so central to God's Kingdom, should we not allow God Himself to define it?

The Bible says,

“Without faith it is impossible to please Him...”

Hebrews 11:6

Why? Why has God chosen faith? The answer reaches to the very heart of His relationship with mankind. Faith is not merely something believers occasionally exercise. Faith is the way God's children relate to Him.

It always has been. Long before Abraham... before Moses... before David... before the prophets... before Pentecost... God's relationship with His people has always been built upon one principle.

Trusting His Word.

This is why Habakkuk declared,

“The just shall live by his faith.”

Habakkuk 2:4

Paul repeats it.

“The just shall live by faith.”

Romans 1:17

The writer of Hebrews repeats it again.

“Now the just shall live by faith.”

Hebrews 10:38

Notice what Scripture does not say. It does not say, "The just begin by faith." Nor, "The just occasionally use faith." It says,

"The just shall live by faith."

Faith is not merely the doorway into the Christian life.

It is the atmosphere in which the Christian life is lived.

This chapter therefore seeks to answer one of the greatest questions in Scripture.

What is biblical faith?

Not according to tradition. Not according to popular teaching. Not according to human philosophy. But according to God Himself.

For unless we understand faith correctly, we will never understand how the realities of the Fourth Dimension become realities in our everyday lives.

Everything that follows in this book rests upon the answer to this question.

So before we ask what faith can accomplish... we must first ask something even more important.

What is faith?

What Is Faith?

What exactly is faith?

Does faith originate in man, or does it begin with God?

These questions matter because much of what is called "faith" today would scarcely be recognised by the Scriptures.

Many people have confidence. Some have confidence in themselves. Others have confidence in science. Some have confidence in money. Others have confidence in experience.

Confidence, by itself, is not biblical faith.

That object is God Himself, as He has revealed Himself through His Word.

This is why the Bible never asks us to have faith in faith.

It calls us to have faith in God.

Jesus said,

“Have faith in God.”

Mark 11:22

Notice how simple—and yet how profound—that command is. He did not say, "Have faith in your ability." Neither did He say, "Have faith in your feelings." Nor did He say, "Have faith in your circumstances eventually improving." He said,

"Have faith in God."

Faith is therefore not confidence generated by human determination. Faith is confidence produced by knowing the One who has spoken. The writer of Hebrews gives us Scripture's classic definition.

“Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.”

Hebrews 11:1

This verse has often been quoted, but not always carefully understood. Notice first what faith is not. Faith is not imagination. Faith is not pretending. Faith is not denying reality.

Faith is not convincing yourself that something is true when God has never spoken.

Faith is substance. Faith is evidence. How can something unseen possess substance? How can something invisible become evidence? Only because faith rests upon something infinitely more reliable than human sight.

The Word of God.

God's Word gives substance to what cannot yet be seen. His promise becomes the believer's evidence before circumstances change. Consider Noah. When God warned him about a flood, had Noah ever seen rain? Scripture gives no indication that he had.

Yet Hebrews says,

“By faith Noah, being divinely warned of things not yet seen, moved with godly fear, prepared an ark...”

Hebrews 11:7

Notice carefully. Noah did not build the ark because he saw the flood. He built it because God had spoken. The unseen Word became more certain than the visible world around him. That is faith.

Consider Abraham. God promised him descendants as numerous as the stars. Years passed. Nothing appeared to change. His body continued to age.

Sarah remained barren. Everything visible contradicted the promise. Yet Paul writes,

“He did not waver at the promise of God through unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God, and being fully convinced that what He had promised He was also able to perform.”

Romans 4:20–21

Notice what Abraham believed. Not merely that something wonderful would happen. He believed God. His confidence rested upon God's character. God had spoken.

Therefore God would perform what He had promised. Faith always honours the character of God. It says, "If God has spoken, His Word is more dependable than my circumstances." This explains why Paul writes,

“So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ.”

Romans 10:17

Pause and consider that statement carefully. Where does faith come from? Not from emotional excitement. Not from repeating certain words often enough. Faith comes from hearing.

The Word of Christ.

Faith is born when God's revelation enters the heart. This immediately exposes one of the greatest misconceptions about faith. Many people try to manufacture faith. They repeat statements. They stir emotions.

They convince themselves that they believe. But can anyone manufacture genuine biblical faith? Paul says no. Faith comes. It is received.

It is awakened by God's revelation. This means faith never begins with man. It always begins with God. God speaks. Man hears.

Man believes. Man responds. That order never changes throughout Scripture. This is why faith is never irrational. It is never a leap into the unknown.

It is a response to the God who has revealed Himself. Some imagine that faith means believing without evidence. The Bible teaches the opposite. Faith has the greatest evidence imaginable.

God Himself has spoken.

The issue has never been whether God's Word is trustworthy.

The issue has always been whether we believe it.

This explains why Hebrews immediately begins listing men and women whose lives were transformed by faith.

Abel. Enoch. Noah. Abraham. Sarah.

Moses. Rahab. David. The prophets. What do they all have in common?

Different personalities. Different cultures. Different generations. Different callings.

Yet every one of them trusted God's revelation above what their natural senses suggested.

Faith was never confidence in themselves. And that raises another important question.

If faith always depends upon God's Word, can there be genuine faith where God has not first spoken?

Faith Always Begins with God Speaking

Can there be biblical faith where God has not spoken? Or does faith always begin because God has first revealed Himself? These questions strike at the heart of what biblical faith really is. Much of what passes for faith today begins with human desire. "I want this." "I hope this happens." "I believe it will happen." But is desire the same as faith?

Scripture answers with remarkable consistency.

Faith never begins with man.

It always begins with God.

This is why Paul writes,

“So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ.”

Romans 10:17

Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say faith comes by wishing. He does not say faith comes by repeating certain words often enough. Faith comes because God has spoken. This principle appears from Genesis to Revelation. Consider Noah.

Why did Noah spend decades building an ark? Was he simply an optimistic man? Was he naturally hopeful? No. Scripture tells us exactly where his faith began.

“By faith Noah, being divinely warned of things not yet seen...”

Hebrews 11:7

Notice the order. God warned. Noah believed. Noah obeyed. Faith began because God first revealed His purpose.

Suppose Noah had decided one morning to build an ark without God speaking.

Would that have been faith? No. It would merely have been human initiative. Faith is never human initiative. It is always a response to divine revelation.

The same pattern appears in Abraham's life. Why did Abraham leave his country? Was he simply adventurous? Did he enjoy uncertainty? Scripture answers,

“Now the LORD had said to Abram: 'Get out of your country...'”

Genesis 12:1

Hebrews adds,

“By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called...”

Hebrews 11:8

Again notice the order. God spoke. Abraham believed. Abraham obeyed. Faith did not produce God's Word.

Moses followed exactly the same pattern. Why did he return to Egypt? Because he felt courageous? Because he suddenly believed in himself? No.

Everything changed because God revealed Himself at the burning bush.

“Come now, therefore, and I will send you to Pharaoh...”

Exodus 3:10

Moses' faith rested upon God's commission. The same principle appears in the life of Mary. Imagine the magnitude of what the angel announced. A virgin would conceive. The promised Messiah would be born.

How did Mary respond?

“Behold the maidservant of the Lord! Let it be to me according to your word.”

Luke 1:38

Those words capture the very essence of faith.

"According to Your word."

Mary did not invent God's plan. She responded to God's revelation. Her faith rested entirely upon what God had spoken.

Perhaps nowhere is this principle illustrated more clearly than in Peter walking on the water.

Many people say, "Peter had enough faith to walk on water." But is that really what happened? Look carefully. Peter first asked,

“Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You on the water.”

Matthew 14:28

Jesus answered with one word.

“Come.”

Matthew 14:29

Everything depended upon that word. Peter did not step onto the water because he felt courageous. He stepped because Christ had spoken. Suppose Peter had jumped before Jesus said, "Come." Would that have been faith? No.

It would have been presumption.

This distinction is one of the most important in the entire Christian life.

Faith responds to God's Word.

Presumption runs ahead of God's Word.

Faith follows. Presumption invents. Faith depends upon God. Presumption depends upon self. How many disappointments have occurred because believers confused presumption with faith?

How many have claimed promises God never made? How many have expected outcomes God never promised? Biblical faith never asks God to endorse human plans. Biblical faith joyfully embraces God's plans. This is why Jesus said,

“Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4

Notice that life itself is sustained by God's speaking.

Faith therefore becomes the continual response of God's people to His ongoing revelation.

This explains why Hebrews repeatedly says,

"By faith..."

By faith Abel... By faith Moses... Each story begins differently. Each calling is unique. Each circumstance is different.

Yet every account follows the same pattern.

God speaks.

Faith responds.

This changes the way we think about faith entirely. Faith is not trying to convince yourself that something is true. Faith is agreeing with what God has already declared to be true. Faith is therefore deeply relational. It is built upon knowing the One who speaks.

The more clearly you know God's character, the more readily you trust His Word.

This is why faith is ultimately confidence in a Person before it is confidence in a promise.

We believe the promise because we know the Promiser. And because He cannot lie, His Word becomes more certain than everything our eyes can see. That naturally leads to another profound question.

If faith begins with God's Word, how does faith see the world differently from everyone else?

For the difference between faith and unbelief is not merely what each believes.

It is how each interprets reality.

Faith Is Seeing from God's Perspective

Does faith deny reality? Or does faith actually see more—not less—than natural sight? These questions are essential because biblical faith is often misunderstood. Some imagine that faith requires pretending sickness is not real. But Scripture never teaches denial.

God never asks His people to deny reality. He asks them to interpret reality through His revelation. Faith does not close its eyes to the facts. Faith simply refuses to let the facts have the final word. This is exactly what Paul meant when he wrote,

“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

2 Corinthians 5:7

Notice that Paul does not say believers walk without sight. Sight is no longer their final authority. God's Word is. Faith therefore is not blindness. Faith is seeing from God's perspective.

Abraham saw beyond biology. His body was old and Sarah's womb was barren, yet God had called him the father of many nations. He did not deny the medical facts; he simply refused to let them be the final word.

Elisha saw the greater army. Surrounded at Dothan, his servant counted the enemy and despaired. Elisha counted what could not be seen. The chariots of fire were on the mountain before anyone's eyes were opened.

In both cases, faith did not create a new reality. It perceived the reality that was already there.

Faith Interprets Reality Through God's Word

This is the consistent testimony of Scripture. Faith does not deny the existence of storms. It remembers that Christ is Lord over them. Faith does not deny the existence of giants. It remembers that God is greater.

Faith does not deny suffering.

It remembers that present sufferings are not worthy to be compared with the glory that shall be revealed (Romans 8:18).

Faith does not deny death. It remembers that Christ is the resurrection and the life (John 11:25). Faith does not deny weakness. It remembers,

“My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Faith therefore is not seeing less than everyone else. It sees what God has revealed. It interprets the visible through the invisible. It judges the temporary by the eternal.

It refuses to allow circumstances to become the highest authority because it has already accepted God's Word as the final authority.

And this leads us to one of the greatest truths in all of Scripture.

Why has God always chosen faith—and not human performance—as the basis of righteousness?

For unless we understand righteousness by faith, we will misunderstand the very foundation upon which the Christian life stands.

Righteousness Has Always Been by Faith

Why has God always chosen faith rather than human effort?

Abraham was declared righteous before he was circumcised and before the Law was ever given. If obedience could make a man righteous, the Law would have done it.

Unless we understand righteousness by faith, we will eventually reduce Christianity to moral improvement instead of reconciliation with God.

Many people think the greatest problem humanity faced was sinful behaviour. Scripture goes deeper. The greatest problem was separation from God caused by unbelief. Behaviour was the fruit. Unbelief was the root.

The solution therefore could never simply be improved behaviour. It had to be restored relationship. And restored relationship could never be achieved through human performance. It could only come through trusting God. This is why the Bible repeatedly returns to Abraham.

Paul writes,

“For what does the Scripture say? 'Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.'”

Romans 4:3

Notice what the verse does not say. It does not say Abraham earned, deserved or achieved righteousness.

It says, "Abraham believed God." That belief was credited to him as righteousness. Why? Because faith honours God in a way that works never can. Works say, "I have earned acceptance." Faith says, "I receive what only God can provide." Works direct attention toward human achievement. Faith directs all glory toward divine grace.

This explains why Paul continues,

“Now to him who works, the wages are not counted as grace but as debt. But to him who does not work but believes on Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness.”

Romans 4:4–5

Consider the force of Paul's argument.

If righteousness could be earned, then God would simply be paying a debt.

Grace would cease to be grace. Salvation would become a transaction. The Cross would become unnecessary. But the Gospel declares something entirely different.

God justifies the ungodly—not because they have become worthy, but because Christ is worthy.

Faith receives what grace provides.

That is why Paul later writes,

“Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

Romans 5:1

Notice the certainty. Not, "We hope to have peace." Not, "We may eventually have peace."

"We have peace with God."

How?

Through faith.

Faith is therefore not merely the means by which we receive answers to prayer.

It is the means by which we stand accepted before God Himself. This truth did not begin with Paul. Hundreds of years earlier, Habakkuk declared,

“Behold the proud, his soul is not upright in him; but the just shall live by his faith.”

Habakkuk 2:4

Notice the contrast. The proud person depends upon himself. Pride trusts personal merit. Faith trusts divine mercy. One says, "I can make myself acceptable." The other says, "Only God can make me righteous."

Paul recognised that Habakkuk had uncovered one of the greatest principles in God's dealings with mankind.

He therefore places it at the very heart of the Gospel.

“For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith; as it is written, 'The just shall live by faith.'”

Romans 1:17

Think about those words.

"From faith to faith."

Paul is not describing two different methods. He is describing one continuous life. The believer begins by faith. Grows by faith. Lives by faith.

Finishes by faith.

There is no point at which dependence upon God is replaced by dependence upon self.

This is why Paul writes so passionately to the Galatians.

Having begun by faith, they were being tempted to return to confidence in human effort.

Paul therefore asks,

“Are you so foolish? Having begun in the Spirit, are you now being made perfect by the flesh?”

Galatians 3:3

That question deserves to be asked of every generation of believers. How many begin by trusting Christ... but gradually begin trusting their own discipline... their own spirituality... their own religious performance?

How many subtly exchange dependence upon Christ for confidence in themselves? Paul says this is impossible. The flesh can never complete what only the Spirit began. Righteousness is never maintained by self-effort. It continues to be received by faith.

Paul's own testimony is deeply moving.

Writing to the Philippians, he describes everything he once trusted—his heritage, education, zeal and religious achievements.

Then he concludes,

“...and be found in Him, not having my own righteousness, which is from the law, but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which is from God by faith.”

Philippians 3:9

Notice the two kinds of righteousness. One comes from self. The other comes from God. One depends upon performance. The other depends upon Christ.

One produces boasting. The other produces worship. Every believer must eventually answer this question.

Where is my confidence?

Is it in how well I have performed? Or is it in what Christ has accomplished? The writer of Hebrews returns once more to Habakkuk.

“Now the just shall live by faith...”

Hebrews 10:38

Again, notice the language. Not, "The just were saved by faith." But,

"The just shall live by faith."

Faith is not simply the entrance into the Christian life.

Faith is its atmosphere.

Every day the believer stands before God clothed, not in personal merit, but in the righteousness of Christ received by faith.

This has enormous implications. If righteousness comes through faith... The Christian never graduates from faith. He grows deeper into it. This also explains why unbelief is so destructive.

Whenever faith weakens, self-reliance quietly returns. Whenever dependence upon Christ decreases, dependence upon personal effort increases. Whenever faith fades, self-righteousness begins to reappear. That is why Satan continually attacks faith.

For if he can persuade believers to trust themselves instead of Christ, he has already weakened their fellowship with God.

The Gospel calls us back to something infinitely better. Not greater confidence in ourselves. Faith therefore is not merely believing God for blessings. It is resting completely in the sufficiency of His Son. It is agreeing that Christ is enough.

His righteousness is enough. His grace is enough. His finished work is enough. That is why the righteous have always lived by faith. Not because faith is a spiritual technique.

But because faith is the only response that gives all the glory to God.

And that raises another important question.

If righteousness is received by faith, what relationship exists between faith and the renewed mind?

For faith does not operate independently of the way we think.

The two are inseparably connected.

The Relationship Between Faith and the Renewed Mind

If faith comes by hearing God's Word, why do two believers hearing the same message sometimes respond so differently?

Can an unrenewed mind consistently walk by faith?

These questions bring us to one of the most practical truths in the Christian life.

Faith and the renewed mind cannot be separated. Faith is not merely an act of the heart. Neither is it merely an act of the intellect.

Faith is the response of a heart that has been persuaded by God's revelation and a mind that is increasingly being renewed by His truth.

This explains why Chapter Five was essential before we could discuss faith.

God did not accidentally place the renewed mind before the life of faith.

The renewed mind prepares the believer to recognise, embrace and trust God's perspective.

Without a renewed mind, faith constantly struggles against old patterns of thinking.

Paul writes,

“Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind...”

Romans 12:2

Notice what happens after the renewal of the mind.

“…that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God.”

Romans 12:2

Why is the renewed mind necessary for discerning God's will? Because an unrenewed mind continually misinterprets God's ways. It judges God's promises through human reasoning. It measures God's possibilities by natural limitations. It evaluates eternal realities by temporary circumstances.

Faith cannot flourish where the mind continually argues against God's Word. This is exactly what happened to Israel in the wilderness. Few stories reveal the relationship between faith and the mind more clearly. Israel witnessed the greatest miracles recorded up to that time. They saw the Nile turned to blood.

They watched the plagues fall upon Egypt. They walked through the Red Sea on dry ground. They ate manna from Heaven. They drank water from the rock.

They saw God's presence in the pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire.

If miracles alone produced faith, Israel should have become the greatest example of faith in Scripture.

Instead, they became one of its greatest warnings. Why? The writer of Hebrews gives the answer.

"For indeed the gospel was preached to us as well as to them; but the word which they heard did not profit

“...the word which they heard did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in those who heard it.”

Hebrews 4:2

Notice what the writer does not say. He does not say they lacked information. He does not say God failed to keep His promise. The problem was not the Word. God had spoken.

They had heard.

But what they heard never became the lens through which they interpreted what they saw.

Instead, they reversed the order. They interpreted God's promise through their circumstances. Faith always does the opposite. It interprets circumstances through God's promise. This is one of the greatest lessons every believer must learn.

The renewed mind does not ask, "What do my circumstances allow me to believe about God?" It asks,

"What has God revealed about Himself, and how should I now understand my circumstances?"

That single difference separates faith from unbelief.

Faith and the Renewed Mind

Faith and a renewed mind belong together. A mind still shaped by the visible world will always struggle to rest in an invisible promise.

This is why miracles alone never produce lasting faith. Israel saw the Red Sea part and still doubted at the next obstacle. The crowds who ate the loaves demanded another sign the following day. Wonder fades quickly; only a renewed mind holds what God has said when nothing can be seen.

Every battle of faith is finally a battle of authority. Whose word will govern this situation — the report, the feeling, the memory of past failure, or the Word of God?

Faith Has a Voice

If faith truly lives in the heart, can it remain silent?

Why does Paul describe "the spirit of faith" as something that both believes and speaks?

These questions reveal something profoundly important. Faith is never merely an inward conviction. Neither is it merely intellectual agreement. Faith always finds expression. Sometimes through obedience.

And often through speech. Not because words possess mystical power. But because the mouth eventually reveals what the heart truly believes. Paul writes,

“And since we have the same spirit of faith, according to what is written, 'I believed and therefore I spoke,' we also believe and therefore speak.”

2 Corinthians 4:13

Notice Paul's order.

We believed.

Therefore we spoke.

He does not reverse it. He does not say, "We spoke so that we might believe." Believing came first. Speaking followed. Paul is quoting David from Psalm 116. David had endured affliction.

He had faced death. He had experienced overwhelming pressure. Yet he declared,

“I believed, therefore I spoke.”

Psalm 116:10

Faith gave birth to his confession. His words were not an attempt to manufacture confidence. They were the expression of confidence already established in his heart. This distinction is vital. Many people have concluded that faith is created by speaking repeatedly.

Scripture teaches something different. Faith comes by hearing the Word of God (Romans 10:17). Speaking is not the source of faith. It is one of the evidences of faith. The mouth simply reveals what has already filled the heart.

Jesus taught exactly the same principle.

“For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.”

Matthew 12:34

Notice where speech begins. Not with the lips. With the heart. Whatever fills the heart eventually finds expression through the mouth. This explains why fearful people continually speak fear.

Speech reveals belief. The mouth is often the window through which the heart is seen.

David spoke to Goliath about the covenant before he ever swung the sling. Abraham was called the father of many nations before Isaac existed. The apostles, commanded to be silent, answered that they could not stop speaking of what they had seen and heard.

Faith reaches the mouth. It always has.

Believing and confessing belong together. Paul writes that with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. The two are not separate acts but one response.

And faith speaks because it trusts God's character. We do not repeat words to convince ourselves. We speak because the One who promised is faithful.

Faith Always Produces Obedience

If faith is invisible, how can it ever be recognised? Or does biblical faith always express itself in action? These questions expose another widespread misunderstanding. Many people think faith is merely believing certain truths. Others reduce faith to agreeing with a set of doctrines.

Some even imagine that faith is an inward feeling of confidence. But Scripture consistently presents faith differently. Faith certainly begins in the heart. But it never remains there. It always moves the feet.

It always affects decisions. It always changes behaviour. Not because works produce faith. But because genuine faith inevitably produces works. James asks a searching question.

“What does it profit, my brethren, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can faith save him?”

James 2:14

James is not contradicting Paul.

Paul teaches that we are justified before God by faith apart from works (Romans 3:28).

James teaches that genuine faith inevitably reveals itself through works. Paul addresses the root. James addresses the fruit. Paul explains how righteousness is received. James explains how genuine faith is recognised.

The two are perfectly consistent. James continues,

“Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.”

James 2:17

Notice what he does not say. He does not say works create faith. He says works reveal whether faith is alive. Living faith moves. Dead faith merely talks.

Noah built for a flood he had never seen. Abraham left without knowing his destination. Israel had to step into the Jordan before the water parted. Peter had to leave the boat. The woman with the issue of blood had to press through a crowd. Blind Bartimaeus had to keep calling when the crowd told him to be quiet.

In every case the movement came first, and the evidence came second.

Faith that never moves is not yet faith. It is agreement.

Faith Always Moves Toward God's Word

This principle appears throughout Scripture. Noah built. Abraham left. Moses confronted Pharaoh. Rahab welcomed the spies.

David ran toward Goliath. The disciples left their nets. The early Church preached despite persecution. None of these actions created God's promises. They simply responded to them.

This is why Paul describes,

“…for obedience to the faith among all nations for His name…”

Romans 1:5

Notice that phrase. Not obedience instead of faith.

The obedience that flows from faith.

Whenever God speaks, faith asks only one question:

"Lord, how should I respond?"

That response may differ for every believer. God told Noah to build. He told Abraham to leave. He told Paul to preach. Faith does not imitate another person's assignment.

Faith responds to God's Word for its own life.

This protects us from one of the greatest dangers in the Christian life.

Trying to copy someone else's faith. Biblical faith is never imitation. It is personal trust in God's personal revelation.

Faith Is Never Passive

Perhaps the greatest misunderstanding is to imagine that faith simply waits. Biblical faith certainly waits upon God's timing. But it never waits in unbelief. It waits with expectation. It waits while continuing to obey.

Faith is active because trust is active.

A husband who says he trusts his wife but constantly suspects her reveals that he does not truly trust her.

Likewise, if we say we trust God while continually refusing to obey Him, our actions expose what we actually believe.

Jesus asked a question that still confronts every generation.

“But why do you call Me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do the things which I say?”

Luke 6:46

Those words are not merely about obedience. They are about faith. For genuine faith always honours the authority of the One who speaks. Whenever God reveals His will, faith responds. Not reluctantly.

Not fearfully. But joyfully.

Because faith knows that God's commands are never burdensome and His purposes are always good.

And that brings us to another vital question.

When Jesus Himself spoke about faith, what exactly did He commend?

Did He praise confidence?

Or was there something deeper that He continually looked for in those who came to Him?

That is where we now turn.

What Jesus Commended

The centurion did not ask Jesus to come to his house; he understood authority and asked only for a word. The Syrophoenician woman would not release her claim on God's mercy. The friends of the paralytic tore open a roof.

Jesus never commended intelligence, status or religious pedigree. He commended faith — and He marvelled only twice, once at great faith in a Roman soldier and once at unbelief in His own town.

What Faith Is Not

If faith is confidence in God's revealed Word, why has it been so widely misunderstood?

Can we misunderstand faith so completely that, while using biblical language, we are trusting in something entirely different?

These questions deserve careful attention. Throughout history, every precious biblical truth has eventually attracted distortions. Faith is no exception.

The best way to recognise genuine faith is often by clearly identifying what it is not.

Faith is not positive thinking. Optimism looks at circumstances and hopes for the best. Faith looks at God and rests in what He has said.

Faith is not positive confession as a formula. Words matter, but they are not incantations. Faith speaks because it believes, not in order to compel God.

Faith is not denying reality. Abraham considered his body, already dead, and did not waver. He faced the facts and refused to let them outrank the promise.

Faith is not manipulating God. It never treats obedience as leverage or gives in order to obligate Him.

Faith is not self-confidence. Its object is never our own ability, sincerity or intensity of feeling.

Faith Is Confidence in God Alone

After removing these misunderstandings, the biblical picture becomes beautifully clear. Faith is not positive thinking. It is not positive confession. It is not self-confidence.

Faith is wholehearted confidence in the God who has revealed Himself through His Word.

Faith honours His character. Faith believes His testimony. Faith responds with joyful obedience. Faith rests because God is faithful.

The Prayer of Faith: Faith Expressed in Prayer and Fasting

If faith is the means by which believers participate in the realities of God's Kingdom, why did God command His people to pray?

Or does it reveal the posture of our own hearts? These questions deserve careful attention because prayer and fasting are often misunderstood. Some approach prayer as though God were reluctant and needed convincing.

Others treat fasting as though it were a spiritual currency by which God's favour can be purchased.

But are these ideas consistent with Scripture?

If faith is confidence in God's revealed Word, then prayer and fasting must also be understood through that same lens.

The Prayer of Faith

Prayer is faith speaking back to God. It is not informing Him of what He does not know, nor persuading Him toward what He is unwilling to do. It is agreement — the creature saying back to the Creator what the Creator has already said.

At Lazarus' tomb Jesus prayed as one already heard. Elijah prayed for rain because God had declared it would come. Neither was trying to move God. Both were moving with Him.

Fasting belongs here too. It is not a hunger strike against heaven and it earns nothing. It is the deliberate choosing of dependence over self, quieting the appetites that shout so that the voice of God may be heard more clearly.

Why Unbelief Is So Serious

Why does Scripture speak so strongly about unbelief? What if unbelief is ultimately a question of whose testimony we trust? These questions take us to the very heart of the Christian life. Many people think unbelief is merely intellectual doubt. Others imagine it is simply the absence of strong faith.

But Scripture presents unbelief as something much deeper. Unbelief is refusing to trust God's testimony. It is choosing another voice above the voice of God.

It is allowing circumstances, feelings, human reasoning or experience to become a greater authority than God's Word.

That is why unbelief is so serious.

It questions not merely God's promises, but God's character.

Unbelief began in Eden, when a word from the serpent was preferred to a word from God. Israel refused God's testimony at the border of the land and wandered forty years. Even in Nazareth, where Jesus Himself was present, unbelief limited what was received.

Scripture speaks of unbelief with unusual severity for one reason: it makes God a liar. To refuse His testimony is to say that what He has spoken cannot be trusted.

Every day we choose whose testimony will govern us. Faith honours God above every other voice, and that is why it pleases Him.

Faith has always been the language of God's Kingdom. Abel worshipped by faith. Enoch walked by faith. Noah worked by faith. The prophets spoke by faith. The apostles lived by it, and the Church has never stopped.

Faith Is the Language of the Fourth Dimension

As we return to the central theme of this book, one truth becomes beautifully clear.

The Fourth Dimension is not accessed through human intelligence. It is not entered by religious effort. It is not manipulated by spiritual techniques. Its realities are received by faith. Faith is the bridge between God's invisible Kingdom and our visible experience.

Faith does not create spiritual reality. God has already established that reality. Faith simply receives it. Faith does not make God's promises true. His promises were true long before we believed them.

Faith brings our lives into joyful agreement with what God has already accomplished.

This is why Paul declares,

“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

2 Corinthians 5:7

Notice again the word walk. Faith is not reserved for extraordinary moments. It is the ordinary rhythm of the Christian life. Every decision. Every trial.

Every prayer. Every act of obedience. Every season of waiting. Every victory. Every disappointment.

Every opportunity. Everything is lived by faith. This is the great invitation of the Gospel. Not merely to believe certain truths.

But to become men and women whose entire lives are governed by confidence in God's character and His Word.

The believer who lives by faith has learned to see from Heaven's perspective.

He has learned to trust Christ's finished work.

He has learned to interpret life through God's promises rather than through changing circumstances.

He has learned that the realities of the Fourth Dimension are more enduring than the realities perceived by his natural senses.

For this reason, faith has always been—and will always remain—the language of God's Kingdom.

And this is the great conclusion of everything we have established in this chapter:

Faith is not striving to bring Heaven down to earth. Faith is earth responding to Heaven. Faith is not persuading God to become faithful. Faith rests because God is already faithful. Faith is not creating a new reality. It is participating in the reality that God has already established through Christ. Therefore, the righteous have always lived—and will always live—by faith.

Chapter Summary

Throughout this chapter, one truth has been established again and again:

The realities of God's Kingdom are not entered by human effort but by faith.

From Genesis to Revelation, God has never related to His people on the basis of human merit.

He has always related to them through faith. Faith is not merely believing that God exists. It is trusting His character because He has revealed Himself. Faith begins where God speaks. It receives His testimony as the highest authority.

It interprets every circumstance through His Word rather than interpreting His Word through changing circumstances.

This is why faith is not positive thinking. It is not wishful optimism. It is not confidence in ourselves. Faith is wholehearted confidence in the God who cannot lie. Because God has spoken, faith rests.

Because God is faithful, faith obeys.

Because Christ has finished the work of redemption, faith receives what grace has already provided.

Throughout Scripture, every man and woman commended by God lived by this principle.

Abel worshipped by faith. Enoch walked with God by faith. Noah built by faith. Abraham journeyed by faith. Moses obeyed by faith.

David fought by faith. The prophets proclaimed God's Word by faith. The apostles preached by faith. The Church continues to live by faith. Nothing has changed.

The righteous have always lived by faith. Faith therefore becomes far more than an isolated doctrine. It becomes the believer's way of life. Faith prays because it trusts God's heart. Faith fasts because it depends upon God's sufficiency.

Faith obeys because it believes God's wisdom. Faith perseveres because it knows God's promises cannot fail. Faith worships because it has become convinced of God's greatness. Faith waits because it knows God's timing is perfect.

Faith gives glory to God because it acknowledges that every good thing comes from Him alone.

This is why unbelief is so serious. Unbelief does not merely question God's promises. It questions God's character. Faith, however, honours Him.

It declares before Heaven and earth that God is worthy of complete trust.

As we return to the central theme of this book, the conclusion becomes unmistakable.

The Fourth Dimension is not a mystical realm accessed through secret knowledge. It is the present reality of God's Kingdom. Christ has opened the way into that reality through His finished work. The Holy Spirit reveals that reality to believers. The renewed mind learns to think according to that reality.

And faith becomes the means by which believers participate in it every day.

Faith is therefore the bridge between Heaven's reality and earthly experience. Not because faith creates God's Kingdom. But because faith joyfully receives what God has already accomplished in Christ. The believer who lives by faith gradually learns to see life differently. He interprets every circumstance through God's promises.

He measures every challenge against God's greatness. He responds to every opportunity with joyful obedience.

He no longer lives as though the visible world were the highest reality.

He lives from the certainty that God's invisible Kingdom is eternal, unshakable and already reigning.

For this reason, the words of Scripture become not merely a doctrine to believe but a way of life to embrace:

“For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

2 Corinthians 5:7

  1. Looking honestly at your daily life, would someone conclude that you are living primarily by sight or by faith?
